# Reference job set for the G-code gates

Chosen in work breakdown slice 6 item 12; recorded in [decision 0020](decisions/0020-slice-6-post-and-round-trip.md) item 8. The set is what gates 3, 4 and 5 of [verification plan section 6](verification-plan.md) run. **Generated G-code is the user's to verify before running on a machine (NFR-12); nothing here has been run on a Z1.**

## The jobs

All use the same tool and material: a 3 mm ball end mill (2 flutes, 8 mm flute length), oak (12000 rpm, 0.02 mm per tooth, plunge 150 mm/min, stepover 2 mm), on the Makera Z1 profile. `groove`, `cone` and `sphere` use helical finishing with 5 degree steps; `lobed` (added by [decision 0036](decisions/0036-slice-13-simultaneous-finishing.md) item 12) uses simultaneous 4-axis finishing with 2 degree steps on a finer mesh (201 x 720), so the contact feed is judged on evenly spaced points (OI-77). Stock is a cylinder 2 mm larger than the part in diameter and 2 mm longer. The parts are generated by `openchuck-testkit::parts`, so no model files are stored.

| Job | Part | What it exercises | Golden programs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `groove` | Cylinder, radius 12 mm, 30 mm long, with a helical groove (pitch 12 mm, 1.5 mm deep) | Simultaneous X and A with Z following the groove; about 15 turns of A, then the Z1 unwind (`G92.4 A0 S0`) | `testdata/golden/reference/groove_makera-z1.nc`, `groove_linuxcnc.ngc` |
| `cone` | Cone from radius 10 mm to 5 mm, 30 mm long | Z changing steadily with X; feed rule on a changing radius | `cone_*` |
| `sphere` | Sphere, radius 10 mm | Rim and pole behaviour; short moves | `sphere_*` |
| `lobed` | Lobed vase: radius 10 mm + 1.5 sin(2 pi x / 40) + 1 cos(3 angle), 20 mm long | Simultaneous X, Y, Z and A: the tool follows a normal that leaves the plane through the axis, so Y moves and A leads or lags the drive point; A smoothed within 10 degrees of the normal | `lobed_*` |

`cargo test -p openchuck-app --test reference_jobs` posts each job with both shipped posts, requires gates 1 and 2 to pass, checks the output is identical at 1 and 4 threads, and compares it with the golden files (`OPENCHUCK_BLESS=1` rewrites them; review the diff). The operation id in the comment line is replaced by `<id>` in the golden files.

`cargo test -p openchuck-cli --test e2e` builds the same jobs (all but `drilling` and `swarf`, which are read from STEP files and need the kernel) with the command-line tool, from STL import to G-code, and requires each program, regenerated in separate processes at 1, 2 and N threads with both posts, to equal its golden file byte for byte; it also simulates them and runs the undo, redo and cancellation scenarios ([decision 0096](decisions/0096-slice-20-end-to-end-and-determinism.md)). A job added here is added there too.

## Baseline finishing jobs (slice 12)

Three more jobs run on the `cone` part with the same tool, material and Z1 profile, one for each new strategy ([decision 0034](decisions/0034-slice-12-baseline-finishing.md)): `cone_axial` (axial finishing, stepover 2 mm, zig-zag), `cone_indexed` (indexed finishing at 0, 90, 180 and 270 degrees) and `cone_axial_lead` (axial finishing with `lead` entry). Golden files `testdata/golden/reference/cone_axial_*`, `cone_indexed_*` and `cone_axial_lead_*` for both posts. `cargo test -p openchuck-app --test reference_jobs` checks gates 1 and 2 and the golden files; `--test golden_audit` (`req_op_04_baseline_finishing_golden_files_pass_the_audit`) checks conventions, that A does not move within a pass, no gouge (deepest 0.0026 mm) and that the two posts reach the same positions. **Gate 3 (the LinuxCNC replay) has not been run for these programs**: `cargo xtask replay` picks them up from the folder and CI runs it; the session that built them had no Docker daemon. Gates 4 and 5 are not run.

## Drilling job (decision 0056)

`drilling` is the first job read from a model file rather than generated: the OpenCASCADE fixture `testdata/step/valid/rotary_blank_drilled.step` (a rod of 24 mm diameter, 60 mm long, with a 6 mm hole across it at X 30), added to the project through the STEP importer and placed turned 30 degrees about the rotary axis, so the hole is drilled at an A other than zero. Radial drilling ([decision 0051](decisions/0051-slice-18-radial-drilling.md)) with a 6 mm, 118 degree, two-flute drill in oak (6000 rpm, 0.03 mm per tooth, plunge 120 mm/min), pecks of 8 mm with a full retract, on the Makera Z1 profile; stock a cylinder of 26 mm diameter, 62 mm long. Golden files `testdata/golden/reference/drilling_makera-z1.nc` and `drilling_linuxcnc.ngc`. Reading the file needs the kernel, so the job is built by `cargo test -p openchuck-app --features kernel-tests --test step_pipeline` (gates 1 and 2, the golden files, 1 and N threads), which the STEP kernel CI job runs on all three platforms; gate 3 replays the LinuxCNC program with the others. One hole at one A (OI-234).

## Swarf job (decision 0111)

`swarf` is the second job read from a STEP file: the OpenCASCADE fixture `testdata/step/valid/hex_bar.step` (a hexagonal bar along X, 20 mm across the flats, 40 mm long from X 0, a flat on top), added as it is in the file. Its six flats are picked in the scene from outside and saved as one face set, so the operation cuts those faces only ([decision 0111](decisions/0111-swarf-through-the-application.md) item 1). Swarf ([decision 0061](decisions/0061-slice-17-swarf.md)) with a 6 mm two-flute flat end mill with 15 mm of flute in oak (12000 rpm, 0.02 mm per tooth, plunge 150 mm/min) and the strategy's defaults (tolerance 0.01 mm, the tip at the end chosen automatically), on the Makera Z1 profile; stock a cylinder of 24 mm diameter, 42 mm long from X -1. Each flat is one pass along X at one A, the side of the tool on the flat; opposite flats share an A, as the walls either side of the upright tool, so the program turns to three A positions (30, 90 and 150 degrees). Golden files `testdata/golden/reference/swarf_makera-z1.nc` and `swarf_linuxcnc.ngc`. Built by `cargo test -p openchuck-app --features kernel-tests --test step_pipeline` (gates 1 and 2, the golden files, 1 and N threads, and a simulation that cuts nothing of the part), which the STEP kernel CI job runs on all three platforms; gate 3 replays the LinuxCNC program with the others.

## Blisk jobs (decision 0096)

The owner's blisk, `testdata/stl/blisk.stl`, is the first sample from a real part rather than a generated one (OI-311). It is a binary STL of 6,386 triangles: a disc of radius 20 mm from X 0 to 10, a flat shoulder at X 10, and blades on a conical hub (radius 15 down to 9) out to radius 20, 50 mm long. It is imported through the command-line tool onto the Makera Z1 profile, with the reference jobs' 3 mm ball in oak and cylinder stock 42 mm in diameter and 52 mm long from X -1. It runs as three jobs: `blisk_helical_5` (helical finishing, 5 degree steps), `blisk_helical_2` (2 degree steps) and `blisk_axial` (axial finishing, `max_x_step` 3). Each checks gates 1 and 2 on both posts and requires byte-identical programs at 1, 2 and N threads, matching the golden files in `testdata/golden/blisk/`. Their programs differed between Linux, Windows and macOS (OI-319) until [decision 0113](decisions/0113-toolpath-choices-rounding-cannot-flip.md) made the path fitter's and drop-cutter's choices independent of the platform's rounding; they run in CI again (owner's decision, 18 Oct 2026), and the three-platform Tests job is their check. Those files are kept out of `golden/reference` so that gate 3 does not replay them. Lane BT's estimate, not measured, is about 11, 11 and 17 to 19 minutes on LinuxCNC (cut times 490.0 s, 488.9 s and 894.1 s), about 40 minutes for the three. Where they run is the owner's decision. Simultaneous finishing refuses the part where the blades meet the shoulder (OI-318), and the blisk is not simulated until its simulation memory is fixed (OI-317).

## Assumptions to settle before gates 4 and 5

- **Axis height.** The owner gave the chuck centre as 45 mm above the bed (13 Oct 2026). The Z1 profile takes the bed at the bottom of the Z travel (-100), so the axis is at machine Z -55, and the safe Z is 50 mm above the axis (5 mm below the top of the travel). That reading of "the bed" is an assumption (OI-60). **Measure the axis height on the machine and rerun the jobs before any trial.** A wrong value moves every Z.
- **Work zero.** Programs run in G54 with Y0 Z0 on the A axis and X0 at the part's model origin (OI-40). The operator sets X0 by hand.
- **A direction** was checked on the machine (13 Oct 2026): A+ turns the top of the chuck toward the front (-Y), as the profile says (OI-03). **Wrap, feed rule and unwind** (`G92.4 A0 S0`) are the derived SP-12 conventions, unconfirmed until the air cut (OI-37).
- **No tool changer test.** One tool, `T1 M6` at the start; the tool change is exercised in a separate attended test (OI-41).

## Golden file audit

`cargo xtask golden` (also part of the ordinary tests) checks the committed programs against the exact parts, the post conventions and the controller's feed rule, so they need no hand review: see decision 0020 item 14 for what it checks and what it found. Measured on these files: deepest cut into the part 0.0005 to 0.0019 mm (0.0006 mm for `lobed`); contact feed within 2 % (worst 1.98 %; `lobed` median 0.17 %, worst 1.96 %) away from the part's ends; the Z1 and LinuxCNC programs of a job reach the same contact points.

## Gate 3

`cargo xtask replay` runs each LinuxCNC program (`testdata/golden/reference/*_linuxcnc.ngc` and the small part) in LinuxCNC's headless simulator and compares what it reached with the replay (`openchuck-cli gcode-sim`). ## Air-cut plan (gate 4) for the operator

The full procedure and record sheet are in [z1-air-cut-procedure.md](z1-air-cut-procedure.md) (reviewed 14 Oct 2026). For the operator to review before the first trial; amend it after the review. Rules: verification plan section 6 (attended, emergency stop in reach, no tool, no material, reduced feed).

1. Check the profile values the programs depend on: axis height (OI-60), travel (OI-39). The A direction is already confirmed. Regenerate the Z1 programs and compare with the golden files.
2. Run SP-12's conventions program first (`spikes/sp12/AIR-CUT.md` on the SP-12 branch) and record the results in decision 0007's open items (wrap, feed rule, G93; the direction is confirmed).
3. Run `sphere_makera-z1.nc` with feed override at 10 %: the shortest program and the smallest envelope. Watch the first 20 lines; stop on anything unexpected.
4. Time one simultaneous XYZA move and compare with the IR's predicted time (the program comment states the total).
5. Run `groove_makera-z1.nc` and `cone_makera-z1.nc` the same way; watch the unwind at the end (`G92.4 A0 S0`: A must not move).
6. Record, for each run: file, override, result, measured against predicted time, alarms. Any unexpected motion is a defect with the G-code attached.

Gate 5 (first cut in wax or foam) follows only when gate 4 passes, with the safe-practice checklist ([reviewed by the operator](z1-safe-practice-checklist.md), 14 Oct 2026).
