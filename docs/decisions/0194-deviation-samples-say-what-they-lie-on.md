# 0194: Each deviation sample says what it lies on: the volume of a tab is never material left (lane BUG-TABDEV; issue 248)

- **Status:** proposed (21 Oct 2026). Item 3 is the owner's choice on OI-740, option A, relayed by the coordinator on 21 Oct 2026.
- **What it amends:** [decision 0045](0045-slice-21c-wiring.md) item 6 (the deviation target decides the faces inside the union at each triangle's centroid) and the alternative it rejected ("filter deviation samples inside the union instead of degenerate triangles"). The target is still the part with its held regions and tabs as one surface ([decision 0042](0042-slice-21b-composite-target.md), [decision 0160](0160-held-region-is-the-stock-section.md)); no requirement changes.
- **Date:** 21 Oct 2026.
- **Open items:** OI-740 (how the faces of held regions and tabs count) is new and closed by the owner's answer (item 3). OI-196 is progressed (the centroid approximation for a face partly covered is gone).

## Context

Trial feedback F-78 (issue 248). On the owner's `blisk_glitchy_generating` project the Simulate step's deviation view showed "Material left 3.000 mm or more" at the chuck end of the part, only at the tab. The owner's hypothesis: the tab is counted as deviation from the part, and it should not be, because the user set it on purpose (OP-21; SET-09 for the held region). Requirements: SIM-05 (compare the stock with the target, colour by remaining and overcut, report the worst values), OP-21 and SET-09 (the simulation keeps the tab and the held region), NFR-12 (nothing may hide a real fault).

Records read: 0042 (the composite target), 0045 item 6 (the deviation target, decided at the centroid), 0104 (held regions and tabs in the interface), 0160 (a held region without a holding diameter is the stock's own section, a box on box stock), 0161 (the tools keep clear of the gripped length, widened by the tool's radius), 0162 (the histogram and the probe).

What the lane found (release build, the owner's project, 3.76 million samples at the default 0.1 mm cell):

- **The tab and the held region are in the target**, as decisions 0042 and 0045 say. The bug is in how the faces inside the union are dropped: at each triangle's centroid. The blisk's hub end face at X 10 is a fan of large triangles whose centroids lie outside the 10 mm tab but which run under it. Their samples under the tab sit inside the tab's material and read it as left on the part: up to the tab's radius, 5 mm, shown as the 3 mm bound of the search. 8,166 samples read 1 mm or more there (2,016 at the bound), and the worst "material left" of the run was that bound at X 10.0, radius 2.1. It showed in the Deviation and Remaining colours and the probe (the part's triangles), the histogram, the worst value and the share within tolerance; not in the Overcut view.
- **A second bug in the same code:** a triangle's solid was found by assuming every solid has `4 x 96` triangles, but a held region of a box section (decision 0160) has 12. In this project the held region comes first, so most of the tab's triangles were taken as the held region's own and never tested against it, and the tab's end face, buried against the held region's face at X 5, was sampled as a surface of the finished part (part of the 15,457 samples at the bound on that face).
- **The held region's exposed face** at X 5 held about 475,000 samples (13 %) reading material left up to about 1.6 mm: the band beside the held region the tools may not enter (decision 0161). That stock is real, left by design.

## Decision

1. **Each sample says what it lies on.** `openchuck-sim` gains `SampleOn` (`Part`, `Workholding`, `Inside`), a field `on` on `DeviationPoint`, and `compare_classified`, which takes a function giving each sample's `SampleOn` from its triangle, position and normal. `compare` and `compare_cancellable` mark every sample `Part`, as before. The samples stay in triangle order, all of them, so the colours of the part's triangles still line up with the mesh: the reason 0045 gave for not filtering samples no longer applies.
2. **Inside is decided at the sample, by the rule 0045 used at the centroid.** `openchuck-app::workholding::DeviationTarget` holds the target mesh with the solids and each solid's triangle range (replacing the fixed count). A sample is `Inside` when another part of the union holds it: strictly inside a held region or tab, or on its surface with that surface facing the sample's own (a face under a tab, a tab's end against a held region), or, for a sample on a solid, inside the model. Else it is `Workholding` on a solid's triangle and `Part` on the model's. Triangles wholly inside, by the centroid, are still made degenerate so they take no samples; the per-sample rule decides the faces partly covered.
3. **What counts (OI-740: the owner chose option A).** `DeviationPoint::counts` says whether a sample makes the figures: an `Inside` sample never; a `Part` sample always, right up to the tab, so a real fault next to it still shows (NFR-12); a `Workholding` sample only where it is overcut, so a cut into a tab or held region is never hidden, while the material left beside them, where the tools keep clear (decision 0161), is not counted. The worst values, the overcut count and places, `Deviation::leftover`, `triangle_values` (the colours and the probe), and the results panel's histogram and share within tolerance use the counted samples only. A triangle with none, wholly under a tab, is "Not measured". The command line's Simulate report says how many samples were counted. The other options put to the owner: B, count the held-region and tab faces as before (only the bugs of items 2 and 4 fixed); C, as A, with the material beside them on a line of its own in the results.
4. **The solids' triangles are found by their ranges**, so a box-section held region before a tab no longer hides the tab's faces from the test against it.

## Alternatives considered

- **Leave the samples in and give the tab its own colour in the part's view.** The samples under the tab are not on any surface of the finished part; colouring them would still put them in the figures.
- **Drop the samples inside the union from the list.** It breaks the order the colours rely on (decision 0045's reason); marking them keeps the order and costs one byte a sample.
- **Measure along the normal instead of the closest distance.** Decision 0025 chose the closest distance for good reasons at the part's edges; the fault here is which samples are taken, not how each is measured.
- **Mesh the solids finer, or split the part's triangles where a tab meets them.** It would make the centroid rule closer but never exact, and changes the target the colours are of.

## Consequences

- On the owner's project after the change (same container, release build, 4 threads): the worst material left is 1.15 mm at X 20, radius 15 (where the blades meet the hub, on the part's own surface), where before it was the 3 mm bound under the tab; no counted sample at the tab or the held region reads 1 mm or more; 2,301,228 of 3,781,923 samples count (the rest lie inside the finished part or on the held region's and tab's faces with no overcut). The whole simulation, with the comparison, took 169.6 s against 165.2 s before.
- The boundary between the tab and the part (NFR-12): only what a tab or held region holds is left out, sample by sample; a sample on the part's face beside the tab counts as any other, so material left or an overcut next to the tab still shows. Where a tab overhangs the part (a frustum widening away from it), the stock the tool cannot reach under the overhang lies on the part's face beside the tab, not under it, and still counts.
- Changelog: `openchuck-sim` (`SampleOn`, `Classify`, `compare_classified`, `DeviationPoint::on` and `counts`; a new public field is a breaking change for a host that builds `DeviationPoint` by its fields) and `openchuck-app` (`workholding::DeviationTarget`; `workholding::deviation_target` is replaced by `DeviationTarget::new`). Neither crate is in the library family that `cargo-semver-checks` checks, so the workspace version is not bumped (decision 0048).
- Performance: one classification per sample; a sample on the part tests the solids (analytic), one on a solid also projects onto the model (the figure above includes it).
