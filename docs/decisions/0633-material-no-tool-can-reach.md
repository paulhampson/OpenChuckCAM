## Context

The owner's carving project (a woggle: a tube whose bore lies on the rotary axis; kept outside the repository, in the lane's scratch space only; only numbers are recorded here) was simulated after the stock was lengthened and rest machining added (#471). The results table showed 27% of the part with 1 mm to 3 mm or more left, and the cut part showed nothing of it.

The lane ran the real pipeline on the owner's project from a scratch program outside the repository (release build, cell 0.1 mm, simulation 119 s):

- **The table was right.** 4,543,382 counted samples of 5,954,274: ±0.1 mm 71.3%, 0.1 to 0.25 mm 0.4%, 0.25 to 1 mm 1.2%, 1 to 3 mm 3.5%, 3 mm or more (the search's bound) 23.6%; more than 1 mm 27.1%; no overcut.
- **Where it was.** Every one of the 1,232,364 samples with more than 1 mm left faces the rotary axis, at 60 to 80% of the part's largest radius, spread evenly from 10% to 100% of the part's length: the bore. The 1 to 3 mm ones are the bore within about the first and last 10% of the length. No sample on an end face had more than 1 mm left: the far-end facing works. Neither the longer stock nor the rest machining changed which samples count.
- **Why the cut part did not show it.** The bore is full of stock, so no surface of the cut part lies near the bore wall; its only visible faces are the stock's caps over the bore at the two ends. Each is further than the search (3 mm) plus a cell from every counted sample, so decision 0238 item 4 drew them "Not measured", in the grey of "within tolerance" with a slate hatch. On the cut part (1,323,834 triangles, 11,244 mm²) 57.6% of the area was "on the surface", 42.4% "not measured", and no triangle "material left".
- On the part model coloured by its own values (lane VIEW-OVERLAYS, #472), 1,522 of 500,738 triangles (0.3%) are "more than 1 mm left": the bore is a few large triangles holding 27% of the samples (samples follow the area, decision 0108). There the leftover is visible on the bore wall, only by looking into the bore with the part shown.

The owner's answer: "Distinguish between excess material and unreachable material. Also if material is unreachable and internal this should generate a message. It will be more intuitive if the distinction is made between these deviation types."

## Decision

1. **Unreachable means enclosed across the rotary axis** (owner, Q2). On this machine every tool axis lies in a plane across the rotary axis. A tool whose radius never narrows from its tip upward (the monotone rule of the tool profile), with a shank no narrower than its cutter, is cut by the plane `x = p.x` in one strip that runs from the point it touches out past the stock. So where the empty space beside a sample is closed off from the outside in the sample's own section across X, no such tool can reach it, whatever its size or lean.
2. **How it is decided** (`openchuck-sim` `reach.rs`, `classify_reach`). A grid across X at the simulation cell, coarser for a part more than a thousand cells across. One ray along X per grid point through each closed body of the target (the part, whole, and each held region and tab), keeping where it crosses. X is cut into slabs (at most 500); a grid point is solid in a slab only where one body fills a hair more than the whole slab there. The empty grid points are flood-filled from the border, four-connected. A counted part sample with more than one cell left is *enclosed* when the grid points on its empty side, from half to one and a half pitches along its normal across X, are all empty and none reached; *open* when all are empty and reached; else *undecided*. Each slab is worked on its own, in fixed groups, so the result is the same at any thread count (NFR-06).
3. **Never guessed (NFR-12).** Material left is "not classified" (never "excess") when the test cannot prove it enclosed: open, or undecided. Nothing is called enclosed when a tool of the setup is narrower than two grid pitches (an opening the grid reads as closed might pass it) or has a shank narrower than its cutter; for a sample facing along X (an end face, reached from the end); where the grid's step crosses a wall; or beside a body that is not closed (a ray with an odd number of crossings fills nothing). The slab at each end of the part never encloses, so a sample on the rim of an opening there is open. An opening narrower than about two pitches reads as closed: the classification resolves the part to the simulation's own cell, as the comparison does.
4. **The cut part shows it** (amends decision 0238 item 4). A cut-part triangle further than the search plus a cell from every counted sample stands for the material behind it: it takes the value and class of the sample of any kind nearest to the point one search distance behind the face, into the stock, when that is a counted sample on the part whose value is a bound (at least that much is left: stock past an end that was not faced) or whose material left is enclosed (a bore full of stock behind its cap at a faced end, however thin its wall: there the value is the wall's thickness, not a bound). It is Not measured otherwise: behind it a held region or tab, a face inside the part, or a part sample that is neither. Looking behind the face matters because the cut part's mesh is the dexel stock's: a face across the stock's end is made of strips as tall as their columns, whose centres lie nearest the rim of an opening, where a faced end leaves almost nothing. The part model's own colours take each triangle's class from the sample it is coloured by (`triangle_worst`).
5. **The band and the colours** (owner, Q3 and Q4). A seventh band, *Unreachable (enclosed)*: violet `#b652ff` under a cross-hatch of `#15171a` (4.8:1 on it). Material left beyond the tolerance on an enclosed sample is drawn in it whatever its value. *Not measured* becomes the slate `#5d6570` under a light single hatch `#e3e7ec` (4.7:1 on the slate, 10.5:1 on the view), no longer the grey of "within" (cause c of the diagnosis). Measured with the tests of decision 0216 (CIEDE2000; dichromacy after Machado and others, 2009):
   - Unreachable: 3.51:1 on the view; at least 19.2 from every band in normal vision; for every simulated colour vision type 25.8 from overcut, 21.8 from "within" and the hidden slate; 20.7 from every selection-set swatch (0216 item 3) and 14.7 from the other roles. For red-green colour vision it is near the blues of material left (no purple stays apart from them: `#9b5de5`, the first candidate, was 2.0 from "more than 1 mm left" and 17.9 from set swatch 2), so the cross-hatch tells it apart there (NFR-15, by pattern).
   - Not measured is the hidden band's slate and 2.2:1 on the view: it is told apart from a hidden band by its hatch, and its hatch lines carry the contrast. **Amends 0216 items 7 and 8** for this band: the 3:1 on the view applies to its hatch, and the hidden-band difference is replaced by the hatch. Every other band keeps every 0216 check. A yellow (`#f2e05a`) would have passed every check but the overcut margin (23.2 of 25) and sits near the amber of cut moves; the owner chose purple.
6. **One warning per setup** (owner, Q5). When a setup has enclosed material the run's notes, which the Results window shows as warnings and the command-line tool prints, say, with the setup's numbers (shares of the counted samples, as the histogram's; the area from each sample's share of its triangle; the range along the part as shares of its length from its start; no coordinates). On the owner's project:

   > Setup 'Setup 1': 28.7% of the part's surface (about 2727 mm²) is enclosed by the part across the rotary axis, between 0% and 100% of the part's length from its start, on surfaces facing the rotary axis. No tool on this machine can reach it, so 0.14 mm to 3.0 mm or more of material is left there (for example in a bore along the rotary axis). Remove it another way, such as by drilling or boring along the rotary axis before or after this job. Generated G-code must be verified by the user before it is run on a machine. OpenChuckCAM does not guarantee that a program is safe or correct for any machine, tool or setup.

   "On surfaces facing the rotary axis" is said only when nine in ten of them do. The classification is kept with the comparison in the cached stock (decision 0217), so a run that takes the stock from the cache gives the same warning without comparing again.
7. **The results table.** Two rows under the worst values: "Unreachable (enclosed)", the share in violet, and "Left, not classified", the rest of the material left ("not classified" for a run without the classification). Under each histogram row with unreachable samples, a row "of which unreachable" with a violet bar and its count and share. The plan's fourth column did not fit the narrowest inspector (309 px of content in 264 px, `req_sim_05_the_labelled_histogram_fits_the_narrowest_inspector`), so the share is a row of its own. Below the histogram, a "Show unreachable (enclosed)" tick box (SIM-15): unticked, those triangles keep the part's own colour, the view says "Some bands are hidden", "Show all" ticks it again, and the counts stay those of the whole part. Telling the open material apart into *excess* (a tool of the setup fits) and *too narrow for the tools* is OI-1988.
8. **SIM-05 amended** (agreed by the owner, relayed by the coordinator, 1 Nov 2026): "Compare the simulated stock with the target model and colour the result by remaining material and overcut, and tell material the tools cannot reach (enclosed) apart from material left within reach; report the worst values." The verification plan's SIM-05 row and specification 11.5 (the projection rule, the band table, the Not-measured look, the results inspector, the probe) are updated in the same pull request.
9. **Split.** Pull request 1 is items 1 to 8 except the legend row, the Not-measured swatch and the probe text, which live in `viewport_overlays.rs` beside lane VIEW-OVERLAYS' changes (#472): pull request 2 after #472 (OI-1987), with the excess/too-narrow split (OI-1988) and a note on the colour scale that enclosed material is inside the cut part and shows on the part.

## Alternatives considered

- **Reuse OP-20's unreachable-areas code** (`openchuck-path` `simultaneous/hidden.rs`): rejected. It is private to simultaneous finishing, works on sections at the stepover with one tool, and is in the library family; this works on the comparison's own samples, for every operation's tools.
- **Only fix the projection (the first plan)**, so the caps over the bore read "more than 1 mm left": kept as item 4, but alone it would not say why the material is there or what to do, which the owner asked for.
- **A fourth column in the histogram**: did not fit the narrowest inspector (item 7).
- **Yellow for Unreachable**: passes all but the overcut margin; the owner chose purple (item 5).

## Consequences

- A bore along the rotary axis, or any pocket closed across X, is now named as such in the results and drawn in its own band; the owner is told to drill or bore it.
- The cut part's faces over stock left beyond the search are coloured instead of Not measured; Not measured remains for stock nearest the workholding and for stock nearest a part sample with less than the search left.
- Cost of the cut part's colours on the owner's project (1,323,834 triangles, release build): 3.2 s on this branch against 3.7 s on `main` `17e1492` for the same job (`start_stock_surface` at the end). The index now holds every sample, so the search for counted samples skips subtrees with none (a held region's or tab's alone); without that it took 14.1 s.
- Cost on the owner's project: the classification adds 0.31 s to a 119 s simulation (rays 374 by 354 in 0.09 s, 351 slabs, 1,304,634 samples classified in 0.16 s; release build, 4 vCPUs). Memory: one class per sample (5.9 million bytes on the owner's project) kept with the comparison.
- Until pull request 2, the legend shows neither the new band nor the new Not-measured look (OI-1987).
