## Decision (accepted)

1. **A strategy of its own, `simultaneous_finishing`** (`openchuck_path::simultaneous`), registered in `Registry::builtin` beside helical finishing, so the application, the CLI and the generated parameter form offer it with no other change (OP-16). It honours region constraints (OP-02, decision 0032) the way helical finishing does: the tool is dropped on the whole part, the constraint decides where it may cut, the cut runs to the region's edge by bisection, and with a constraint the seeded points are no further apart than 0.1 mm of path (`CONSTRAINED_SPACING`, OI-138). **Drive curves** are tracks on the surface in (X, polar angle) coordinates, chosen by the `pattern` parameter:
   - `spiral` (default): one helix round the axis whose axial advance per turn is the stepover, as for helical finishing (decision 0018 item 7), from the part's lowest X to its highest;
   - `axial`: one track along X per angular station, the stepover apart as arc length at the part's largest radius (decision 0018 item 7), alternating direction.

   A **drive point** is where a ray from outside the part towards the axis first meets the surface, through `SurfaceSource::cast_ray`, so the same code serves meshes and exact faces. Exact faces give their own normal; a mesh gives the smoothed normal of item 4. This is SP-4's "step A, cast along X" read as the surface sampled at stations round and along the axis. A surface a ray from outside cannot see (an undercut, the far wall of a slot narrower than the ray's reach) is not driven; the tool could not reach most of it at a vertical tool axis either. The owner accepted this as a P1 limitation; driving those surfaces where the tool can reach them is requirement OP-20 (P2), added by this record (OI-148 closed).
2. **Tool-axis policy (OP-06; decision 0015 item 3).** At a drive point with outward normal `n`, the best axis A allows is `n`'s part in the plane perpendicular to X, at polar angle `phi_n` (from +Y towards +Z). The tool axis is given the target angle `phi_n + lead * travel`, where `travel` is +1 or -1 as the track moves round the axis, and `A = sense * (90 - phi)`. The part of `n` along X, the **X-slope angle**, cannot be removed by any A; the largest is reported in a note (item 9), as decision 0015 asks.
3. **Lead and tilt (OI-145; accepted by the owner, 15 Oct 2026).** A rotary axis leaves one degree of freedom for the tool axis, the angle in the plane perpendicular to X, so "lead" and "tilt" are two uses of it:
   - `lead_angle` (degrees, -30 to 30, default 0) leans the tool axis from the normal towards the direction of travel round the axis (for example to keep a ball's centre off the cut). It applies to the spiral; axial passes travel along X, where A cannot lean the tool, and a non-zero lead is refused for them with a message.
   - `max_tilt` (degrees, 0 to 30, default 10) is the most the tool axis may leave its target (the normal plus the lead) so that A turns smoothly with fewer reversals (item 5). 0 follows the normal exactly.

   So the verification plan's "tool axis stays within lead and tilt limits" reads: the in-plane angle between the tool axis and the normal is the lead, give or take at most `max_tilt`. The sum stays well short of a right angle, so the tool never faces away from the surface.

   **In the UI** (owner, 15 Oct 2026): lead and tilt are easy to misread on a 4-axis machine, so each needs an explanatory tooltip. The generated parameter form of slice 8 ([decision 0029](0029-slice-8-viewport-and-shell.md); `apps/openchuck-ui/src/form_panel.rs`) already shows a parameter's schema description, with its allowed range, as the label's tooltip, so the two descriptions are written as those tooltips: what the angle leans or allows, that lead applies to travel round the axis (spiral) and is refused for axial passes, that `max_tilt` trades closeness to the normal for fewer A reversals and changes the finish for flat, bull-nose and V tools, and that the slope along X cannot be followed by any A. Any richer help (a diagram, a link to this record) belongs to the UI lanes.
4. **Smoothed normals (OI-67, closed).** On a mesh the normal at a drive point is the normal of the surface through four neighbouring drive points `NORMAL_SPAN` = 0.5 mm away (two along X, two round the axis): the cross product of the central differences, oriented with the facet normal, and the facet normal where a neighbour is missing or lies on another face of an edge (more than 60 degrees from the facet's). On SP-4's lobed part (401 x 1440 mesh) the tool axis is within **0.028 degrees** of the exact in-plane normal at every cut away from the ends, against SP-4's 1.2 degrees for facet normals.
5. **Smooth A with the fewest reversals (OP-15).** Along each track the target angles are sampled every 0.5 degrees round the axis or 0.1 mm along X, and replaced by:
   - the **taut string** through the band `target ± max_tilt` (the shortest path inside the band, with free ends), which fixes where the angle must turn back: no path inside the band turns back fewer times;
   - between those turns, the monotone function nearest the targets in the largest deviation (the mean of the running maximum from one side and the running minimum from the other). It equals the target wherever the target is already monotone, so the tool stays on the normal unless smoothing needs the band. At a turn the taut string lies on the band's edge, so the target there is the extreme of the targets on both sides, and the fits on either side meet on it.

   The shortest path alone is not used: across a straight band it runs diagonally and leaves the normal by up to `max_tilt` everywhere (measured: 1.6 mm off the axis on a sphere). A between samples is interpolated linearly. Range limits, continuity across passes, links and the unwind stay with the shared services (decision 0018 item 5); a spiral cannot fit a limited A range and is refused there as helical finishing is, while axial passes can.
6. **Tool-centre solving for every profile (slice item 2; decision 0012 item 4).** The generic drop-cutter places every point. For a convex profile the drop's line is chosen by the normal-offset fast path: the tool's point whose outward normal is `-n` (`RevolvedProfile::point_for_normal`) is put on the drive point, and the drop then moves the tool along its axis until it touches, so a tool that would cut anything else is raised clear. A non-convex custom profile has no unique point for a normal and is dropped along the line through the drive point. Stock to leave drops the grown tool (decision 0018 item 13). **The climb of decision 0015 item 2 is not built for meshes** (OI-146, deferred by the owner as a future optimisation). Checked against the spike's own code (`spike/sp-4-ray-cast-sweep`, `src/sweep.rs`): the climb computes the same quantity as the drop, the tip height along the tool line, by casting rays parallel to the axis and pattern-searching over the tool disc from the central ray down to a 0.02 mm step. So it is an approximation of the drop, and the trade is:
   - **Accuracy and gouge safety favour the drop.** The drop is exact on the mesh (vertex, facet and edge tests) and exhaustive over every triangle within the cutting radius. The climb was within -0.003 to +0.0015 mm on SP-4's smooth parts, but it is a local search over sampled rays: a contact between the samples, or a second hill of the surface away from the one it climbs, is missed, and a missed contact is a gouge (OI-66 names concave pockets and thin walls as untested for that reason).
   - **Speed favours the climb.** Timed on the same machine, same lobed part, 3 mm tools, 20,000 poses on one thread, release builds: at 150,000 triangles the climb takes 45 us (ball) and 55 us (flat) a pose, the drop 109 us and 46 us; at 985,000 triangles the climb 54 and 58 us, the drop 162 and 104 us. The drop is 2.4 to 3 times slower for a ball, about even for a flat at 150,000 triangles and 1.8 times slower at a million. NFR-04 sets no time budget (it asks for multiple cores and a responsive UI), and simultaneous finishing of SP-4's 20 mm lobed section at 1 mm pitch takes about 6 s on this machine, so the drop is used; a faster drop (warm start from the previous point, or the climb as a seed that the drop then confirms) is the place to recover time if a budget is set.
   - **The climb still has a case the mesh drop does not cover:** exact faces. OI-81 lists "the climb of decision 0015 on exact ray hits" as a candidate for exact tool drops on B-rep faces, where the drop still uses the tessellation. Not building it here leaves that candidate open, not rejected.
7. **Gouge protection between points (OP-12; slice item 5).** The machine moves in a straight line in X, Y, Z and A between points. A move is halved until the tool at the middle of that machine move, dropped along its own axis, moves by no more than `CHORD_FACTOR` (0.4, decision 0018 item 12) times the tolerance, taken along the surface normal: positive when the move would cut in, negative when it would leave material. This measures the cut surface at the motion the machine makes, not a distance to a drive point. A move still cutting in by more than the tolerance after 12 halvings is **refused**, naming its X and A ("reduce the largest tilt or the largest angle step"); that is the rejection the slice asks for, and halving is the fix-up. Holders are P2 (COL-04).
8. **Contact feed (OP-13).** Unchanged: the shared services take the contact length from the five-point smoothed contacts and write durations (decision 0018 item 8). Measured on the lobed part (801 x 2880 mesh, 1 degree steps), the exact contact path under the machine's linear XYZA motion is within **0.09 %** of the programmed feed (median 0.04 %) away from the part's ends.
9. **Notes from a strategy.** `Strategy` gains a provided method `passes_with_notes` returning a new type `StrategyOutput { passes, notes }`; its default runs `passes` or `passes_in_region` and has no notes, so no existing strategy or host changes, and nothing is added to a public struct (`cargo-semver-checks`; work breakdown, "Library API"). `generate` puts a strategy's notes first in `GeneratedPath::notes`, against segment 0, and the application prints them as it does the others. Simultaneous finishing says: the largest X-slope angle and where; the largest tilt used, when `max_tilt` is above 0; and **"the tolerance is measured against the mesh, not the design surface it came from"** whenever a drive point came from a mesh, which is the slice's "the UI says so" for STL parts.
10. **Parameters** beyond the common ones: `pattern` (`spiral`, `axial`), `rotation` (`positive`, `negative`), `start_angle` (A where the tool would face a round part at the start, default 0, as for helical finishing), `max_angle_step` (the largest step round the axis between seeded points, default 5, above 0 to 90; path fitting adds points), `lead_angle` and `max_tilt` (item 3). Axial passes seed a point every 1 mm along X. A job needing more than 5 million drive samples is refused naming the stepover (NFR-07).
11. **Determinism (NFR-06, GC-07).** Drive samples, seeded points and path fitting go through `parallel::map_in_order`; the smoothing is sequential per track. Identical output, notes included, at 1, 2 and N threads.
12. **A fourth reference job** (verification plan section 6; decision 0020 item 8): `lobed`, a lobed vase (`r = 10 + 1.5 sin(2 pi x / 40) + cos(3 angle)`, 20 mm) machined by simultaneous finishing with the same tool, material and machine as the others, so gate 3 replays a program in which A, Y and Z all move with X in the LinuxCNC simulator, and the golden-file audit checks it against the exact part (deepest cut 0.0006 mm; contact feed median 0.17 %, worst 1.96 % away from the ends). [reference-jobs.md](../reference-jobs.md) lists it. It differs from the others in three settings, each for a reason found while making it:
    - **2 degree steps and a 201 x 720 mesh** (the others: 5 degrees, 121 x 144). At 5 degrees path fitting halves the moves where the ball rolls over a lobe, and the shared services' five-point contact smoothing weighs points by count, not spacing, so a halved move beside full ones read up to 38 % off the exact contact feed. Even spacing avoids it here; the smoothing rule itself belongs to OI-77, which this record does not change (it would move every helical golden file).
    - **The audit's largest A step is 10 degrees** for this job (5 for the others): the audit had assumed A is the drive angle, which holds for helical finishing only.
    - **A gate 2 correction in `openchuck-gcode`.** The comparison counted the A travel of the program's first move (from the controller's A 0 to the group's start) but the IR counts none for it, so any toolpath starting away from A 0 failed gate 2: this one starts at A 18.005 degrees, where the lobed part's normal at the start points, and helical finishing with a `start_angle` of 30 failed by 30 degrees. The program's first move now counts no travel on either side; moves between groups count on both, as slice 19 made the IR count them (a first version skipped every group start, which CodeRabbit caught: two operations with A turned between them failed gate 2). Tests in `openchuck-app/tests/posting.rs` cover both cases and fail without the fix.
13. **Test part.** `openchuck_testkit::parts::LobedVase` is SP-4's analytic part, `r = base + wave sin(2 pi x / wave_length) + lobe cos(lobes angle)`, with its exact normal in closed form. With no lobe it is the slice's S-curve surface; with lobes its normal leaves the plane through the axis.

## Alternatives considered

- **The taut string alone for A.** Fewest reversals, but it leaves the normal by the whole band on a plain helix (item 5).
- **Clamping each step (lazy following).** Removes reversals but holds A still and then follows the band's edge, so the tool is as far from the normal as the band allows; rejected for the same reason.
- **A low-pass filter on A.** Simple, but it neither bounds the angle from the normal nor removes a reversal for certain.
- **Facet normals for the tool angle.** SP-4 measured 1.2 degrees of noise, which turns A back and forth on a smooth surface (OI-67).
- **Drive points from the surface's closest point to a grid.** A ray from outside gives a point the tool can reach and works unchanged on exact faces; a closest point can land on a hidden wall.
- **The climb of decision 0015** for tool centres on meshes: faster (2.4 to 3 times for a ball) but a sampled local search that can miss a contact; see item 6. It stays a candidate for exact faces (OI-81).
- **Lead in the plane of travel along X** (axial passes): not possible on a rotary axis; refused rather than ignored.
