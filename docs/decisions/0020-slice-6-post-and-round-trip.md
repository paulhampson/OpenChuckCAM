## Decision (proposed)

1. **Neutral machine program (slice item 1).** `openchuck_post::to_machine_program` turns the IR into `Step`s in the work frame (`RotaryFrame`): comment, tool change, spindle on, rapid, fed move with its `FeedWord`, and unwind. Machine XYZ comes from the shared kinematics. A rapid at an unchanged tip whose A changes by whole turns is an `Unwind`. Moves with no motion are dropped. A fed move with motion and no duration is an error (the feed cannot be worked out). Tool numbers and spindle speeds come from a `ToolSetting` per IR tool tag (GC-14); a tag with none is refused naming the group and operation.
2. **Post definition and options (items 3 and 4; OI-85).** A post is a folder: `post.toml` (`schema`, `name`, `template`, `feed_rule`, `[options]`) and one template. `PostOptions` is typed (units, A letter, direction, unwind mode and command, header, footer, work offset, tool change, coolant, line numbers, decimals, maximum feed, feed mode, comments); unknown keys, wrong types and out-of-range values are refused at load naming the option, the feed mode must agree with the feed rule, and the template is compiled at load so a syntax error names its line. The template sees every option (unset is `none`). Environment as decision 0019 item 3; fuel is 100,000 plus 400 per step. A direction and unwinding are applied in Rust before the template runs: with `drop_turns` the controller keeps the remainder of A with its sign, so later A values are shifted by whole turns to keep every machine position, which the template then writes unchanged.
3. **Loader rules (OI-84).** Newlines normalised to LF; `posts/** -text` in `.gitattributes`; files over 1 MiB refused; the template must be a plain file name in the post folder; comment text from the project has parentheses replaced by brackets, control characters by spaces and non-ASCII by `?`.
4. **`PostProcessor` and selection (item 2; GC-02).** `PostProcessor::post(&Toolpath, &PostJob) -> PostOutput` (text, clamped feed count, estimated seconds); `TemplatePost` is the first implementation. The two posts ship in `posts/` and are embedded (`builtin`, `builtin_ids`), and load from a folder identically. Tying a machine profile to a post is OI-88.
5. **Shipped posts (items 5 and 6).** `posts/linuxcnc` (GC-10) and `posts/makera-z1` (GC-09) share one template (a test keeps the copies identical) and differ by options and feed rule. Z1: SP-12 conventions, `makera` rule, `G92.4 A0 S0` unwind, no G93, no line numbers, `M5` before `T1 M6`, footer `M5`, `G53 G0 Z-3`, `G28`, `M2`. Numbers go through `fixed` (round half to even, no `-0`). Golden files `testdata/golden/post_*_small_part.ngc` (a 6 mm, three-turn helical groove on a 20 mm cylinder, 45 degree steps, with a three-turn unwind) are reviewed line by line: positions match hand calculation (for example A 45 gives Y -7.071, Z 7.071), the Z1 feed 696.2 is `45 * (2 pi 10 + 30) / 360` mm in 1 s, and LinuxCNC's 459.5 is the 7.658 mm chord in 1 s.
6. **Narrower than GC-04 reads (OI-89).** Millimetres only and continuous A only; other values are refused, not approximated.

7. **Reader, replay, gates 1 and 2 (items 7 and 11).** `openchuck-gcode` has the in-house reader of decision 0005 (f64 values, errors naming line and column, `%`, comments of both kinds, block delete, parameters and O-words refused by name, 4,096-character lines), the modal replay (known G and M lists as in SP-5, `Dialect::Linuxcnc` or `MakeraZ1` which also reads `G92.4 A0 S0` as dropping whole turns; arcs are read, recorded and not followed, OI-68), `static_check` (gate 1: units, distance mode, work offset before the first move, a feed on every cutting move and on every G93 move, spindle on while cutting, X, Y, Z and A limits, feed ceiling, tool change at the safe Z and with the spindle off, an end code) and `compare` (gate 2: every reached position mapped back to a tool tip by the shared kinematics and compared with the IR, A modulo whole turns, A travel without unwinds, the cut time under the controller's feed rule written separately from the post's). The fixtures are in `testdata/gcode/` (OI-46: the corrected arc lines are applied). Two fuzz targets, `gcode_reader` and `post_definition`, are in `fuzz/` and CI. **Measured on the shipped posts:** a project posts to files whose replay agrees with the IR to 0.00064 mm at the tip (three axes of 0.0005 mm rounding combine, so the tolerance is half a unit times root 3, 0.00087 mm at three decimals) and 0 degrees in A.
8. **Feeds come from the positions as written.** The controller times a move from the rounded positions in the file, not the IR's. A first version worked out F from unrounded lengths and the end-to-end test found a move 6.6 % off (a 0.009 mm move written as 0.006, 0.007). The neutral stage now rounds positions to the post's decimals first, drops moves that round onto the position before, and works out lengths and feeds from the rounded values; gate 2 does the same on the IR side. A consequence: a very short move gets a very low F (11.3 mm/min in the test), which is correct for that controller rule.
9. **Application and CLI (item 10; GC-02, GC-05, GC-11).** `Application::set_post(PostSelection)` (a built-in id or a post folder) makes regeneration post each run of consecutive operation groups from one setup to its own file (`01-setup-1.nc`), in the setup's work frame and work offset register, with the project's tool numbers and the spindle speed from the cutting data for the setup's stock material (a missing material or data is an error naming it). Each file is checked by gates 1 and 2 and the result is `Regeneration::reports`. `openchuck-cli regenerate --post <id|folder> --out <folder> [--tool-numbers keep|auto]` writes the files, prints the report and fails if any file does not pass. GC-05's other splits (per operation, per tool, one file for the project) are not built.
10. **Gate 3 (item 9; OI-47, OI-93).** `tools/linuxcnc/` holds the headless XYZA configuration (`checks.ini`, `sim.tbl`) and the driver (`sim_driver.py`); `cargo xtask replay [--docker IMAGE] [PROGRAM...]` runs LinuxCNC programs in it (with no program, every LinuxCNC golden file) and `openchuck-cli gcode-sim program.ngc report.json` compares the driver's report with the replay. LinuxCNC comes from a Docker image (`docker/linuxcnc/Dockerfile`, item 13), a run-in-place build (`LINUXCNC_RIP`) or the `PATH`. **Run against LinuxCNC 2.9.10 built from source (13 Oct 2026), idle machine, current programs:**

    | Program | Moves reached | Largest difference | Simulated motion | Faults |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | `post_linuxcnc_small_part.ngc` | 30 | 0.0015 | 29.7 s | none |
    | `reference/sphere_linuxcnc.ngc` | 726 | 0.1000 | 87.2 s | none |
    | `reference/cone_linuxcnc.ngc` | 1086 | 0.1000 | 117.0 s | none |
    | `reference/groove_linuxcnc.ngc` | 1086 | 0.1000 | 151.6 s | none |

    These are the current programs (reference jobs at 5 degree steps, item 14), run through the Docker image of item 13 as its unprivileged user. A difference of 0.1000 is the tolerance: in the container the sampling was sparse at some nodes and the comparison accepted them as passed between two samples, so these reference-job figures are the looser kind of pass. An earlier local run on the 10 degree programs reached every move with differences of 0.0344 to 0.0667.

    All four reach every move in order and end at the program's final position (held to 0.001). **What the first real runs taught** (each changed the code):
    - **LinuxCNC's reported line number lags the motion** by a sample or two, so a per-line "ended at" comparison failed on correct programs (a line seemed to end 0.03 degrees short). The driver now writes every distinct sampled position, and the comparison checks that each program target is visited, in order, within the tolerance (0.1 mm or degrees; samples are 1 ms apart and A moves up to 0.36 degrees per sample on rapids). A node the machine passed between two samples counts (a loaded machine paused sampling once and two nodes seemed missed; the same programs passed on a rerun). Run it on an idle machine. The first run of the small part on its current program failed (a corner was not seen while I was compiling on the same machine) and passed on a rerun alone, so `cargo xtask replay` runs a failing program once more and reports the second result (a real failure repeats); the driver records the longest sampling gap in its report.
    - **Rapids blend without exact stop.** Without `G61` LinuxCNC rounded the corner between `G0 Z30` and `G0 A0`. The LinuxCNC verification post now writes `G61` in its header (its goldens changed). The startup `G61` in the INI does not hold once a program is opened.
    - trivkins treats A as a plain number, so the run confirms the dialect, the reached positions and the absence of faults (limits included), **not the A direction**. The Z1 post (`G92.4`, the Makera feed rule) is not run in LinuxCNC; gates 4 and 5 are.
11. **Reference job set (item 12; [docs/reference-jobs.md](../reference-jobs.md)).** Three generated parts (grooved cylinder, cone, sphere), one tool, helical finishing, both posts, golden files in `testdata/golden/reference/`. The Z1 profile now has the axis at machine Z -55 and the safe Z at 50 mm above it: the owner gave the chuck centre as 45 mm above the bed (13 Oct 2026), taken with the bed at the bottom of the Z travel (-100), which is an assumption (OI-60). The old placeholder of 0 made gate 1 correctly fail every Z, and the old safe Z of 60 would have been above the top of the travel. The air-cut plan is drafted in the reference jobs document for the operator.
12. **Tool numbers (item 13; GC-14, OI-62).** `tool_number_plan` lists the tools the operations use in order of first use with their numbers; `tool_numbering_command` confirms, renumbers in order of use from 1 or applies numbers by hand as one `Command` (one undo step), refusing a number below 1, above a given machine maximum, used twice, or held by a tool no operation uses. The CLI shows the plan and asks on a terminal, takes `--tool-numbers` otherwise, and has `tool-numbers [--auto] [--set TOOL=N] [--max N]`; changes are saved in the project. The UI dialog is slice 19. The machine's tool number range is not in the profile, and the profile now has `max_tool_number`, 1000 by default (the owner: the Z1's range is effectively unlimited, the probe uses a high number, unconfirmed; OI-62 closed). Posting refuses a number outside 1 to the limit, and the CLI uses the profile's limit unless `--max` is given.

13. **LinuxCNC in CI (OI-47).** `docker/linuxcnc/Dockerfile` builds LinuxCNC 2.9.10 from source (Ubuntu 24.04 has no package), run in place, as the unprivileged user `cnc`; `.github/workflows/linuxcnc-image.yml` builds it (pushing to `ghcr.io/<owner>/openchuckcam-linuxcnc:2.9.10` on main, monthly and by hand) and the `gate3` job of `ci.yml` runs `cargo xtask replay --docker` on every change to what the golden programs depend on, pulling the image or building it when the Dockerfile changed. Any later CI task that needs LinuxCNC uses the same image. The manual pages are not built (`make -k`); the Dockerfile checks the files the simulator needs exist.

14. **Golden file audit (review checklist; `cargo xtask golden`).** So that no one has to read the golden files by hand, `crates/openchuck-app/tests/golden_audit.rs` (it also runs with the ordinary tests, so CI runs it on three platforms) reads every committed program and checks it against things that do not come from the post or the path library:
    - the post conventions (exact start and end sequences, `G61` only in the LinuxCNC post, `G92.4 A0 S0` once in the Z1 post and never in LinuxCNC's, no `G93` or `G43`, no line numbers, ASCII, no `-0` or `NaN`, comments alone on their line);
    - gate 1 against the Z1 profile's limits in work coordinates;
    - for the reference jobs, the geometry: each position goes back to the tool through the independent kinematic oracle, the deepest cut into the exact analytic part is under 0.02 mm (measured 0.0005 to 0.0019 mm), the ball rides on the surface (median clearance within 0.02 mm; measured 0.0001 to 0.0019), and A never steps more than 10 degrees or reverses;
    - the contact feed: the speed of the exact contact point over the exact surface, from the file's own F words under the controller's rule, is 480 mm/min (12000 rpm x 2 flutes x 0.02 mm) within 2 % in the middle of the part (median under 0.8 %, worst 1.98 %), judged 4 mm from the ends as the pathing tests do (OI-77). A move slower than programmed is accepted only if its time is exactly what the profile's rotary limit (3600 deg/min) allows (the path library's lengthening, decision 0018 item 8);
    - agreement: the Z1 and LinuxCNC programs of a job reach the same contact points to 0.005 mm;
    - for the small part, its closed forms: X = 0.25 per move, A = 45 per move, the tip on the top of the 10 mm radius, and every move taking 1 s under the controller's rule.

    **What the audit found.** The reference jobs at 10 degree steps had a median contact feed error of 3.1 to 3.4 % (the five-point contact smoothing spans 40 degrees of A on a radius of 10 to 12 mm). The jobs now use 5 degree steps (the strategy's default), which gives the figures above; the golden files doubled to about 1,100 lines (cone, groove) and 740 (sphere). **What it does not cover:** the A direction, the axis height, `G92.4` and the Makera feed rule on the real controller (gate 4), and anything in the first and last 4 mm of a part, where the contact slides round the rim or the pole and the feed is not what OP-13 defines.

## Alternatives considered

- **Feed rule in the template:** decision 0019 item 5 chose Rust.
- **Options as an untyped table:** a mistyped key would pass through `none` checks silently; the typed struct refuses it.
- **A rotary wrap in the template (the spike's `modulo360`):** breaks a cut that crosses the seam; not offered (OI-89).
