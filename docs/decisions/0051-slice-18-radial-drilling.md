## Context

Slice 18 (work breakdown; OP-10): drill holes at indexed A positions, selected from model geometry, with hole detection from cylindrical faces. Lanes C (selection) and D (STEP importer, topology, B-rep `SurfaceSource`) are on `main`, so a hole can be found from the faces of a `BrepModel` and selected by face ID (`BrepSurface::with_faces`). Decisions 0026 (entry moves; a drill's plunge is its normal cut, item 8), 0034 and 0036 (how strategies, notes and the registry are structured) and 0044 (the most recent strategy, with the same layout) were read. The design questions in the slice are hole detection from cylindrical faces, how a hole's axis maps to A, and canned cycles against expanded moves (expanded by default).

## Decision

1. **A hole is a whole-round cylinder whose wall faces the axis, closed or open at each end by the faces next to it (`openchuck_path::holes`).** The wall faces the axis when the face's triangles, wound counter-clockwise seen from outside the material, face it (so a pin is not a hole and a mirrored frame cannot fool the test). Cylinders of one body with the same radius, axis line and extent are one hole (a kernel may split a wall into two halves), and the faces must add to a full turn. For each end, the faces that share an edge with the wall there are found from the edges' faces and polylines; an end is *closed* when the axis pierces a triangle of one of them beyond the end (a flat or conical bottom, and the depth of its deepest point is read from where the axis pierces it), otherwise *open*. One closed end makes a blind hole entered at the open end; two closed ends make a cavity, which is not a hole; no closed end makes a through hole. The plain data comes from `BrepFace`/`BrepEdge`, so nothing here needs the kernel.
2. **Holes are reported by the surface, and selected by face.** `SurfaceSource` gains a provided method `holes() -> &[Hole]` (empty for a mesh, so no host breaks; `CompositeSurface` forwards the model's). `BrepSurface::new` reports every hole; `BrepSurface::with_faces` reports the holes any of whose wall faces are in the selection, with their ends still found in the whole model. Selecting a hole is therefore selecting a face of its wall, which is what the B-rep face selection of lane O will store. A mesh has no holes (the slice: "hole detection on meshes is not planned"), and drilling one says so.
3. **A through hole is entered from the end farther from the rotary axis, and from the end facing up if both are the same distance.** A hole across a rod is drilled from the near side, each wall of a tube from outside. A hole across the rotary axis has two equal ends, so the choice must not depend on the order of the file: the end whose outward direction has the larger Z, then the larger Y.
4. **The hole's axis gives A, and the tool is held over the axis line.** The tool axis is fixed on the machine's Z, so a hole must be radial, with its axis perpendicular to the rotary axis. A is the angle at which the direction out of the hole faces the tool (`RotaryMapping::a_facing`, the same convention as the finishing strategies), and the tool tip is placed on the hole's own axis line, so a hole whose axis does not meet the rotary axis is drilled with the tool offset across it. A hole tilted from the radial plane by more than `axis_tolerance` (default 0.5 degrees) cannot be drilled on four axes; it is left out with a note naming it and the angle. Holes along X are such holes (OI-218).
5. **The tool is chosen for the hole.** A hole is drilled only if its diameter is within `diameter_tolerance` (default 0.1 mm) of the tool's; others are left out with a note, and a larger tolerance drills them as a pilot. Nothing drillable is an error that lists the reasons. The strategy does not require a drill-type tool: it uses the revolved profile like every strategy, so a centre-cutting end mill can drill (the point height is zero), and the application is where a tool type is offered.
6. **Depth.** A through hole is drilled to its length plus the height of the tool's point plus `through_allowance` (default 0.5 mm), so the full diameter breaks through. A blind hole is drilled until the full diameter reaches the model's depth (`depth_reference` = `full_diameter`, the default, so the tip goes a point's height deeper), or until the tip reaches the deepest point of the hole (`tip`, the apex of a drilled point in the model). The top of the hole is the opening or the stock's surface on the axis if higher (the stock is probed along the axis), so a drilled hole starts from the material that is there.
7. **Moves are expanded, one pass per hole, with rapid points.** The pass starts `retract_height` (default 2 mm) above the top, and the shared entry brings the tool there by a rapid and a plunge-feed approach (the entry style is fixed to plunge). The depth is divided into equal pecks no deeper than `peck_depth` (0, the default, drills in one move). Between pecks the tool either retracts to the retract height and rapids back to `reentry_clearance` (0.5 mm) above the last depth (`full_retract`, the default, so chips clear), or backs out `chip_break_retract` (0.5 mm) and feeds on (`chip_break`). The retracts and returns are rapids inside a pass, which the shared services could not express, so **`Pass` gains a public field `rapids: Vec<usize>`**: the indices of the points reached by a rapid. A pass that has any keeps its contact points as given (the smoothing of the contact path would blur a path that doubles back). This is a breaking change (decision 0048), so the workspace is 0.8.0 and the library crates' changelogs say so. The links between holes are the shared ones: leave the hole at the plunge feed to the approach distance above the stock, rapid to the clearance cylinder, turn A, descend.
8. **Order.** Holes at the same A (within 1e-6 degrees) are cut together, in ascending X at the first angle and alternating direction at each next one. The angles are visited in one sweep that starts after the largest gap between them, so A turns the whole turn less that gap and never crosses it; the angles are unwrapped to rise through the sweep. With a limited A range the angles are placed in the range and visited from its low end; a hole whose angle the range cannot reach stops the operation with a message naming it (OP-15).
9. **Canned cycles are not built.** Slice item 4 (a post option for canned cycles) needs the IR to carry a hole cycle and the posts and the reader to understand G81, G83 and G73; expanded moves are the default the slice asks for and work on every post and in the simulator. The item is OI-216, for the owner to schedule. The slice stays open.
10. **Parameters are uniform (OP-16).** The strategy is `radial_drilling` in `Registry::builtin`, with the common parameters first (stepover and stock to leave are described as not used, as for indexed finishing; `entry` offers only `plunge`) and its own after them: `peck_depth`, `peck_mode`, `chip_break_retract`, `retract_height`, `reentry_clearance`, `through_allowance`, `depth_reference`, `diameter_tolerance` and `axis_tolerance`. It does not honour regions or zones: the holes are the selection.

## Alternatives considered

- **The holes as an operation parameter** (a list of face IDs or hole numbers): rejected. The face selection is the model's, lane O stores it, and `BrepSurface::with_faces` already restricts the surface to it, so the strategy needs no new input and no schema number.
- **`holes()` as a function over `BrepModel` the host calls** (and the strategy taking a list): the strategy would then need a hole list in `StrategyInput`, a change to a struct hosts build. A provided trait method adds no required item.
- **Peck retracts as separate passes** (no API change): each peck would be linked at the clearance cylinder and then plunged at the plunge feed from the surface, feeding down the finished hole every time. **Retracts at the cutting feed inside one pass:** slow, and the IR would call them cuts. Both rejected for a `rapids` field, the smallest change that makes the IR say what the machine does.
- **Cutting a pilot with a smaller tool automatically:** rejected; the user chooses the tool and the tolerance.
- **Choosing the sweep direction to suit the previous angle:** the strategy does not know where A is when it starts, and the sweep starts in the same place every time, so the result is the same on every run (GC-07).
