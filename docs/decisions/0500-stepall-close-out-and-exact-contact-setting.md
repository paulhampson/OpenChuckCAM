## Context

Issue #351 closes the STEPALL-0211 programme: a per-strategy matrix of what steps 1 to 5 proved, an adopt or defer disposition per strategy, and the open items reconciled against the evidence. Steps 1 to 5 are merged. The fixed finer toolpath mesh did not qualify (0256). The exact ball drop met both 2-times limits and is used today by helical, indexed, rest and simultaneous finishing on STEP parts (0277). The outline drop for axial finishing's tapered ball and pencil clean-up's flat end is exact and accurate but about 31 and 7 times slower than the tessellation, so no strategy used it (0338, OI-1351).

On 1 Nov 2026 the owner chose to close out with the matrix and, in addition, to let a user trade time for accuracy per operation: the exact surface contact as an opt-in, off by default where it is over the limits. The limits are not changed or declared met.

## Decision

1. **Exact STEP surface contact, a per-operation setting.** Each operation whose strategy drops the tool on a STEP part's faces has a flag, `exact_contact`, labelled *Exact STEP surface contact*. On, the tool is dropped on the exact faces (the region, avoid, check and stock-to-leave sets become exact faces too, OI-833); off, on the import's tessellation. It has no effect on a mesh part.
   - **Ball kind** (helical, indexed, rest and simultaneous finishing): applies to a ball end (also grown for stock to leave), the drop of 0277. **On by default**, which is what these strategies already did, measured within both limits.
   - **Outline kind** (axial finishing and pencil clean-up): applies to every tool with an exact drop, a ball, a tapered ball or a flat end at zero stock to leave, the drop of 0338. **Off by default**: over both limits (OI-835, OI-1351).
   - Rotary roughing (stays tessellated, #351), Facing, swarf, edge following and radial drilling have no such setting.
2. **Where it lives.** A strategy parameter, as `adaptive_spacing` is (0239): uniform schema (OP-16), stored in the operation's parameters, shown by the inspector's generic form in *Pattern and tolerance*, right after *Tolerance* (UI specification section 8). The row is not shown for a mesh part. No new inspector section: the inspector has no surface-source area, and one row does not need one.
3. **The words.** The setting's description says, in plain words, that it is more accurate and slower, with the measured examples and their conditions (one test part; for the outline, one run), no promised figure, and that the program is still the user's to check (NFR-12). The operation's note names the surface each tool was dropped on and why: exact faces; the tessellation because the setting is off; because the tool's shape has no exact drop (bull nose, flat end with stock to leave, sharp V-bit, concave profile; OI-1350); because the strategy's exact contact is for ball ends only; or, for a flat end with stock to leave, the tessellation where stock is left. A tool the setting cannot apply to runs on the tessellation and the form says why under the row (NFR-07); it is never an error.
4. **Library (breaking, 0.37.0).** New module `openchuck_path::contact`: `ExactContact` (`Ball`, `Outline`; `supports`, `default_on`), `with_switch`, `is_on`, `exact_drops`, `parameter::EXACT_CONTACT`, `BALL_STRATEGIES`, `OUTLINE_STRATEGIES`. `Strategy::exact_contact` returns `Option<ExactContact>` (was `bool`). `BrepSurface::with_exact_drops(true)` now drops every tool with an exact drop on the exact faces through `drop_exact` (it dropped only a ball); a host turns it on only where `contact::exact_drops` says so, so the application's output at the defaults is unchanged.
5. **Project schema 27.** An operation of the six strategies saved before it is given the contact it machined with: `exact_contact = true` for the ball kind, `false` for the outline kind; other operations gain nothing. A schema 26 file that already holds the setting is refused, naming it. Schema 26 is lane CLEARANCE's (decision 0530); until it merges, this branch's 25-to-26 step changes nothing and is replaced by CLEARANCE's when the branches meet.
6. **Simultaneous finishing on the blisk** still refuses with the setting on or off: it refuses at the same move on both surfaces (0.1401 mm on the tessellation, 0.1432 mm on the exact faces; 0277), a move-tolerance refusal, not a contact one.
7. **Slow runs.** With the setting on, axial finishing and pencil clean-up can take many times longer. Progress and cancel work as for any operation: the strategies check cancellation between passes and lines, and one exact drop takes well under a millisecond (0338). No time estimate is given (it would be a promised figure); OI-1720 records the gap.
8. **The limits are unchanged.** This does not change or declare met the 2-times limits of OI-835. Turning the outline kind on by default still needs both limits met (OI-1351).

Residual accuracy with exact contacts: the dropped poses lie within the drop's tolerance (1e-4 mm) of the exact faces; what remains between poses is the path fitter's chord (its 0.004 mm limit, 0211 M2 item 3), and with the outline drop the rim slack (0.0001 mm, 0338). The finer fixed mesh met the 0.005 mm allocation at quick stepovers but failed the per-operation limit (helical 2.36, axial 2.01) and cannot import the corpus torus within the STEP worker's 120 s (0256).

Engineering completion, qualification and release are distinct: the ball kind is built, qualified (both limits, 0277) and released (merged, accepted); the outline kind is built and released as a choice the user makes, and not qualified for a default; the finer mesh is not adopted.

## Requirement OP-27 (agreed by the owner, relayed 1 Nov 2026)

The owner chose a new requirement under the OP-12 family (Q3) and agreed this wording, priority and verification row as proposed, relayed by the coordinator on 1 Nov 2026. OP-27 was the next free ID on `main` and every unmerged branch. Added to `docs/requirements.md` after OP-26:

> **OP-27** (P1). Exact surface contact for STEP parts: each operation whose strategy drops the tool on the part offers a setting to drop it on the STEP part's exact surfaces instead of its tessellation, trading generation time for accuracy, where the strategy and the tool support it. Each strategy's default is the contact measured within the generation-time limits; an operation saved before the setting machines as before. Where the setting cannot apply (a mesh part, a tool shape without an exact drop), the application says so in plain words, and the operation still runs.

Verification plan section 3 gains its row (T; unit, integration), and the work breakdown's traceability table lists it under slice 20 with OP-23. The tests below are named by OP-27.

## Alternatives considered

- **Off by default for every strategy** (the first reading of the owner's brief): changes every saved STEP job's G-code and brings back the 0.03 to 0.04 mm cuts that 0277 removed within the limits. The owner chose on for the ball kind (Q1).
- **A new inspector section for the surface source:** one row, and new UI specification surface; not chosen (Q2).
- **The outline drop for the ball-kind strategies' other tools** (for example helical with a tapered ball): it would change those operations' defaults or need a third state; not measured against the limits, and outside the owner's scope. They stay on the tessellation, and the form says so.
- **A time estimate:** would be a promised figure; OI-1720.

## Consequences

- **Output:** unchanged at the defaults, for every strategy, mesh and STEP. Saved projects are migrated to the contact they machined with.
- **Library:** 0.37.0, breaking (`Strategy::exact_contact`'s return type; `with_exact_drops` covers outlines). Changelog entries in each library crate.
- **Screens:** one new row, *Exact STEP surface contact*, in the operation inspector's *Pattern and tolerance* section for the six strategies on a STEP part, and new note wording; the user guide (lane DOCSUSER) needs a line.
- **Documents:** this record; the decision index; `docs/open-items.md`; the work breakdown's STEPALL row and schema table (27); the verification plan's *Exact surfaces* note; the UI specification's section 8; changelogs.
