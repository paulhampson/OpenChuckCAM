## Context

The owner's blisk samples (`blisk_helical_5`, `blisk_helical_2`, `blisk_axial`; decision [0096](0096-slice-20-end-to-end-and-determinism.md) item 1) gave different Makera Z1 programs on Linux, Windows and macOS in the CI run on 7b7b823 (pull request 98). Lane TE recorded that only the F words differed (OI-319) and the owner took the tests out of every automated run until the contact feed is the same on every platform.

**What differs between platforms.** Rust's `f64` arithmetic, `sqrt` and the integer-power intrinsic are IEEE and bit-identical on the three CI targets, but `sin`, `cos`, `atan2` and `hypot` come from each platform's mathematics library, which rounds the last bit differently on some arguments. The shared kinematics (`openchuck-types::kinematics`, `sin_cos` of A) therefore place every tool line, and so every drop, a unit in the last place apart from one platform to the next. That noise is harmless on its own: a position written to three decimals changes only at a rounding tie, with a probability near 1e-13 per word. It matters where a *choice* turns on which of two equal numbers comes out larger.

**The chain, found on Linux** by perturbing the kinematics' `sin_cos` by one unit in the last place (a temporary experiment, not committed) and comparing the blisk programs:

1. The perturbed `blisk_helical_5` program reproduced macOS's first differing line exactly (`X10.660 Z19.949 A1918.750 F334.2` in place of `F306.8`), with an extra fitting point inserted two lines later. Lane TE's "positions are bit-identical" was an inference from the first differing line only: the golden check reports the first difference, and the F word of a move changes two lines *before* an inserted point, because the contact feed is measured on contacts smoothed over two neighbours either side (decision 0018 item 13). The real difference is the set of points the path fitter keeps.
2. The path fitter (`wrap_fit::Fit::deviation`) measured a move's error along the surface normal at the dropped contact, taken from `SurfaceSource::project`. On a faceted mesh a ball mostly touches *edges* and *vertices*, and a point on an edge is as close to each facet that meets there, up to rounding; parry's best-first search returned whichever facet rounding favoured. At the disc's rim the two facets' normals are 90 degrees apart, so the same contact gave an error of 0.0184 mm (split the move) on one side of the noise and 0.0011 mm (keep it) on the other, against the chord limit of 0.004 mm. One flipped decision inserts or removes a point; the contact smoothing then changes the F word of the moves around it.
3. The drop-cutter found an edge contact by golden-section search on the tip height along the edge (decision 0012 item 5). The peak is flat, so comparing heights locates it only to the square root of rounding: under one unit of mesh noise the blisk's edge contacts moved by up to 2.5e-7 mm (1,973 of 5,284 contacts by more than 1e-9). A contact length that noisy flips an F word written to one decimal only at a rounding tie, which happened once in the eight reference jobs (`lobed`, line 1642, `F410.6` against `F410.5`): rare, but a GC-07 breach on any platform whose rounding falls the other way.
4. Two drop candidates with the same tip height but different contacts (a ball resting on both flanks of a symmetric groove, a flat tool on a flat facet and its edges) were decided by a strict comparison, so by rounding. The blisk has no such tie (the nearest competing contact is 2.2e-5 mm lower everywhere), but the case is structural on any symmetric groove.

The reproduction is now a test: `crates/openchuck-path/tests/rounding_noise.rs` runs helical finishing on `testdata/stl/blisk.stl` as read and with every vertex's coordinates across the axis moved one unit in the last place, and requires the same moves with the same tips, A and contact lengths. Before this change the two toolpaths had 7,448 and 7,499 moves.

## Decision

1. **Projection onto a mesh is the same from either side of an edge** (`MeshSurface::project`). After the nearest triangle is found, every triangle within `PROJECT_TIE` (one part in 1e9 of the mesh's diagonal, at least 1e-9 mm) of that distance counts as equally near; the lowest-numbered gives the point and the normal is the mean of all their normals. A point on an edge or at a vertex answers the same whichever facet rounding favours; a point clearly on one facet keeps that facet's normal.
2. **The drop-cutter breaks ties by rule** (`cutter::TIP_TIE`, 1e-9 mm). A candidate replaces the best only when its tip is higher by more than the tie; within the tie the lowest-numbered triangle wins, and within a triangle the candidate found first (vertex, then edges, then facet). The early stop skips a triangle only when it cannot reach within the tie of the best. The composite target (model against holding solids) and the per-zone drop for stock to leave apply the same tie: a solid or a later zone must be higher by more than it to win. The rule is not transitive at exactly the tie, which needs two materially different contacts about 1e-9 mm apart in tip height: not a structural case.
3. **An edge contact is found where the tip height's derivative changes sign**, by bisection to rounding precision, on the line and convex-arc pieces where the height along the edge is concave (the cases decision 0012 item 5 names as exact to rounding for the golden section). The root of a derivative is well conditioned where the peak is flat, so the contact moves by rounding only when the inputs do (test: under one unit of noise on the tool line it moves by less than 1e-12 mm, against 1.1e-8 before). Concave arcs keep the sampled search and its golden refinement: no standard tool has one, and the noise there is recorded in OI-336.
4. **The fitter measures a move's deviation along the tool's contact normal** (`cutter::contact_normal`: the profile's outward normal at the contact, turned into the model frame). Where the tool touches a smooth surface this is the surface normal, as decision 0018 item 12 asks; at a mesh edge or vertex, where the surface has none, it is the normal of the surface the tool can reach there, which varies continuously as the tool rolls over the edge and which rounding cannot flip. The mean facet normal of item 1 was tried here first: it is robust too, but at a wall it splits the tool's vertical drop (a move along the wall's face, which cuts nothing new) into several wall moves, undoing decision [0050](0050-walls-merge-micro-moves.md) (the walls test went from 3 wall moves to 9). The tool's own normal at a wall contact is the wall's, so the drop stays one move. Where the contact is not on the tool's lower envelope (a defensive case, NFR-07) the surface's own normal is used as before.
5. **The blisk tests return to the automated runs** (`#[ignore]` removed from the three `blisk_` samples of `apps/openchuck-cli/tests/e2e.rs`): **the owner's decision (18 Oct 2026, option A, relayed by the coordinator session, decision 0049)**, which reverses the owner's stopgap of 17 and 18 Oct 2026 in OI-311 and OI-319 (the blisk by hand only, out of every automated run). Lane BX-a cannot run Windows or macOS: a draft pull request runs no Tests job (decision 0055), so the first three-platform check is the ready run of this pull request. If a platform still differs there, the pull request fails and comes back to this lane, and the fallback under *Alternatives* becomes a new decision for the owner. Each blisk test takes 15 to 24 s uninstrumented (`blisk_helical_5` 15.4 s, `blisk_helical_2` 18.7 s, `blisk_axial` 23.8 s, debug build, the three run together on four cores), so none is long-running (`.config/nextest.toml` is unchanged).

## What stays noise-sensitive (OI-336)

- A choice at exactly the tie (two contacts about 1e-9 mm apart in tip height, a point about `PROJECT_TIE` from a second facet, a fitting error within rounding of the chord limit, a written word at a rounding tie): these need a coincidence of the geometry with the tie, not a structural case, and have a probability near 1e-12 per decision.
- The sampled edge search on a concave arc piece (item 3) and the entry arcs, lead angles and `a_facing`, which still call the platform's `sin`, `cos`, `tan`, `acos` and `atan2`: positions, not choices, so visible only at a rounding tie of a written word.
- Simultaneous finishing takes its drive normal from `cast_ray` smoothed over neighbours (decision 0036 item 4), not from `project`; a ray that meets a mesh edge exactly gets one facet's normal. Its drive points are sampled, so the case is not structural, but it is not covered by item 1.
- Helical finishing's seed count is `ceil(360 length / (pitch step))`: at an exact integer (the blisk: 9000 / 5) a change of the part's length in the last place changes the step. Mesh coordinates come from the file exactly, so no platform changes them; a B-rep's bounds could.
- Whether Windows and macOS now agree is unverified until the ready run.

## Also investigated: OI-318 (simultaneous finishing refuses the blisk)

Reproduced through the library on the blisk mesh with the 3 mm ball (0.7 to 9.5 s each, debug build): `max_angle_step` 5 and `max_tilt` 10 or 0 refuse at X 10.284, A 1902.944 (0.0169 mm); step 2 at X 11.108, A 1765.109 (0.0975 mm); the axial pattern at X 11.500, A 0.703 (1.8295 mm), the owner's figures. At every refusal the two nodes either side of the move are 1.4e-7 apart in the track parameter (the twelfth halving) and the tool between them jumps:

- At X 10.28 and X 11.11 the drive normal jumps by about 80 degrees from one node to the next (the ray from outside meets a blade face on one side and the disc's rim or the hub on the other), so with `max_tilt` 0 the tool axis jumps with it, and with `max_tilt` 10 the seed point of the convex fast path (decision 0036: the tool placed so that its point with the surface's normal touches the drive point) moves sideways by up to the ball radius; the drop then lands on another surface and the tip jumps 1.3 to 2.7 mm. No straight move can follow a jump, so the refusal is the strategy's gouge protection working as designed (OP-12), not the tolerance.
- At X 11.5 on the axial pattern the tool rises along its own axis from the hub to the shoulder's rim, a wall step of 3.7 mm. The between-point test drops the tool at the middle of the move and reads a positive offset as a cut; on a face parallel to the tool axis the tool slides without cutting, so the offset (half the wall's height, 1.83 mm) is not a gouge. Helical and axial finishing meet walls in their own fitter and assembly (decision 0050); simultaneous finishing's fitter has no wall case.

So OI-318 is a limit of simultaneous finishing as designed on a part with sharp edges and a wall facing along the axis, not a wrong computation and not the 0.01 mm tolerance (verification plan 4.1, unchanged); the refusal is right, its advice ("reduce the largest tilt or the largest angle step") is not, since no tilt or step helps. The fix is design work in the slice 13 strategy (a seed whose lateral position does not follow the drive normal, and a penetration test rather than the drop offset at a wall), recorded as a proposal in OI-337 for a lane that owns `simultaneous.rs`; until then the owner's boundary that keeps the strategy off the shoulder (OI-318) is the way to run it. Nothing in the strategy is changed by this record.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Route every transcendental call through the `libm` crate** (pure Rust, bit-identical across platforms; what parry's `enhanced-determinism` feature does, OI-64). It would make the platforms agree without touching the choices, but it hides the structural ties rather than removing them (the same noise would then decide the same way everywhere, until a compiler or crate upgrade), it changes Linux's results too (every golden file would move), and it is a dependency outside the crate selection (NFR-14). Kept as the fallback if the ready run still shows a difference.
2. **Per-platform golden files.** Verification plan policy 7 allows them only where a difference is unavoidable; it is not.
3. **Keep the blisk tests manual.** The status quo of OI-319; the owner asked for the investigation.
4. **The mean facet normal in the fitter's deviation.** Tried; see item 4.
