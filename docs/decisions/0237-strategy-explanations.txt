Rotary roughing
Removes most of the material from the stock in layers, working from the outside in. The part can turn continuously or be held at chosen angles. Use it before finishing; stock to leave sets how much material to keep for the finishing tool.

Facing
Reduces the stock along its length to flat faces at chosen rotary angles, or to a round diameter. Use it to prepare stock before roughing. The part and workholding can limit the cut.

Helical finishing
Follows the surface in a spiral around the part as the tool moves along its length. Use it for a general finish on round shapes. Selections and gaps can split the spiral into separate cuts, and the tool may not reach tight inside corners.

Axial finishing
Follows the surface in passes along the length of the part, turning the part a small step between passes. Use it where marks running along the part suit the job better than a spiral.

Indexed finishing
Turns the part to each chosen angle, holds it still and follows the surface along its length. Use it for features reached from a few set directions. It does not turn the part during a cutting pass.

Simultaneous 4-axis finishing
Moves the tool while turning the part to approach the surface from different directions. Use it for curved shapes that need the cutting angle to change along a pass. Four-axis motion cannot follow every surface direction; check the resulting path and simulation.

Rest machining
Uses the stock left by earlier operations in the same setup to find and cut remaining material. Use a smaller tool to reach areas a larger tool left behind, such as inside corners. The remaining material is estimated from the stock simulation.

Pencil clean-up
Follows supported sharp inside corners to remove ridges of material left by other finishing operations. Use it as a final clean-up, rather than to cut large areas. It does not cover every inside corner or smooth transition.

Edge and curve following
Moves the tool along the part's edges or supplied curves to cut lines for engraving or carving. It follows lines rather than clearing areas.

Swarf (flank) machining
Cuts selected wall faces, such as a blade, with the side of the tool. The wall can curve along its length, with straight lines running across its height for the tool's side to follow. Needs a STEP model. Some faces make the tool cut into the surface by more than the operation's tolerance; a warning gives the depth and location. Check the warning, toolpath and simulation before running the program.

Radial drilling
Drills holes into the side of the part, turning the part so each hole faces the tool. Needs a STEP model to find the round holes. Use a drill that matches the hole size.
