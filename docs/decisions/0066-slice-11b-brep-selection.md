## Context

Slice 16's last box ("edge selections persist across save and load") waits on OI-204: a stored choice of edge chains. For a STEP part the existing `Edges` target (schema 1) holds that choice; a mesh has no stored edge kind.

Edge following on a STEP part drops edges where two faces meet smoothly unless it is on (a crease angle of 0 in the B-rep source, decision 0053 item 2; a filleted box gives 48 chains with it on and none with it off). Lane Z builds the seam that takes the resolved flag (the set's value, else the project's default, else off), off until lane O wires it.

## Decision

1. **Schema 12: a mesh edge selection kind (OI-204; the choice a reviewer may change).** A selection set's target may be `mesh_edges`: edges of a mesh model's triangles, each named `[triangle, side]`, side `side` (0 to 2) of facet `triangle` of the file, from corner `side` to corner `side + 1`. Triangle `i` is facet `i` of the file whatever the placement (decision 0032), so the name is relative to the file's content hash, as a triangle selection is. An edge shared by two triangles has two names; the application stores the lower, so a set holds each edge once. Mesh edge sets apply to mesh models only (the project refuses one on a STEP model, and refuses renaming a model to a STEP file while it has one). `openchuck-project` gains `MeshEdge`, `SelectionTarget::MeshEdges`, `SelectionTarget::len`, `is_empty` and `kind_name`, and `From` a set of each kind (it is not in the library family).
2. **A STEP part is drawn with its own edges (UI-02, UI-04; slice 11 item 5).** The scene item of a STEP part keeps the face of each triangle of its joined tessellation and its edges (`SceneBrep`): every edge but seams and edges of no length, tangent edges included (`BrepEdges::with_crease(0.0)`), so each face's outline shows. They replace the tessellation's feature edges, in the same colour and under the same display mode, so a STEP part's faces read as faces. A part whose faces cannot be evaluated is drawn as a mesh with a note. `openchuck-path` gains `BrepEdges::edge_turns` (a new method) so the viewport uses lane P's rule for seams and tangency rather than a copy of it. Until OI-242 the scene receives no placed B-rep, so this path runs in tests only.
3. **Surfaces or edges: a switch in the selection tools box (UI-04; the choice a reviewer may change).** The tools box (specification 7.2) gains "Surfaces" and "Edges" above the tools. With Surfaces a click selects triangles of a mesh or faces of a STEP part; with Edges it selects the edges drawn on the part: a STEP part's own edges, or a mesh's feature edges. A set holds one kind, so a click of the other kind starts the working selection again. Alternative: pick the edge when the pointer is within a few pixels of one and the surface otherwise, as many CAD tools do; rejected as the default because a click near an edge of a small part would select the edge when a face was meant, and the switch makes the kind of a new set explicit. "Select in view" on an edge set turns Edges on.
4. **What the tools select on faces and edges (UI-04).** The keys keep their meaning (specification 15). On a STEP part's faces: Q the face; W the faces joined to it through edges (the body); E the faces reached across edges whose faces turn by at most the angle (default 30 degrees); R the faces all of whose triangles face within the angle of the picked triangle. On edges: Q the edge; W its chain, through corners where exactly two drawn edges meet (a cylinder's rim on a mesh in one click; a block's corner ends a chain); E the chain onward through each corner to the edge that turns least while it turns at most the angle; R is not offered for edges. The tools read "Select one", "Chain" and "Smooth chain" while Edges is on. Face and edge selection runs on the scene's own data on the interface thread (face adjacency, edge turns and chains are built in the scene job), so it needs no job; triangle fills stay jobs (decision 0037).
5. **Edges are picked near the pointer, never through the part (UI-04).** An edge is picked when the pointer's ray passes within 6 pixels of it (`openchuck_geom::pick::EdgePicker`, a new type: the polyline nearest the ray as a share of a reach that grows with distance in a perspective view and is constant in an orthographic one), and only up to just past the surface the ray meets first (twice the reach), so an edge on the part's outline is picked and one behind the part is not. Of two equally near, the nearer the eye. Each part's edges are tested segment by segment after a box test; no hierarchy is built (OI-243).
6. **Highlights (specification 7.4).** The hovered face is outlined in yellow by its edges, as a hovered triangle is by its sides; a hovered edge is drawn in yellow over the part. Selected faces fill in the working blue or the set's swatch, like triangles; selected edges are drawn over the part in the working blue or the set's swatch. All are one-pixel lines (OI-202), so a selected edge differs from the others by colour only (OI-245).
7. **Saving and editing sets of every kind (UI-05, PRJ-06; slice 11 item 7).** `Application::save_selection` takes any target and checks it (`check_target`) against the model: the kind the model is selected by, not empty, every triangle, face or edge in the model, and the file unchanged since it was added. `SelectionEdit::Add`, `Remove` and `Replace` take a target of the set's kind (they took triangles); an edit of another kind is refused. The selection panel lists the kind and edits every kind. The UI offers no triangle selection on a STEP model: a pick on one names its face. Saving a face or edge set through the application needs the placed B-rep (OI-242); the check itself is tested on the closed-form blank.
8. **Edge following on a mesh follows its chosen edges (OP-08, OI-204).** A mesh edge set in an edge-following operation's selections chooses the edges it follows: the chosen segments are chained at corners where exactly two meet (`openchuck_path::curves::ChosenSegments`, a new `CurveSource`) with no crease angle (the user chose them) and the operation's shortest chain. A mesh edge set as a boundary, avoid or check set, in another strategy, beside a triangle set, or made on another model or version is refused with a message naming the set (OP-02). This is a few lines in the mesh path of `operation.rs`; the STEP path is lane Z's.
9. **Keep tangent edges: per edge set, with a project default (owner's request; the choice a reviewer may change).** In schema 12 a set of B-rep edges gains an optional `keep_tangent_edges` (true or false), written only when the user set it; `project.toml` gains `keep_tangent_edges`, the project's default, written only when true (off is the default). Only an `Edges` set may hold one (the project refuses it on any other kind). What applies is the set's value, else the project's default (`Project::keeps_tangent_edges(set)`); lane Z's seam receives that value in the OI-242 follow-up, so until then edge following on a STEP part keeps its 1 degree crease. The resolved value is part of the operation's selection inputs hash for operations that use an edge set (and of no other), so changing it marks exactly those operations stale. The interface: where an edge selection is made and edited, the Selection sets panel shows "Tangent edges" with "Project default (drop)" or "(keep)", "Keep tangent edges" and "Drop tangent edges": for the working selection before a set is made from it, and for a chosen edge set; the project's default is a "Keep tangent edges by default" box in the same panel, because the application has no project settings screen (specification 9 lists none). Setting the default is one undoable command (`Command::SetKeepTangentEdges`); the set's value is a set edit (`SelectionEdit::KeepTangentEdges`). Options considered: (a) per set with a project default, as built (the owner's request); (b) an operation parameter, which would split one choice between the set and every operation using it and repeat it per operation; (c) project-wide only, which the owner ruled out. Recommendation: (a). Mesh edge sets do not hold it: a mesh's chosen segments are followed as chosen, with no crease angle (item 8).
10. **Library family: new items only.** `openchuck-path` gains `ChosenSegments` and `BrepEdges::edge_turns`; `openchuck-geom` (not in the family) gains `EdgePicker`, `PickTolerance` and `EdgeHit`. No public struct gains a field and nothing is removed, so `cargo-semver-checks` has nothing to flag and the workspace stays 0.8.0 (decision 0048).

The schema 11 to 12 migration changes only the number; a schema 11 file that already holds `mesh_edges` or `keep_tangent_edges` was not written by any version that knew them and is refused with what to do, as schema 8 did for `centre_cutting` (decision 0033). The change is additive, so there is no fixture folder.

## Alternatives considered

- **Merging lane Z's branch (option A) or duplicating its placement (option B).** A would tie this lane's merge to decision 0056 and lane Z's CI, and B would duplicate `transform_brep` and `placed_part` and guarantee a conflict; the orchestrator chose C (above).
- **Mesh edges named by vertex pairs.** The importer's vertex numbering (or a weld tolerance) would become part of the format; a triangle and side needs only the facet order, which decision 0032 already fixes.
- **Storing chains as polylines.** A set would keep geometry that goes stale silently when the file changes; IDs relative to the hash are checked like every other selection (PRJ-06), and re-matching by position is PRJ-07 (P2), for which `descriptors` are reserved.
- **One kind "edges" for both models.** The B-rep `Edges` target holds stable edge IDs; a mesh has none. Two kinds keep the file honest; the interface calls both "edges".
- **The B-rep edge display at the operation's 1 degree crease (tangent edges hidden).** It would hide face boundaries the user may want to select (a fillet's edge) and make the drawn edges depend on an operation's setting.
- **Face and edge selection as background jobs, as triangle fills are.** The data are small and already in the scene; a job would add a frame of delay and a cancellation path for no gain.
