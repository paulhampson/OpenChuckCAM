## Context

Slice 11 items 5 and 7: the viewport draws a STEP part's faces and edges distinctly and selects them (UI-04), and B-rep selections are stored as face and edge IDs relative to the model's content hash (PRJ-06), with no triangle selection on STEP models. Slice 16's last box ("edge selections persist across save and load") waits on OI-204: a stored choice of edge chains. For a STEP part the existing `Edges` target (schema 1) holds that choice; a mesh has no stored edge kind.

The storage of B-rep selections was already in the format: a selection set's target may be `faces` or `edges` (schema 1, architecture section 5.6), and the project refuses a triangle set on a STEP model and a face or edge set on a mesh. What was missing is making and showing them, which needs the placed B-rep (lane Z), and a mesh edge kind.

## Decision

1. **Schema 12: a mesh edge selection kind (OI-204; the choice a reviewer may change).** A selection set's target may be `mesh_edges`: edges of a mesh model's triangles, each named `[triangle, side]`, side `side` (0 to 2) of facet `triangle` of the file, from corner `side` to corner `side + 1`. Triangle `i` is facet `i` of the file whatever the placement (decision 0032), so the name is relative to the file's content hash, as a triangle selection is. An edge shared by two triangles has two names; the application stores the lower, so a set holds each edge once. Mesh edge sets apply to mesh models only (the project refuses one on a STEP model, and refuses renaming a model to a STEP file while it has one). The schema 11 to 12 migration changes only the number; a schema 11 selection file that already holds `mesh_edges` is refused with what to do, as schema 8 did for `centre_cutting` (decision 0033). `openchuck-project` gains `MeshEdge`, `SelectionTarget::MeshEdges`, `SelectionTarget::len`, `is_empty` and `kind_name`, and `From` a set of each kind (it is not in the library family). No fixture folder: the change is additive.
2. **A STEP part is drawn with its own edges (UI-02, UI-04; slice 11 item 5).** The scene keeps, for a STEP part, the face of each triangle of its joined tessellation and its edges (`SceneBrep`): every edge but seams and edges of no length, tangent edges included (`BrepEdges::with_crease(0.0)`), so each face's outline shows. They replace the tessellation's feature edges, in the same colour and under the same display mode (shaded with edges, wireframe), so a STEP part's faces read as faces, not as triangles. A part whose faces cannot be evaluated is drawn as a mesh with a note. `openchuck-path` gains `BrepEdges::edge_turns` (a new method) so the viewport uses lane P's rule for seams and tangency rather than a copy of it.
3. **Surfaces or edges: a switch in the selection tools box (UI-04; the choice a reviewer may change).** The tools box (specification 7.2) gains "Surfaces" and "Edges" above the tools. With Surfaces a click selects triangles of a mesh or faces of a STEP part; with Edges it selects the edges drawn on the part: a STEP part's own edges, or a mesh's feature edges. A set holds one kind, so a click of the other kind starts the working selection again. Alternative: pick the edge when the pointer is within a few pixels of one and the surface otherwise, as many CAD tools do; rejected as the default because a click near an edge on a small part would select the edge when a face was meant, and the switch makes the kind of a new set explicit. "Select in view" on an edge set turns Edges on.
4. **What the tools select on faces and edges (UI-04).** The keys keep their meaning (specification 15). On a STEP part's faces: Q the face; W the faces joined to it through edges (the body); E the faces reached across edges whose faces turn by at most the angle (default 30 degrees); R the faces all of whose triangles face within the angle of the picked triangle. On edges: Q the edge; W its chain, through corners where exactly two drawn edges meet (so a cylinder's rim on a mesh, or a chain of B-rep edges round a pocket, in one click; a block's corner ends a chain); E the chain onward through each corner to the edge that turns least while it turns at most the angle (a smooth outline through corners where more edges meet); R is not offered for edges. The tools are named "Select one", "Chain" and "Smooth chain" while Edges is on. Face and edge selection runs on the scene's own data on the interface thread (the face adjacency, the turn of each edge and the edge chains are built in the scene job), so it needs no job; triangle fills stay jobs (decision 0037).
5. **Edges are picked near the pointer, never through the part (UI-04).** An edge is picked when the pointer's ray passes within 6 pixels of it (`openchuck_geom::pick::EdgePicker`, a new type: the polyline nearest the ray as a share of a reach that grows with distance in a perspective view and is constant in an orthographic one), and only up to just past the surface the ray meets first (twice the reach), so an edge on the part's outline is picked and one behind the part is not. Of two equally near, the nearer the eye. Each part's edges are tested segment by segment after a box test; no hierarchy is built (OI-242).
6. **Highlights (specification 7.4).** The hovered face is outlined in yellow by its edges, as the hovered triangle is by its sides; a hovered edge is drawn in yellow over the part. Selected faces fill in the working blue or the set's swatch, like triangles; selected edges are drawn over the part in the working blue or the set's swatch. All are one-pixel lines (OI-202), so a selected edge differs from the others by colour only (OI-244).
7. **Saving and editing sets of every kind (UI-05, PRJ-06; slice 11 item 7).** `Application::save_selection` takes any target (triangles, mesh edges, faces, B-rep edges) and checks it against the model as read now: the kind the model is selected by, not empty, every triangle, face or edge in the model, and the file unchanged since it was added. `SelectionEdit::Add`, `Remove` and `Replace` take a target of the set's kind (they took triangles); an edit of another kind is refused. The selection panel lists the kind ("Faces", "Edges", "Triangles") and edits every kind. The UI does not offer triangle selection on a STEP model: a pick on one names its face.
8. **Edge following on a mesh follows its chosen edges (OP-08, OI-204).** A mesh edge set in an edge-following operation's selections chooses the edges it follows: the chosen segments are chained at corners where exactly two meet (`openchuck_path::curves::ChosenSegments`, a new `CurveSource`) with no crease angle (the user chose them) and the operation's shortest chain. A mesh edge set as a boundary, avoid or check set, in another strategy, beside a triangle set, or made on another model or version is refused with a message naming the set (OP-02), never dropped. On a STEP part the `Edges` set chooses B-rep edges, as lane Z built.
9. **Library family: new items only.** `openchuck-path` gains `ChosenSegments` and `BrepEdges::edge_turns`; `openchuck-geom` (not in the family) gains `EdgePicker`, `PickTolerance` and `EdgeHit`. No public struct gains a field and nothing is removed, so `cargo-semver-checks` has nothing to flag and the workspace stays 0.8.0 (decision 0048).

## Alternatives considered

- **Mesh edges named by vertex pairs.** The importer's vertex numbering (or a weld tolerance) would become part of the format; a triangle and side needs only the facet order, which decision 0032 already fixes for triangle sets.
- **Storing chains as polylines.** A set would keep geometry that goes stale silently when the file changes; IDs relative to the hash are checked like every other selection (PRJ-06), and re-matching by position is PRJ-07 (P2), for which `descriptors` are reserved.
- **One kind "edges" for both models.** The B-rep `Edges` target holds stable edge IDs; a mesh has none. Two kinds keep the file honest; the interface calls both "edges".
- **The B-rep edge display at the operation's 1 degree crease (tangent edges hidden).** It would hide face boundaries the user may want to select (a fillet's edge) and make the drawn edges depend on an operation's setting.
- **Face and edge selection as background jobs, as triangle fills are.** The data are small (faces and edges, not triangles) and already in the scene; a job would add a frame of delay and a cancellation path for no gain.

## Consequences

- **Code:** `openchuck-project` (schema 12), `openchuck-geom` (`EdgePicker`), `openchuck-path` (`ChosenSegments`, `BrepEdges::edge_turns`), `openchuck-app` (`scene` `SceneBrep`, `pick` face and edge picking, `selection` every target kind and mesh edge resolution, `operation` mesh edges in edge following), `openchuck-ui` (viewport edges and edge picking, the selection panel and tools box), the STEP kernel CI job.
- **Documents:** the work breakdown (schema 12 claim, slices 11 and 16, lane O, the numbers), the verification plan rows UI-04 and PRJ-06, the open-items register, the decision index, the changelogs. The interface specification's sections 7.2 and 15 do not mention the Surfaces and Edges switch or the edge tool names; the change is proposed here (items 3 and 4) and the specification is not edited until it is agreed (OI-243).
- **Open items:** OI-242 (edge picking time on a large mesh not measured against the picking budget), OI-243 (the specification's tools box and keys to gain the Surfaces and Edges switch and the edge tool names), OI-244 (a selected edge differs from the others by colour only while lines are one pixel). Closed on acceptance: OI-119 (with lane Z), OI-204, OI-227 (a chain is highlighted and stored as the edges picked, not mapped from an `EdgeChain`).
