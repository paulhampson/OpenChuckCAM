## Context

Decision 0515 marked the surfaces simultaneous finishing and rest machining cannot reach with a two-tone dot grid. `fs_hatch` drew it from the fragment's screen position, 12 logical pixels apart at any zoom, so the dots stayed where they were on the screen while the part turned under them. The owner reported (issue #637) that this is uncomfortable to look at, may trigger trypophobia, and is "not painted onto the surface"; the expected result is "a better pattern". The same screen-space rule is right for the line hatches of sets, avoid surfaces and held regions (decision 0216, 7.4 and 7.5), which stay as they are.

Review frames of three candidates on the owner's face-flow project (the blisk hub floor, 208.6 mm2 unreached) and on the baluster with a 2 mm ball rest operation (9 areas) were sent for choice: A hatching, B a flat tint with an outline, C hatching with an outline. The owner chose A. The frames are on the throwaway branch `claude/viewhatch-shots` (commit 55e03538, `shots/`, not merged).

## Decision

1. **Hatching painted on the surface (owner: A).** `openchuck_viewport::HatchKind::Surface { ring }` replaces `HatchKind::Dots`. Its lines are where parallel planes cut the mesh, in the mesh's own (model) frame: a fragment's line coordinate is `dot(position, n) / spacing` with `n = (1, 1, 1) / sqrt(3)` and a spacing of `SURFACE_HATCH_SPACING_MM` = 1 mm. So the pattern is a function of the surface point alone: it moves, scales and foreshortens with the part and does not depend on the camera. A normal along no axis is used so that a cylinder along X (the rotary axis) and the flat end faces both carry lines. Along a face the lines are `sqrt(3)` mm apart in X (about 1.7 mm).
2. **Two tones (unchanged colours).** A core of `#0c0e12` (`roles::AREA_HATCH_CORE`, was `AREA_DOT_CORE`) inside a white casing `#ffffff` (`AREA_HATCH_CASING`): the core is 9% and the casing 20% of a line period wide. The core reads on the lighter grounds and the casing on the darker ones, as the dots did; the numbered markers keep their badge colours. The two colours are used by no other role.
3. **No shimmer at a distance.** The line widths follow the screen-space derivative of the line coordinate (`fwidth`): lines keep at least their millimetre width and are drawn anti-aliased, and as they near the pixel size (a period under about 7 pixels) they fade into a flat 30% tint of the core, so a far or oblique view does not alias into moire when the view moves. No repeating small circles remain.
4. **Over what lies beneath.** As the dots were: a transparent fill in the layer `Areas`, so the part's grey and the deviation colours (decision 0492) show between the lines. The legend row "Unreachable (operation name)" shows a swatch of two hatch lines in place of the two dots.
5. **The footprint is closed (owner, 8 Nov 2026).** The marked triangles of an area are the triangles its sample points lie on (item 2 of 0515), which are few and leave unmarked triangles between them, so the hatch showed as ticks across a narrow strip. `openchuck_app::unreached::close_footprint` now marks an unmarked triangle that two marked triangles on the same surface hold (they share an edge with it), in up to six passes, with three limits that keep it from marking what the strategy did not report: (a) the neighbour must lie on the same surface, its normal within 25 degrees of the triangle's, so closing never crosses a crease or a lip (item 2 of 0515); (b) the added triangle must lie within `FOOTPRINT_REACH` = 1.5 mm of a sample point of the area, so the region is never larger than the unreached area by more than that distance and a reached surface is not hidden; (c) nothing marked is unmarked. The result is deterministic (ordered sets; GC-07). `triangles_on` and `match_points` are unchanged; the closing is applied where the areas are mapped (`map_areas` and the job `start_unreached_areas`).
6. **Spacing and reach are measured, not argued.** The spacing is 1 mm (a first build had 2.5 mm; on the owner's repro the strip is about 2 mm wide, and 2.5 mm gave single ticks across it instead of lines along it). The reach of 1.5 mm is a little over the 0.5 mm match tolerance and the strategy's sample spacing (`sqrt(area / points)` is 0.4 mm on the 512 repro); it is the one number to change if the owner wants the closing tighter or looser.

The surfaces an operation could not reach are hatched, with a numbered marker each in the warning's order, while its toolpath is shown and its result is current: black diagonal lines in a white casing, 1 mm apart, painted on the surface so they turn and foreshorten with it, fading to a flat tint when they would be a pixel apart. ## Alternatives considered

- **B, a flat translucent tint with an outline, and C, hatching with an outline** (the owner chose A). The outline of an area follows the edges of the marked triangles; before the closing it was a white zigzag, and it would still follow the tessellation after it.
- **A pattern in screen space with another shape** (stripes, a finer dot): it would still stay still while the part turns, which is the complaint.
- **Hatching in a surface parameterisation (X and the angle about the axis).** It would give lines at a fixed angle on a turned part, but it has a seam where the angle wraps, needs the axis and the part's placement in the shader, and does not apply to a surface that is not turned. Planes in the model frame need none of that.
- **Closing by a larger distance or by area growing over every neighbour.** It would hide reached surface next to a real area; the reach and the same-surface rule keep the region inside what was reported.

## Consequences

- `crates/openchuck-viewport`: `HatchKind::Surface` replaces `HatchKind::Dots`; `DOT_SPACING`, `DOT_CORE_RADIUS` and `DOT_RADIUS` are removed and `SURFACE_HATCH_SPACING_MM` is added; the shader's dot branch is replaced by the surface hatch; the uniform buffer is 176 bytes. Only `openchuck-viewport` has GPU code (architecture, crate rules).
- `crates/openchuck-app`: `unreached::{Adjacency, close_footprint, FOOTPRINT_REACH}`.
- `apps/openchuck-ui`: the roles `AREA_HATCH_CORE` and `AREA_HATCH_CASING`; the areas layer; the legend swatch.
- User guide: the captions and alt texts of the faceflow example (Figure 2), the selection sets example (Figure 6) and the rest machining example (Figure 2) say hatched, not dotted. **Figures to retake** at the release recapture (a separate docs lane): `user-docs/src/examples/images/blisk_faceflow/02-stepover.png`, `.../blisk_selection_sets/06-faceflow-result.png`, `.../rest/02-rest-after-finish.png`, and any other figure whose legend shows "Unreachable (...)". The hero images show no unreachable area.

## Verification

Run on 8 Nov 2026 on the branch `claude/viewhatch-work` (Linux container, Mesa lavapipe software Vulkan, not instrumented).

- **Pattern, test first** (`crates/openchuck-viewport/tests/render.rs`, three tests replace the dot test): `req_nfr_15_a_surface_hatch_is_spaced_in_millimetres_whatever_the_zoom` (the centres of the lines along a face are `sqrt(3)` mm apart within a pixel, at two zooms, and the part shows between them); `req_nfr_15_a_surface_hatch_stays_on_the_surface_when_the_camera_moves` (the same lines at the same world X from a camera panned 0.7 mm and from another zoom, within 0.2 and 0.25 mm, and the pixels differ, so it is not fixed to the screen); `req_nfr_15_a_surface_hatch_fades_to_a_flat_tint_at_a_distance` (a row of the face varies by over 120 levels near and by under 45 far, and the far face is still tinted). They failed before the shader change. The existing byte-count tests follow the 176-byte uniform. `cargo test -p openchuck-viewport`: all pass.
- **Colours** (`roles::tests::req_nfr_15_unreachable_hatch_reads_on_every_band_and_the_part`): the core reads 3:1 or more on every deviation band, the part's grey and the hidden slate in normal and simulated colour vision, and the line as a whole (the better tone) on a light and a dark view ground (worst case unchanged, 3.28:1 on the hidden-band slate). The application ships only the dark theme (the light theme is P2, UI-20), so no light screenshot exists: OI-2135.
- **Footprint, test first** (`crates/openchuck-app/tests/unreached.rs`): `req_op_20_a_marked_area_has_no_holes`, `req_op_20_a_closed_area_stays_within_reach_of_its_samples` (every added triangle within the reach; the middle of a ring of samples wider than twice the reach stays unmarked), `req_op_20_closing_a_footprint_never_marks_across_a_lip` (the dovetail: no lip outside, the floor whole), `req_nfr_07_closing_an_empty_footprint_is_nothing`. The existing mapping tests pass unchanged.
- **Measured on the real projects** (a temporary count, not kept): on the owner's 512 repro the one area went from 336 to 411 triangles (+22%) and its boundary against unmarked triangles of the same surface from 316 to 221 edges (-30%); on the baluster rest case the nine areas' boundaries went 0 to 0 (areas 1, 3 to 6, whole rings), 51 to 48, 63 to 43, 27 to 23 and 42 to 2, with up to 95% more triangles (the small areas 7 to 9). Frames after the change are on `claude/viewhatch-shots`.
- **Not verified:** the light theme; a Windows or macOS display (the captures are Linux lavapipe); the coverage check of the three crates, `cargo fmt`, `clippy` and the `xtask` checks, which are run before the code's pull request is opened and noted in it.
