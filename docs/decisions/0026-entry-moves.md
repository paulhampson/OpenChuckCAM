## Context

OP-11 (P1) lists "lead-in and lead-out, ramping, and retract strategy" among the common parameters; OP-14 asks for linking moves that stay clear of stock and part. Work breakdown slice 12 item 5 gives lead-in, lead-out and ramping; slice 14 item 4 gives "ramped entry"; no slice names helical entry. What exists today (decision 0018 item 9, `assemble::approach`): a rapid to the approach distance above the stock, then a straight plunge at the plunge feed to the first cut point of each pass. The IR already has the move kinds `Plunge`, `LeadIn` and `LeadOut`, and nothing produces the last two. Architecture sections 5.1 (IR) and 5.2 (strategy services). The IR is not frozen (OI-21), but this proposal does not change it.

Slices 12 and 14 can run in parallel once slice 10 lands. Without a shared home for entry moves each would write its own.

## Decision (accepted)

1. **Entry styles (OI-80 a).** An operation has one `entry` parameter with four styles: `plunge` (today's behaviour, the default), `lead` (a tangent arc into the cut and out of it, for finishing), `ramp` (a straight descent along the cut direction at a ramp angle) and `helix` (a descending circle). Helix is an entry style of its own, not a kind of ramp: it has different parameters (diameter and pitch against angle and length) and different room requirements. In the parameter form and in OP-11's wording, `ramp` and `helix` are both "ramping". The alternative reading, helix as a mode of one `ramp` style, saves one enum value and hides a parameter set that only applies to it.
2. **Which slice builds it (OI-80 b).** One module, built once, as a small preliminary change before slices 12 and 14 diverge: plunge moves into it with unchanged output (the golden files must not change), and the other three styles are added in it. Slice 12 item 5 then wires `lead` into the strategies; slice 14 item 4 wires `ramp` and `helix` into roughing. This keeps the two slices parallel. The alternative is to build `lead` in 12 and `ramp` and `helix` in 14 in the same module, which makes whichever starts second wait on the other's merge.
3. **Where entry lives (OI-80 c).** In `openchuck-path`, a module `entry` beside `assemble` and used by it, next to the linking code. A strategy does not write entry moves. For each pass it returns the cut and, optionally, an `EntryRequest` (style and parameters); `assemble` places the entry moves before the first cut point and the exit moves after the last, using the same A-axis management, clearance check, collision oracle and move timing as the cuts. OP-14's clearance rule, the feed rules (decision 0018 item 8) and the move kinds apply in one place. Each strategy's own tests cover which style it asks for; the entry module's tests cover the geometry.
4. **Move kinds and feeds, no IR change.** `plunge` writes `Plunge` as now. `lead` writes `LeadIn` before and `LeadOut` after the cut, at the cutting feed with the contact point on the surface (they are tangent to the cut, so the contact feed rule applies). `ramp` and `helix` write `LeadIn` at the plunge feed with no contact point (the tool is not on the finished surface, as for `Plunge`); the post's existing handling of non-contact fed moves covers them. The IR needs no field or kind.
5. **A and tool axis during entry.** A does not move during `ramp` and `helix`: the entry lies in the plane that contains the tool axis at the pass's start A. That keeps OP-15 continuity (no wrap or reversal can be introduced by an entry) and makes the feed on these moves a plain linear feed. `lead` follows the pass's A as the cut does.
6. **Parameters.** `entry`, `ramp_angle` (degrees), `helix_diameter` (as a fraction of the tool diameter) and `lead_radius` (mm), with a unit, range and default each (OP-16), and the plunge feed used for `ramp` and `helix` (no new tool data in TL-05). Starting values, accepted by the owner as typical of other tools, not verified on any machine or material (to be revisited when a machine test shows a need): ramp angle 3 degrees, helix diameter 75 % of the tool diameter, lead radius equal to the tool radius. All three are measured against the tool profile of the tool in use (a tapered or V-bit tool has a smaller effective diameter at depth than at the top; the entry geometry uses the profile's radius at the entry depth).
7. **Safety checks, and no silent fallback.** An entry is checked in the same call that builds it: against the clearance geometry (OP-14), the collision oracle, the stock model (`ray_entry`, so a ramp that would cut where the stock model says there is no material is reported as an air move, not an error) and the surface (the swept entry may not come closer to the target than the stock to leave less the tolerance). If the chosen style does not fit (a region narrower than the helix diameter, a ramp longer than the pass), the operation is refused with a message that names the style, the size needed and the room found. It does not change style on its own. The user can then pick another style or a smaller diameter. A `PathNote` records each entry the generator adjusted within its stated limits, such as shortening a ramp to fit.
8. **Tools that cannot plunge or ramp.** Whether a tool can cut straight down (a centre-cutting flag) is not in the tool data (TL-04). Until it is, `entry = plunge` is allowed for every tool, as today, and this is logged as OI-117. A drill's plunge is its normal cut (slice 18).

## As built

The preliminary change is in `crates/openchuck-path` (`src/assemble/entry.rs`, parameters in `params.rs`, tests in `tests/entry.rs`). Two points differ from the wording above, both smaller than the decision:

- **The style is an operation parameter, not a per-pass request.** Item 3 has a strategy return an optional `EntryRequest` for each pass. `Pass` is a public struct built by literal in hosts and tests, so a new field would break them. The operation's `entry`, `ramp_angle`, `helix_diameter` and `lead_radius` parameters (common to every strategy) choose the style for all its passes. A per-pass override is a compatible addition later (for example `Pass::entry`) if a strategy needs one; none does yet.
- **The helix circle uses the tool's cutting radius.** Item 6 measures against the profile at the entry depth. The circle is `helix_diameter` times the cutting diameter, the circle the tool centre follows (the hole it cuts is that plus the tool diameter). The finished-surface check uses the whole profile, so a tapered tool that is narrower at the tip is safe, only more conservative.

- **The entry parameters sit beside `CommonParameters`, not in it.** Adding a field to that public struct is a semver break that the `cargo-semver-checks` job caught (INT-07). `EntryParameters::read` reads them, `generate` passes them to the new `assemble_with_entry`, and `assemble` keeps its signature and plunges.

Other built details: `lead_radius` of 0 means the tool radius; the ramp runs back from the first cut point along the first horizontal move of the pass, and the helix leaves the circle tangent to it; a ramp, helix or lead that gouges the finished surface by more than the path tolerance is refused (the lead tries the left side, then the right, before refusing); a ramp whose start is inside the stock is refused; the ramp angle is limited to 89 degrees. The checks use the surface with the stock to leave and drop the tool at up to 400 positions of an entry. Containment to a selected region is not checked (OP-02 arrives with slice 10).

## Alternatives considered

- **Each strategy writes its own entry:** repeats the clearance, feed and move-kind logic per strategy (the reason OI-80 was raised), and the OP-14 checks would be tested once per strategy.
- **New IR move kinds (`Ramp`, `Helix`):** no gain: the post and the replay treat them as fed non-contact moves like `LeadIn`, and the IR is not frozen yet (OI-21), so avoid adding to it.
- **Entry handled in the post:** the post sees only the IR and has no stock or surface; it cannot check an entry.
- **Fall back automatically from helix to ramp to plunge:** it hides a change in how the tool enters the material from the user, and the tool or material may not suit the lower style. Refusing with a message costs the user one edit.
