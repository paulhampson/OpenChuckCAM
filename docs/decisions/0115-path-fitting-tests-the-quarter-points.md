## Context

Lane BM's simulation of the owner's blisk (`testdata/stl/blisk.stl`; Makera Z1, Ø42 x 52 mm cylinder stock from X -1, 0.1 mm cell) with a 3 mm ball of 12 mm flutes and 0.3 mm stepover under helical finishing reported overcut: worst 0.69 mm on 1,992 of 2,306,985 samples at a 5 degree angle step and 0.18 mm on 117 at 2 degrees (OI-331, decision [0108](0108-deviation-samples-follow-the-area.md)). The overcut shrank with the step, which pointed at the path rather than the comparison, but nobody had looked at where the overcut samples were. This lane was asked whether the overcut is real, which stage makes it, and why it exceeds the 0.01 mm toolpath tolerance (NFR-05; verification plan 4.1) that path fitting is meant to keep.

**Re-measured on `main` after decision 0113** (release build, `Application::simulate`, the same project): at 5 degrees the worst overcut is 0.177 mm on 1,063 samples; at 2 degrees no sample is overcut beyond the cell (worst 0.066 mm). The 1,063 samples all lie within 0.05 mm of the blade tips (radius 19.95 to 20.0 mm), on both flanks of the blades and on the tip faces, at X 10 to 46, with a median depth of 0.116 mm.

**The overcut is real** (the first question). An independent check against the mesh itself, written for this lane and separate from the simulator, replays every cutting move as the controller runs it (linear in machine X, Y, Z and A, eight poses inside each move, decision [0025](0025-slice-7-simulation.md) item 3) and measures how far the tool solid (the ball and the flutes above it) reaches inside the mesh, as the distance from points on the tool axis to the mesh against the tool radius. At 5 degrees the tool cuts up to **0.199 mm** into the mesh: 196 of 42,351 cutting moves cut more than the 0.01 mm tolerance and 20 more than the cell, at the places the simulation reports (for the worst, the simulation's 0.177 mm at X 45.76 against the check's 0.199 mm at X 46.50, within the ball's reach). At 2 degrees the tool cuts up to **0.049 mm**, in 93 moves, which the simulation's one-cell limit (decision 0025 item 6) hides. **At every dropped point the penetration is zero**, at both steps: the drop-cutter, the tie rules of decision 0113, the stock to leave and the entry are not involved, and neither are the simulation's sampling, its dexel stock or the deviation comparison, which read the gouge as it is. The gouges lie inside the moves, about a tenth of the way from one end or the other.

**The stage is the path fitter** (the second question), and the cause is its test. Decision 0018 item 12 accepts a move when the tool at the *middle* of the straight machine move is within `CHORD_FACTOR` (0.4) times the tolerance of the tool dropped there; nothing else along the move is tested. The worst move (A 55800.35 to 55805.35, X 46.50, one whole 5 degree seed interval, kept unsplit by the fitter itself before any assembly) shows what that misses. The ball rides up a blade's leaning flank (its normal 0.55 along X, 0.82 tangential and 0.16 radial, about 11 degrees from the tool axis) from a tip radius of 11.72 mm to the blade's tip edge at 19.45 mm. In the model frame the ball's centre follows the flank's straight offset, but the machine's straight move is linear in A and in the tool's height, a spiral, so the two cross: dropped along the move at 51 stations, the straight move is 0.25 mm *outside* the dropped tool a quarter of the way along (air), 1.06 mm *inside* it three quarters along (where the contact has reached the tip edge and the normal turns towards the tool axis), and 0.0078 mm from it at the middle, where the S-shaped curve of the dropped tool's height has its inflection. Along the contact normal (0.12 radial there) the middle reads 0.0010 mm, a quarter of the chord limit, and the move is kept. On an 11 degree flank, 1.06 mm along the tool axis is the 0.2 mm gouge. The same shape recurs at every blade on both flanks, wherever the seed phase puts a blade flank's inflection near a seed interval's middle, which is why the count and depth fall with the step and why lane TE's tool (2 mm stepover, another seed phase) showed none. Decision 0113's change of the measured normal and of the contacts moved the figures from 0.69 mm on 1,992 samples to 0.177 mm on 1,063 without touching the cause.

**A second weakness, found by the fix.** With the quarter points in place the groove reference job's golden program failed the golden audit (`crates/openchuck-app/tests/golden_audit.rs`, OP-13): a new 2.5 degree move beside a point the quarter test had added ran 36 % slower than programmed at the contact, which no axis limit explained. The cause is in the assembly, not the fitter. Decision 0018 item 13 measures the contact feed on contacts smoothed over two neighbours either side, and `assemble::smoothed_contacts` took the plain mean of the five contact *positions*. That mean lies where the window's points lie: between two seeds 5 degrees apart, one added point pulls its neighbours' smoothed contacts 1.5 degrees towards it, so the two short moves beside it carry a contact path 60 % longer than their tips travel and get an F word 37 % low, while the 5 degree moves either side get one 25 % high. Path fitting has always spaced points unevenly, but a single point added to an otherwise even run is exactly what the quarter test produces, so the weakness now shows on a smooth part the audit covers.

An exact surface shows the same blindness without the blisk's complications: a cylinder whose radius follows a cubic ramp over one seed interval, point-symmetric about the interval's middle with gentle ends (`crates/openchuck-path/tests/fit_interior.rs`, `CubicRamp`). The straight move across the ramp passes within the chord limit of the dropped tool at the middle, so the fitter before this record keeps the whole 72 degree move, which cuts 0.186 mm into the ramp a quarter of the way along by the independent checker on the exact surface (`openchuck_testkit::gouge::max_gouge`).

## Decision

1. **A move is accepted only when the straight machine move is within the chord limit at its middle and at the middles of its two halves** (the quarter points), each measured as decision 0113 item 4 measures the middle: the tool dropped at that station against the straight move's position there, along the tool's contact normal (`wrap_fit::Fit::refine`). Otherwise the move is halved and each half is tested the same way. The quarter points of a move are the middles of its halves, so their drops are made once and handed down: a move that is split costs no drop it did not cost before, and an accepted move costs two more. The seeds, the chord limit (`CHORD_FACTOR` 0.4, OI-08) and `MAX_DEPTH` (ten halvings, after which a move is kept whatever its error, the wall case of decision [0050](0050-walls-merge-micro-moves.md)) are unchanged.
2. **A quarter with no material under it is no reason to split** (as a middle with no material accepts the move: the surface falls away between two points that touch it), **unless a region constraint forbids cutting there**: then the move is halved so that the half finds the gap at its middle, as decision 0018 item 12 finds a gap at a middle, and cuts the pass short at the limits either side. Before, a forbidden stretch between a seed and a middle was found only when a later middle landed in it.
3. **Helical, axial and rest machining share the change** (they share `wrap_fit`). Simultaneous finishing has its own fitter with the same test at the middle alone (decision [0036](0036-slice-13-simultaneous-finishing.md) item 7, `simultaneous::Fitter::refine`); it is not changed here (its lane, and OI-337, own `simultaneous.rs`) and is recorded as OI-348.
4. **The golden files change**, as this lane says they should and lists below: every move the quarter points now split was outside the chord limit along its length, and a reference job's moves change only where that happened; its F words change everywhere by item 5 (they fall by about 0.73 %).
5. **The smoothed contact is the point's own tip plus the mean of its window's contact offsets** (`assemble::smoothed_contacts`, which now takes the `RotaryMapping` the pass is assembled with): the offset of each contact from its tip is taken in the machine's fixed frame, where the tool stands still and the part turns, so it says where on the tool the contact sits; the window's offsets are averaged there and added to the point's own tip. The jitter decision 0018 item 13 removes (the contact jumping from facet to facet on a mesh) is in that offset and is averaged away as before; the position no longer moves with the spacing of the window's points, so an added point leaves its neighbours' contacts where they are. On an even run the old mean lay inside the path by the window's sagitta (0.76 % of the radius at a 5 degree step), so the old contact lengths were short of the true contact travel by that much and every program ran about 0.76 % fast at the contact: the golden audit, which measures the contact speed on the exact surface, read a median error of 0.69 to 0.79 % on every reference job before this change and 0.00 to 0.15 % after it (the groove's worst, 1.90 %, is 1.58 %; the lobed job, whose moves are shorter, 0.17 % to 0.08 %). Every program's F words fall by about 0.73 % with it.

## What a sampled test still cannot see

The test is sampled: a move is accepted on three stations, so a deviation that is within the chord limit at its ends, its middle and its quarters but not between them is still missed. On a surface a ball of radius `R` can follow, the dropped tool's height along the move has no feature narrower than about `2R` (a narrower bump is widened by the ball and a narrower hollow is bridged), so with the default 5 degree step a feature would have to be narrower than an eighth of the seed step at the part's radius, 0.22 mm at a radius of 20 mm, which no standard tool of 1 mm or more can make; with the largest step the parameter allows (90 degrees) the limit is 3.9 mm at that radius. A wall (a jump in the dropped height) is caught as before: a move that straddles it has its middle or a quarter on one side of the jump and the straight move half way across it. The honest bound is the one the verification plan has always relied on: the gouge check of OP-12 is a test, and the simulation (SIM-05) remains the check on the whole program.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Measure the deviation at more stations** (eight, say, or every seed of the finer runs as `Fit::thin` does for a region's intervals). Each costs a drop, the dominant cost of path fitting; the quarter points cost two drops per accepted move and nothing per split, and catch the shape found here, whose scale is the whole move. Rejected as a default; the bound above says when more would be needed.
2. **Bound the error from the drop's curvature** instead of sampling (a Lipschitz bound on the dropped height from the tool radius and the surface's turning). Exact in principle, but the drop on a faceted mesh with walls has no useful bound at an edge, which is where the blisk's moves live. Rejected.
3. **Leave the fitter and sharpen the simulation's report** (the simulation did find the gouge, at 5 degrees). The gouge is the path's and the tolerance is the path's requirement (NFR-05, OP-12); at 2 degrees the simulation's one-cell limit hides a 0.049 mm gouge that is five times the tolerance. Rejected.
4. **Lower `MAX_DEPTH` or the chord factor.** Neither is the cause: the move that gouged was never split at all.
5. **For item 5, average the contact offsets in the model frame** (no new parameter). Tried first: the offset rotates with the part, so an uneven window still tilts the mean by the window's asymmetry (1.5 degrees beside an added point), which at a contact 1.5 mm from the tip (a ball's equator on a flank) shifts it 0.04 mm, 8 % of a 2.5 degree move at a radius of 10 mm, outside the 2 % of OP-13. In the machine's fixed frame the offset of a contact that stays put on the tool is constant, and the test `req_op_13_contact_smoothing_follows_each_tip_whatever_the_spacing` requires it exactly.
6. **For item 5, keep the mean of positions and avoid isolated points in the fitter.** The point is needed where it is put (the move was outside the chord limit there), and spacing two points to hide a measuring artefact is the wrong way round.

## Consequences

- Code: `crates/openchuck-path/src/wrap_fit.rs` (`Fit::refine` tests the quarter points, `refine_from` hands a known middle down), the module and `helical.rs` documentation; `crates/openchuck-path/src/assemble.rs` (`smoothed_contacts` takes the rotary mapping and averages offsets in its fixed frame). **Breaking:** `assemble::smoothed_contacts(rotary, points)`; a host passes the mapping it assembles with. Workspace version 0.10.0, with the entry in every library crate's changelog.
- Tests: `crates/openchuck-path/tests/fit_interior.rs`: `req_op_12_req_nfr_05_a_move_is_within_the_chord_between_its_points_not_only_at_its_middle` (the exact cubic ramp, the independent checker between the points of every move) and `req_op_12_req_sim_05_the_blisk_helical_pass_cuts_no_deeper_than_the_tolerance_between_its_points` (the blisk with lane BM's tool, against the mesh); both fail before this change (0.186 mm and 0.196 mm). `assemble::tests::req_op_13_contact_smoothing_follows_each_tip_whatever_the_spacing` (an added point between even neighbours leaves every smoothed contact on its own tool; fails with the old mean by 0.026 mm).
