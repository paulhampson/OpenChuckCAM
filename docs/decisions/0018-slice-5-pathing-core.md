## Decision

1. **Scope.** Everything the slice lists is built. What it leaves to later is named, not forgotten: the rest of OP-11 (item 7), the B-rep evaluators, face record and exact tool drop (slice 11 and OI-81), the region offsets' use by selections (slice 12) and the entry strategies (OI-80).
2. **Public API of `openchuck-path`.** Plain data from `openchuck-types` in, the neutral IR out (INT-01, INT-02); no parry or `cavalier_contours` type appears in it. Modules: `surface` (the `SurfaceSource` port, `ParametricFace`, `MeshSurface`), `cutter` (`ToolLine`, `Contact`, the drop-cutter, the grown tool), `rotary` (`RotaryMapping` and the A-axis functions), `stock` (`StockModel`, `AnalyticStock`, `CollisionOracle`, `AcceptAll`), `params` (schemas, values, the common parameters), `strategy` (`Strategy`, `Pass`, `CutPoint`, `StrategyInput`, `Registry`, `generate`), `assemble` (the shared services), `helical` (the first strategy), `unroll` (regions on the development and their offsets) and `parallel` (deterministic parallel maps). Errors are one `PathError` with user-worded messages; a cancelled job is `PathError::Cancelled`.
3. **`SurfaceSource` for meshes and for exact faces (slice item 1; decision 0017 item 3).** Model-frame queries: `bounds`, `project` (closest point and outward normal), `distance`, `cast_ray`, `tessellation` (the surface as triangles: all of it for a mesh, the display and fallback form for exact faces) and `drop_tool` (where a tool moved along a line first touches: tip and contact, decision 0012 item 4). Answers carry `position: Option<FacePosition>` (the face index and its (u, v)); a mesh has none. `drop_tool` is in the port because tool-centre solving is the question every strategy asks of the surface and a host with its own kernel may answer it exactly. The five queries of decision 0017 item 3 are these, per face, in the `ParametricFace` trait: `point_normal(uv)` (the orientation applied), `closest(point)`, `ray_hits(...)` (trimmed, nearest first), `contains(uv)` (the trim test) and the source's `tessellation()`; `uv_bounds()` gives the parameter box. A B-rep `SurfaceSource` holds its faces and answers `project` and `cast_ray` by the nearest point and first hit over them; slice 11 builds the face record in `openchuck-types` and the evaluators behind it. A test-only trimmed cylinder face and a face set prove the contract: queries agree with closed forms, the hole in a face is honoured by the trim test and by rays, and the face and (u, v) of an answer are reported. **Exact tool-centre solving on parametric faces is not settled by SP-9** and is OI-81; until then a face set falls back to dropping onto its tessellation, which decision 0017 measured at up to 0.011 mm off at its finest setting (over the 0.005 mm budget), so the fallback is a stopgap, not a result. `MeshSurface` keeps a copy of the mesh and the facet normals and uses a parry `TriMesh` only as the bounding volume hierarchy: candidate triangles come from `Bvh::leaves`, closest points from `Bvh::find_best`, rays from `Bvh::cast_ray`, and every number comes from our own triangle code (Ericson's closest point, Möller and Trumbore's ray test, evaluated with `+ - * / sqrt` only).
4. **Tool-centre solving (decision 0012 item 4).** The drop-cutter of SP-13, ported: vertex, facet and edge tests over the profile's lower envelope `h(r)` and its pieces, distances evaluated directly, golden-section search on convex pieces and sampling then refinement on concave arcs. It works for a tool line in any direction (the line's own orthonormal frame), so 4-axis poses need no rotated mesh. **Pruning** (the "bounding structure and height cull" of the slice's design refinement): the axis line is first cast against the surface, and the highest hit is a tip height the answer cannot be below (`h(0) = 0`, the tip rests on it); the bounding volume hierarchy is then walked with a test per node that keeps it only if its box can come within the cutting radius of the axis and its highest point less `h` at the box's nearest distance is not below that floor; the surviving triangles are visited from the highest such bound down, stopping when none can beat the best so far. The result is the same as without pruning (the same tests give the same numbers) and it is much faster: a helical test on a mesh of 700,000 triangles went from 90 s to 3.5 s. The climb of decision 0015 item 2 and the normal-offset fast path are not built: the continuous family arrives in slice 13.
5. **Rotary mapping and A-axis management (OP-15; proposes the answer to OI-06).** `RotaryMapping { axis, sense, range }` is taken from the machine profile, and maps model points to coordinates fixed to the machine with the rotary axis through the origin (`along`, `across`, `height`) through the shared kinematics, so the rotary axis stays a parameter; `tool_line(x, a, across)` gives the line a vertical tool moves along, and `a_facing(normal)` the A at which a normal's in-plane part faces the tool (decision 0015 item 3). The policy, for the owner's agreement:
   - Within a pass A is continuous: each angle is replaced by its equivalent nearest the one before, so no step turns more than half a turn.
   - Each pass is moved by whole turns to start as near the previous pass's end as the range allows. On a limited range a pass that spans more than the range is refused, naming the pass: the axis cannot unwind in the middle of a cut, and an unwind needs a retract.
   - Links take the shorter way round, because passes start at the nearest equivalent; reversals within a pass are the strategy's, and `reversals` counts them for tests and reports.
   - A group ends with an unwind to (-180, 180] at the safe Z: a rapid to the same model tip with A changed by whole turns, as decision 0001 item 1 describes. How it is written (a real rotation, or the Z1's `G92.4 A0 S0`, decision 0007 item 4) is the post's choice in slice 6.
6. **Strategies (OP-16).** A `Strategy` has a stable registry `name`, a `label`, a `schema` and `passes`: ordered `CutPoint`s (tip, A, contact) cut without lifting. `generate` resolves the operation's values against the schema, reads the common parameters, runs the strategy and hands its passes to the shared services, so a strategy never writes links, A unwinding or durations itself. A `ParameterSchema` lists each parameter's name, label, one-sentence description and kind: a number with unit, optional minimum (inclusive or exclusive), optional maximum and optional default (none means required); a whole number with range; a flag; or a choice from a list. `resolve` refuses an unknown name, a missing required value, a wrong kind, a non-finite number, a value out of range and a choice not in the list, each naming the parameter; a whole number is accepted where a number is expected (decision 0008 item 6). The registry refuses a second strategy of the same name and an empty name. `Registry::builtin()` holds helical finishing.
7. **Common parameters (OP-11 core; proposes defaults, OI-79, and the stepover meaning, OI-63).** Every schema starts with them:

   | Name | Meaning | Default |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | `tolerance` | toolpath tolerance, mm, greater than 0 | 0.01 (NFR-05) |
   | `stepover` | distance between neighbouring passes, mm, greater than 0 | none: from the tool's cutting data |
   | `stock_to_leave` | material left on the surface, mm, at least 0 | 0 |
   | `feed` | contact feed while cutting, mm/min | none: from the cutting data (speed x flutes x feed per tooth) |
   | `plunge_feed` | feed along the tool axis into material, mm/min | none: from the cutting data |
   | `retract` | links at the safe cylinder (`clearance`) or at the safe Z (`safe_z`) | `clearance` |
   | `approach_distance` | a rapid approach stops this far above the stock, mm | 1.0 (proposed) |

   The application fills `feed`, `plunge_feed` and `stepover` from the tool snapshot's cutting data for the setup's stock material when the operation leaves them out (TL-05). The feed is the material's cutting feed (spindle speed x flutes x feed per tooth), so two materials on one tool give two feeds, and the IR's durations follow from it. Three rules keep the material from being bypassed unseen (agreed by the owner, 12 Oct 2026):
   - **An operation's own value wins**, and when it differs from the material's, regeneration says so in `Regeneration::notes`, naming the value, the tool and the material ("feed 250 set on the operation overrides 400 from the cutting data of '4 mm ball' for 'oak'"). A value equal to the material's says nothing.
   - **A required value with no source is an error naming the cause**: the operation, the missing values, and whether the setup has no stock material, the tool has no cutting data for the named material (names match exactly, OI-61), or the operation has no tool. It does not fall back to another material's data.
   - **The feed is one number per material and tool.** Nothing adjusts it per move for depth of cut or engagement (that belongs to slice 14's engagement work and SIM-10); the only change to a programmed feed is the machine's feed limits (item 8), which lengthen a move and add a note.

   **Stepover (OI-63, proposed):** a length measured on the part's surface between neighbouring passes; each strategy's description says how: for helical finishing the axial advance per turn (the pitch), for axial and indexed finishing the arc length at the selection's largest radius (so it is never exceeded).

   **The rest of OP-11 is not in this step.** OP-11 also asks for stepdown, lead-in and lead-out, ramping, stock to leave per selection and a retract strategy; the work breakdown places them in later slices, and nothing here builds them:

   | OP-11 parameter | Slice (work breakdown) | State |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Tolerance, stepover, feeds | 5 | Built (table above) |
   | Retract strategy | 5 (core), 14 (completes) | The two choices above only: safe cylinder or safe Z |
   | Stock to leave, per operation | 5 | Built (item 13) |
   | Stock to leave, per selection | 12 (item 4) | Not built; needs selections (OP-02, slice 14) |
   | Lead-in and lead-out, ramping | 12 (item 5) | Not built. The IR has `LeadIn` and `LeadOut` move kinds; nothing produces them |
   | Stepdown, ramped entry | 14 (item 4, rotary roughing) | Not built. Tools carry a default stepdown (TL-05), which no operation reads yet |
   | **Helical entry** | **Not named in any slice** | **Not built; OI-80** |

   What the shared services do today is a straight plunge at the plunge feed, from the approach distance above the stock to the first cut point of each pass (item 9). That is acceptable for a finishing pass on a ball or flat tool at low depth, and it is not an entry strategy: it does not ramp, and a roughing pass or a tool that cannot plunge needs one. OP-11 closes in slice 14 (`xtask/traceability.toml`), so it is not a failing P1 requirement now.
8. **Contact feed and duration (OP-13).** For a cutting move with contact feed `f` (mm/min) whose contact moves from `c0` to `c1`, `duration = 60 |c1 - c0| / f` seconds and `contact_length = |c1 - c0|`; the post derives the machine feed from the move and its duration (compensated per minute, or inverse time). No move is shorter than the slowest axis allows at the machine's feed limits (rapid limits for rapids); where that lengthens a cutting move the contact feed there is below the programmed one, and a `PathNote` says so with the real value. Moves without a contact (rapids, plunges, exits) measure the tip. **Contact smoothing:** the contact positions of a pass are averaged over five points (two either side, a centred window shrinking at the ends) before the lengths are taken, because a drop onto a mesh places the contact by facet orientation (decision 0015: "about radius times the facet angle"). Measured by comparing the IR's contact length per move with the contact path on the exact surface, followed through the machine's linear XYZA motion:

   | Case | Result |
   | --- | --- |
   | Ring passes on a helical groove, ball off the axis, 0.05 mm x 0.5 degree facets | raw contacts up to 2.45 % off; smoothed: median 0.24 %, 95th 1.09 %, worst 2.01 % |
   | The same at 0.025 mm x 0.25 degrees | smoothed: median 0.21 %, 95th 0.75 %, worst 0.98 % to 1.62 % |
   | Helical finishing of the groove (1 degree steps, 0.025 mm x 0.25 degree facets), away from the part's ends | median 0.16 %, 95th 0.67 %, worst 1.03 % |
   | The same within 4 mm of an end of a 14 mm part | worst 2.06 % |

   The mesh's own error is below 0.0002 mm in every row, so the chord tolerance alone does not bound the feed error: the facet size does, and so do the ends. Near an end the ball rolls over the rim, whose contact is not smooth, and within two moves of the ends of a pass the smoothing window is shorter; the ±2 % limit (verification plan 4.1) is met away from both, which is what the test judges. The rule for the facet size the feed needs, and what to do at rims and pass ends, is OI-77, set with the tessellation measurement (OI-65) and smoothed normals (OI-67).
9. **Linking (OP-14) and the ports.** In the fixed coordinates of item 5 every link moves in the plane at the retract height above the rotary axis (the safe cylinder's radius, or the safe Z); every point of that plane is at least that far from the axis, so a link at any A and any position across the axis stays outside the safe cylinder, and the group is refused when the stock reaches beyond the retract height ("enlarge the setup's clearance"). A pass is left along the tool axis at the plunge feed until the tip is the approach distance above the stock on that line (the stock model may not know what this operation cut), then the tool rapids up to the retract height, across and round to above the next pass, rapids down to the approach distance above the stock or the first point, whichever is higher, and plunges (a straight plunge is the only entry; item 7 lists lead-in, ramping and helical entry as not built). The group starts and ends at the safe Z. Every move is put to the `CollisionOracle`; a refused move is an error, since P1 has no way round. `StockModel` answers `contains`, `ray_entry` and `max_radius`; `AnalyticStock` covers cylinder and box stock and bounding-box stock once the part's bounds are known, and refuses imported stock (SET-04, P2). `AcceptAll` is the default oracle (verification plan section 5).
10. **The application (slice items 8 and 10).** `openchuck-app::operation` maps an operation to the library's plain data: the setup's part (imported and placed as the model inspection does), stock, clearance (the setup's or the machine's), the machine's rotary mapping and feed limits, the tool snapshot's profile, the parameter values, and tags holding the operation, setup and tool IDs. The library never sees the project. An operation that cannot be generated is refused with a message naming it and the missing piece (machine, setup, part, stock, tool, an unknown strategy); an operation limited to selections is refused until OP-02 (slice 14) rather than machined whole. Regeneration runs every operation in machining order and stops at the first failure; `Regeneration` gains `notes`, which the CLI prints as warnings. `Application` holds a strategy registry (`Registry::builtin()`), and `register_strategy` lets a host add its own. The `Operation` type of slice 1 already holds strategy, tool, setup, selections and parameters; no project schema change.
11. **Independent gouge and coverage checker (OP-12; architecture section 9).** In `openchuck-testkit::gouge`, ported from SP-13 and extended to any tool axis: it shares only the profile's segment data, samples the exact surface of an analytic part (never the mesh), classifies each sample against the tool's closed revolved outline by a crossing count and measures depth as the distance to the outline. `coverage` checks that every exact surface sample in a range comes within a limit of some pose's tool surface (the poses must be dense enough that the tool between two of them cuts nothing they do not, and lie near the surface); `min_clearance` gives the signed clearance of one pose, which is the stock to leave where there is stock. The oracle and corpus of the 2D offsets are in `openchuck-testkit::region` (item 14).
12. **Helical finishing (OP-04; slice item 5; proposes the answer to OI-08).** A vertical tool over the rotary axis follows a helix about it: A turns once every `stepover` mm of X (the pitch), from the lowest X of the part to the highest, so the passes are `stepover` apart along the axis. In the development of the cylinder (x, s = R A) the helix is a straight line, so this strategy needs no offset of its own: the region offsets of item 14 serve selections, containment and curve following in slices 12 and 16 (the slice's "using the offset approach proven in SP-7" is met by item 14 being in place and tested, and by the strategy working in the development). Parameters beyond the common ones: `rotation` (`positive` or `negative`: which way A turns as X increases), `start_angle` (A at the lowest X, default 0) and `max_angle_step` (the most A may turn between points, default 5 degrees, at most 90). At each point the tool is dropped onto the surface (item 4), so the cutter rides the surface whatever its shape. The whole model is machined: the part's end faces are not (a helix about the axis cannot reach them), and where the tool is over no material the pass is split and the shared services link the pieces. **Path fitting** is by bisection: the points are at most `max_angle_step` apart, and a move is split until the tool at its middle on the straight machine path (X, Y, Z and A move linearly between points) is within `CHORD_FACTOR` times the tolerance of where the tool would be if dropped there, **measured along the surface normal** (the slice's design refinement). **`CHORD_FACTOR` is 0.4 (closing OI-08, proposed).** Measured on a sphere of radius 20 with a 6 mm ball, pitch 8 mm and 60 degree steps (long moves, the sphere's curvature along X needs points), against the exact distance of the ball centre from the sphere centre:

   | Tolerance | Cutting moves | Worst fit error | Chord limit (0.4 x tolerance) |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | 0.04 mm | 38 | 0.01546 mm | 0.016 mm |
   | 0.01 mm (default) | 76 | 0.00405 mm | 0.004 mm |
   | 0.0025 mm | 136 | 0.00145 mm | 0.001 mm |

   The fit error is within 1.01 times the chord limit (the rest is the mesh's own 0.0002 mm), so a factor of 0.5 would sit on the 0.005 mm path-fitting budget of verification plan 4.1 with no room for the mesh, which SP-3 saw as 0.0056 mm; 0.4 leaves about 0.001 mm. With the default 5 degree steps at pitch 1 mm the points are close enough that fitting adds none and the error is the mesh's, 0.0003 mm. Registered in `Registry::builtin`.
13. **Stock to leave (OP-11).** The tool is dropped with a profile grown by the stock (`cutter::grow_profile`: the Minkowski sum of the revolved tool and a ball of that radius, in closed form: each piece offset along its outward normal, with an arc about each convex corner), so the surface is left `stock_to_leave` mm proud everywhere whatever the tool's shape, and the cutter contact is taken on the original tool (`cutter::cutter_contact`: the profile point nearest the surface point), which lies on the left-over surface; the original tool's tip is `stock_to_leave` above the grown tool's tip along the axis. Only convex profiles are grown (every standard tool is); a concave custom profile with stock to leave above zero is refused with a message saying so rather than approximated, and works with none.
14. **2D offsets on the unrolled development (SP-7; decision 0016 items 1 to 7).** The `unroll` module holds regions of lines and arcs in the plane (x, s = R A) and keeps the rules SP-7 found, so no strategy has to know them. `cavalier_contours =0.9.0` does the work and no type of it is public.
   - **Direct offsets (rule 1).** `ValidRegion::offset(d)` always offsets the original region; the API has no way to offset a previous result, and `passes(step, max)` offsets the original by `k` steps, in parallel in fixed chunks of 64 and in order.
   - **Refusal of touching input (rule 2, OI-78 proposes the gap).** `Region::validate` refuses fewer than two vertices, a value that is not finite, a loop with no area, a loop that crosses itself, a hole outside the boundary or inside another hole, and any two loops that cross or come closer than 2e-5 mm (twice the library's position epsilon; the gap is measured between the loops flattened at 1e-6 mm, which is small against it). The message names the loops and the gap. Union through `i_overlay` instead of refusing is the alternative decision 0016 left open; it is not taken.
   - **Simplification and a result check (rule 5, OI-78 proposes the values; the limit is OI-82).** Straight runs are thinned by Douglas-Peucker to 1e-3 mm (arcs are untouched); a 20,000-vertex polygon on a circle of 20 mm keeps 500. Every result is checked: each vertex and arc midpoint must be at the offset distance from the region that was offset, within 1e-3 mm, found through a spatial index; a result that fails is an error ("not trusted") naming the point. `check_offset` is public, and is proved by a shifted loop, a wrong distance and a NaN, each refused. A panic inside the library is caught and becomes an error (NFR-07). **What this does not do** (measured afterwards, in a release build, against the independent oracle, on a noisy circle of radius 40 mm offset by 1 mm):

     | Loop | Result | Judged against the oracle |
     | --- | --- | --- |
     | 500 and 2,000 vertices (0.5 to 0.1 mm apart; noise 0.2 or 0.01 mm) | 1 loop | right (0 wrong points of 1,500) |
     | 5,000 vertices, noise 0.2 mm (50 um apart) | 1 loop and a stray zero-area hole | points right, but the stray loop is a chord 3.2 mm off the curve |
     | 20,000 vertices, noise 0.01 mm (12.5 um apart) | 32 outer and 64 hole loops | 1,013 of 1,500 points wrong |
     | 20,000 vertices, noise 0.05 mm | 11 outer and 18 hole loops | 736 of 1,500 wrong |
     | 50,000 vertices, noise 0.2 mm (5 um apart; the spike's case) | 8 outer and 20 hole loops | 749 of 1,500 wrong |

     The result check **passes** all the wrong rows, because every vertex lies on a true piece of the offset curve; the library stitches the pieces wrongly, and a vertex test cannot see that. The test for the 50,000-vertex loop only proves that nothing crashes: in a debug build the library trips an internal `debug_assert` that the panic catch turns into an error, which is why the test is green, and in a release build there is no error at all. Inputs like these are not mesh sections: a section through a mesh tessellated to 0.005 mm has vertices about a millimetre apart on a 40 mm radius and no noise, which is the 500-vertex row. They could come from scanned or reconstructed surfaces, and nothing builds regions from meshes before slice 12.
   - **Chording (rule 6).** `chord_points` cuts every arc of a loop into chords of at most the chord tolerance and returns (X, A) points, A unwrapped; lines stay single moves. G2 and G3 stay out of it (OI-68, slice 6).
   - **Tiling at the seam (rule 7, OI-79 proposes how loops come back).** `PeriodicBand` offsets islands in a band all the way round the cylinder as three tiles; the loops come back with their lowest `s` in the middle period and one that straddles the seam continues past it, because the IR keeps A unwrapped: nothing is cut at the seam. Islands that grow into one ring round the cylinder are refused with a message.
   - **Dependency (NFR-14).** `cavalier_contours` is declared in `openchuck-path` only. **`i_overlay` is not declared yet:** decision 0016 item 8 has slice 5 declare both, but nothing in slice 5 does a region boolean (they serve containment and avoid surfaces, OP-02, slice 12), and a dependency nothing calls costs build time and review for no gain; it stays recorded in `docs/dependencies.md` and is declared with its first use. This is a deviation from that item, raised for the owner.
   - **The oracle and the corpus** of the spike (exact distance to a boundary of lines and arcs, membership, 23 hand-made cases and a seeded random generator) are in `openchuck-testkit::region`, written without the offset library.
15. **Determinism and platforms (NFR-06, GC-07).** Strategies parallelise through `parallel::map_in_order`: fixed chunks of 64 on the caller's rayon pool, results in input order; the library spawns no threads. The assembly is sequential. Within one platform the output is identical at 1, 2 and N threads (tested for the rings stub, helical finishing and the offset passes). Across platforms our own maths uses only correctly rounded operations, apart from the shared kinematics' `sin_cos` and `atan2`; parry contributes only which triangles are visited, not any number in the output. A golden file of a helical toolpath (`testdata/golden/helical_cone.txt`, every move at six decimals) must match on all three CI platforms; this is the first cross-platform check (verification plan policy 7), and a mismatch there is the investigation OI-64 and OI-75 ask for. Bit identity itself is still not tested.
16. **Test build profile.** The pathing tests drop tools on meshes of up to a million triangles; unoptimised they take minutes. `openchuck-path`, `openchuck-testkit`, `parry3d-f64`, `glamx`, `cavalier_contours` and `static_aabb2d_index` build at `opt-level = 3` in the dev profile, so the path test suite runs in about a minute in a debug build, parallel across its files. Release builds are unchanged.
17. **Example host (INT-01, INT-08; OI-30).** `crates/openchuck-path/examples/host.rs` builds a mesh, registers its own strategy and generates a toolpath through the public API alone; CI runs it with the other examples.

## Alternatives considered

- **`drop_tool` only on `MeshSurface`, not in the port.** Strategies would then need to know the surface is a mesh; a host with an exact kernel could not serve them. Kept in the port, with the architecture wording proposed below.
- **A `SurfaceSource` over the whole B-rep with one `evaluate(face, uv)`.** The per-face trait keeps the parametric queries where decision 0017 puts them (per face) and lets a face set choose its own spatial index; the source answers the whole-surface questions.
- **parry's own triangle queries** (`TriMesh::project_local_point`, `cast_local_ray`). They return the same answers, but our own tests keep every output number in code we control, which matters for OI-64 and decision 0012's precision findings.
- **A per-strategy linking and A-axis policy.** Every strategy would repeat it, and OP-14 and OP-15 would be tested once per strategy. Shared services, as architecture section 5.2 names them.
- **Raw mesh contacts for the contact length.** Measured above: up to 2.45 % off on a mesh whose error is below 0.0002 mm.
- **Rapid retracts straight from the cut.** The analytic stock model does not know what the operation has cut, so a rapid could pass through uncut stock; a fed exit to the approach distance above the stock costs a little time and is safe.
- **Unwinding at every link on a continuous axis.** It adds time and reversals for no gain; the Z1 drops whole turns without motion (`G92.4`), so unwinding at the end of a group is enough.
- **Helical finishing through region offsets.** A helix is a straight line in the development; offsetting a rectangle to get one pass per turn would be slower and needs a seam rule for nothing. The offsets are for regions with boundaries (slice 12).
- **Stock to leave by moving the tip along the normal afterwards.** Exact only for a ball and wrong for every other tool; growing the profile is right for all convex tools.
- **Declaring `i_overlay` now.** See item 14.
