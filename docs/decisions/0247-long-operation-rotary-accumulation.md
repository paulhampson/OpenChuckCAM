## Existing decisions and implementation

Accepted 0007 and 0013 establish a continuous Z1 A axis, signed whole-turn counter reset and machine-specific post conventions. Accepted 0018 item 5 fits complete passes by whole turns into limited ranges and refuses a pass wider than the range, without unwinding mid-cut (closed OI-06/OI-79). Its canonical end unwind is superseded by accepted 0241. Accepted 0033/0036 share rotary assembly for roughing and simultaneous finishing. The unwrapped IR rule in 0001 preserves commanded rotary travel.

ROUGHPATH #340 is merged into this base. Accepted 0241 keeps the final cutting A through safe retract, connects operations with a constant nearest-equivalent whole-turn placement, preserves machining order, and distinguishes physical motion from nonmoving post resets. It forbids reducing cutting A modulo a turn. Its final paragraph explicitly leaves accumulation within a very long cut unresolved. Accepted 0242 adds optional fixed-A reversing ramp entry; it is not a rotary accumulation remedy. This accepted record extends 0241 item 6 from group boundaries to explicit safe helical-pass boundaries; it preserves the distinction between physical motion and controller bookkeeping. It does not change 0242. Accepted 0222 separates reporting from feed-changing mitigation for short blocks; block-rate limits are a different problem from accumulated coordinates. Open OI-41 (tool change/footer evidence) and OI-60 (rotary-origin evidence) remain relevant limitations, not settled here.

`rotary::fit_range` fits each complete pass into `ARange::Limited { min, max }`, or places it near the preceding A for Continuous. `assemble` preserves within-pass travel; `program::connect_group` checks whole-group placement. Large values therefore reflect intentional turns in helical strategies carried by shared assembly, rather than an unexplained accumulation bug elsewhere.

Architecture sections 3–5 place motion planning/clearance in `openchuck-path`, plain machine data in `openchuck-types`, and controller conventions and reporting in `openchuck-post`. These reusable libraries cannot depend on app/project/UI/kernel/simulator; application wiring owns persistent project settings. A physical rewind must be timed IR seen by simulation/playback, never hidden in the post.

## Machine facts and limits of the evidence

The built-in Z1 and reference project copies use Continuous, with no numeric A minimum/maximum. Their provenance cites owner use, the firmware's absence of an A soft limit, and the attended 1.1.2 air cut (0007): A570 and A-370 worked, and `G92.4 A0 S0` removed whole turns without motion. This establishes those cases, not arbitrary numeric magnitude. The 3600 deg/min profile A rate is a velocity limit, not a position limit. FreeCAD's ±1e7 profile value is not evidence of a Z1 travel limit.

Makera `unwind = "drop_turns"` currently emits its nonmoving command at eligible safe-Z group boundaries. Rendering tracks the resulting offset in subsequent absolute words, keeps signed remainders, and leaves exact ±360 unchanged. It cannot simply emit every absolute A modulo 360: a 359→361 move would become 359→1 and command a reverse turn. There is no arbitrary per-block modulo option. A new reset inside a group needs its own explicit controller contract.

LinuxCNC `unwind = "rotate"` emits the unwrapped A words and drops bookkeeping boundaries. It no longer appends a canonical physical unwind. `tools/linuxcnc/checks.ini` has axis A AND angular joint 3 limits ±1e9 degrees, without WRAPPED_ROTARY. These are a finite verification envelope, not Z1 limits. A program outside it can violate that simulator configuration. No measured case below approaches it.

**Unconfirmed:** a Z1 numeric A-word ceiling, maximum usable accumulated angle, and precision loss versus magnitude on the owner's controller. The air-cut DRO discrepancy of 0.001 degrees in 0007 is not a measured large-angle precision bound. Reduced numeric resolution at large magnitude is a plausible controller-representation concern, not a demonstrated failure or a reason to invent a Z1 threshold. No new hardware trial was performed.

Totals in the following summary use a nominal A0 file-start reference; raw evidence separately excludes initial positioning from an unknown actual start. Fresh measurements include first-cut roughing at A135…82080 degrees (82400 degrees total travel) and finishing at A−168.397…20325.352 (20662.146 total); blisk roughing at A125…75495 (80975 total) and helical finishing at A0…9000 (9000 total). Cone finishing at 2/1/0.5 mm pitch measures 5400/10800/21600 degrees. No measured job reaches the known LinuxCNC verification envelope.

See [raw evidence](0247-rotary-accumulation-evidence.md) and its JSON companion for commands, source, settings, timings, output hashes, refusals and measurements. All runs use the measurement-baseline uninstrumented CLI, one worker, temporary project copies and shipped posts. Metrics distinguish physical unwrapped motion from controller counter words; a counter reset is never counted as a physical reverse move. Per-operation files are assembled independently; table extrema are actual commanded A values. Travel from nominal A0 and travel between commanded positions are both given; actual unknown machine-start travel is not inferred.

A helix advances X by pitch p each turn: a single uninterrupted length L contributes approximately 360 L/p degrees; more layers add their spans. Surface curvature and tolerance affect block count and runtime rather than that turn count. Splits, pass fitting and links can alter extrema, so measured values take precedence over this estimate. Cutting duration is feed/geometry dependent; wall generation time is not operation duration.

## Revised proposal: reset between helical passes

The owner directly rejected reporting as the primary remedy: the Z1 maximum A is unavailable to them and they are concerned about numeric precision. They then specified: "For strategies doing helical passes it would make sense to reset when doing the next pass." The recommendation is therefore **a supported nonmoving controller counter reset between helical passes**, rather than a user report or a user-supplied threshold. The owner further explicitly instructed: "Z1 has no need for physical rewind. Ensure you don’t add unnecessary rewinds." **This Z1 policy adds no physical rewind or other motion.**

Accepted behavior:

1. **A pass means a complete cutting traversal, not each revolution or G-code block.** A roughing layer can contain a helix with many turns. Finish that pass with its continuous cutting A unchanged, leave the material and reach proved clearance, then reset before positioning/entering the next helical pass. Use existing safe-retract transitions where they provide the required clearance; add no retract, re-entry or other motion for this Z1 reset policy. If a desired boundary lacks the reset contract's required clearance, return that case for a policy decision rather than silently inserting new motion or resetting in-cut.
2. **Z1 uses its existing whole-turn counter-reset semantics.** At an eligible boundary use `G92.4 A0 S0` when it would remove whole turns; this preserves orientation and need not make the readout exactly zero. Preserve signed remainders and firmware's exact ±360 behavior. Apply the resulting coordinate offset to every subsequent absolute A command, including links and later operations, and carry it through independent replay. The counter reset adds no physical rotary travel or rotary-motion duration and must not animate a turn. Never emit a compensating backward rotation before or after the Z1 reset, including at the final operation/program boundary.
3. **Keep physical geometry and planning unchanged.** Preserve cutting direction, pass order, feed intent, held-region/tab protection and within-pass unwrapped IR. Mark the eligible boundary explicitly without treating any arbitrary retract or missing-material split as authorization for a reset. This extends 0241 item 6's reset locations; its physical links, operation boundaries and file-splitting rules still apply. Each independently exported file starts with its own counter-offset context.
4. **Limited A remains a separate problem.** Do not reset its coordinates to conceal a physical travel limit. Retain complete-pass range fitting/refusal from 0018 and 0241. A future separately accepted limited-axis physical rewind, if necessary and feasible, would have to remain within min/max throughout and be represented as timed, fully clearance-checked motion. That is not part of this build proposal and must never be applied to the Z1. A single helix wider than the physical range still refuses. New automatic splitting or rewind planning is not selected by the owner's counter-reset preference.
5. **Unsupported posts retain their own semantics.** Do not turn LinuxCNC verification's rotate-only mode into a physical full-turn return to imitate a Z1 reset. The verification post may retain unwrapped coordinates; independent Z1 replay must check its counter-reset semantics and the unchanged physical path. A different controller needs an explicit supported reset contract before it receives such a command.
6. **State the remaining limitation precisely.** Resetting between passes prevents completed passes' whole turns from accumulating into the next pass's controller coordinates. It does not bound accumulation inside one uninterrupted helix, and a one-pass finishing operation gains no intermediate reset. The measured 9000-degree blisk finishing helix remains a 9000-degree helix. No Z1 maximum or precision guarantee is invented. Do not add in-cut resets, thresholds or automatic pass splitting under this record without a further explicit choice.
7. **Compatibility and settings.** Recommend automatic behavior, using the existing post reset capability, with no new toggle or user-configured threshold. The proposed automatic policy uses the post's existing reset capability at eligible helical boundaries, with no new user reporting screen or numeric limit to configure. It intentionally changes affected Z1 G-code by adding reset commands and rebasing subsequent A words, while preserving physical cutting motion. The owner’s full-record acceptance includes this affected-legacy-output change; automatic behavior is selected. Jobs with no eligible reset must retain same-version byte identity. No new persisted field is currently proposed, so no schema is claimed; if opt-in persistence is selected, claim the next free schemas before using it. Library API/version choices remain subject to 0048 and Merge Manager release order.

## Recommendation and owner disposition

1. **Recommended path:** automatic Z1 counter resets at established safe transitions between complete helical passes. Use the existing nonmoving reset capability, with no new toggle, numeric threshold or persisted setting. Preserve every physical move, feed intent and cutting direction; add no physical rewind, retract or re-entry. Accept the necessary output changes (reset commands and consistently rebased subsequent A words) for affected jobs; keep unaffected jobs byte-identical at the same version. An opt-in setting would add configuration and persistence work without improving the chosen physical behavior, so it is not recommended. The owner accepted this automatic policy directly; Phase 2 may proceed subject to Merge Manager version/release order.
2. **Single uninterrupted long pass:** deferred by the owner directly in this session. No in-cut reset, automatic subdivision, added retract or precision ceiling is included. Revisit only when the owner requests that separate work or new controller evidence motivates it.
3. **Limited-axis physical rewind/splitting:** explicitly outside this task, as instructed directly by the owner. Existing complete-pass fitting and refusal remain unchanged; no new limited-axis feature or Z1 physical unwind is proposed.

OI-994 records remaining complete-record acceptance/compatibility; OI-995 records the owner-deferred single-pass case; OI-996 records review of the precise eligible boundary contract. OI-997 is closed as outside this task, not as implemented. These items do not require the owner to discover an unknown Z1 maximum. Before implementation, agree any additional requirement wording; do not edit requirements or architecture to make implementation fit.

Requirement-first verification for a later accepted build must include a multilayer long operation that accumulates across passes today; positive/negative counters and exact ±360 boundaries; subsequent positioning and cutting after each reset; no reset in-cut; canonical/cached paths and independent file splitting; unchanged physical paths/feeds and zero additional Z1 rotary travel (including links and program end); same-version identity for unaffected jobs; deterministic output at 1/2/N workers; and independent replay of the Z1 reset state. Regression verification must preserve existing limited-axis fitting/refusal; implementing limited-axis rewinds is outside scope. No new physical motion is authorized by this proposal. A pass-reset test must not claim to prove an unknown Z1 numeric ceiling or solve the deferred single-pass case. No implementation or new tests are added by this proposal revision.

Generated G-code remains the user's to verify before running on a machine (NFR-12). This proposal establishes no real-machine safety limit.
