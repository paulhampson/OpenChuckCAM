# 0047: Slice 16 design refinement: edge and curve following on mesh feature edges (lane K)

- **Status:** proposed. Built on `claude/lane-k-slice-16-mesh-edges`. Nothing in it changes a requirement, the architecture or a file format; the owner accepts it. Its open items (OI-204 to OI-208) are new.
- **Date:** 16 Oct 2026.
- **Open items** are tracked in [docs/open-items.md](../open-items.md): OI-204 to OI-208 are new.

This is lane K of "Parallel work" (slice 16 item 1 for mesh feature edges, and items 2 to 5 as far as they do not need a stored selection or a B-rep edge). It claims record **0047** and **OI-204 to OI-208**, assigned by the orchestrator, and **no project schema number**: strategy settings are operation parameters ([decision 0008](0008-slice-1-project-model.md) item 6) and the strategy reads the part's own feature edges. Storing a chosen set of edge chains would need a new selection kind and a schema bump, so it is not done here (OI-204). **Owner's decision (16 Oct 2026, relayed by the orchestrator):** lane K takes no schema number; lane P and the viewport edge-picking work own the new selection kind and its schema bump.

## Context

Slice 16 (work breakdown; OP-08, P1): engrave or carve along selected edge chains or curves projected onto the surface. Its design refinement asks for the `CurveSource` trait, the projection method, and how A and the tool axis behave along a curve. Architecture section 5.2 puts edge and curve following in the wrap and unroll family. Lane C (mesh feature edges and selection, [0032](0032-slice-10-mesh-selection.md), [0037](0037-slice-10-picking-and-selection-panel.md)) is merged: `openchuck_geom::display::feature_edges` finds the edges the viewport draws. Lane P (the same strategy on B-rep edges) comes later and builds on this one, so the edge source sits behind a trait. How strategies, entry moves and tests are structured follows [0034](0034-slice-12-baseline-finishing.md), [0036](0036-slice-13-simultaneous-finishing.md) and [0044](0044-slice-15-rest-and-pencil.md).

## Decision

1. **The seam is `CurveSource` (design question, proposed).** A trait in `openchuck_path::curves`: `chains(&self, surface) -> Result<Vec<EdgeChain>>` in a deterministic order, and a provided `lies_on_surface()` (default true). An `EdgeChain` is a polyline in the model frame, open or closed (a closed one repeats its first point last), the shape the strategy needs and nothing about where it came from. `MeshFeatureEdges { crease_degrees, min_length }` is the mesh source; lane P writes a B-rep source (its edges' tessellation, from the `BrepSurface`) and a later importer a drawing source (IMP-08, SUG-06) without touching the strategy. `follow_chains(input, chains, on_surface, parameters, context)` is public for a host or a wrapping strategy that already has chains. `EdgeFollowing::default()` is the registered strategy and uses the part's own feature edges; `EdgeFollowing::with_source(source)` takes another source.
2. **Mesh feature edges are found in `openchuck-path`, not taken from `openchuck-geom`.** `openchuck-path` may depend only on `openchuck-types` (the allow-list in `xtask/src/deps.rs`), and `openchuck-geom` is a separate crate. `curves::feature_chains` applies the same rule as `feature_edges` (an edge is a feature edge when its two triangles turn by at least the crease angle, or when it does not join exactly two triangles) after welding vertices a hundred-thousandth of a millimetre apart, and chains the edges at vertices where exactly two meet, as pencil clean-up chains creases ([0044](0044-slice-15-rest-and-pencil.md) item 5). Convex and concave edges both count. A vertex where three or more meet ends a chain, so the edges of a block are single-edge chains and the rim of a cylinder is one closed chain. Which chains to cut is chosen by the `crease_angle` (default 30 degrees, the viewport's, OI-13) and `min_length`; choosing individual chains is OI-204.
3. **The projection is along the tool axis (design question, proposed).** The rotary axis is the model X axis, so the tool axis at A is the radial direction. A faces each point (`RotaryMapping::a_facing`) and follows the chain as it winds round the part: one simultaneous XYZA move, not indexed passes. A chain that does not lie on the surface (`lies_on_surface` false) is projected onto it along the same radial line: a ray from outside the part inwards, and a point that misses is an error naming it. This is the "unrolled development" the slice mentions in effect: for a surface of revolution a radial projection is the development's inverse, and nothing is unrolled because the tool line already is the development's third axis. A chain on the part's own tessellation is not projected, so an edge hidden behind a lobe is not moved onto the lobe in front of it. A point within 1 micrometre of the rotary axis, where A cannot face it, is refused with its position.
4. **Depth is radial and constant by construction (design question, proposed; OP-08, checklist item 1).** The tool tip is `depth` below the surface point along the tool axis: `tip = point - axis * depth`. On a curved surface this is a constant radial depth whatever the surface's slope, which is what an engraved line is. Between points the machine moves in a straight line in XYZ and A, so points are cut so that no two neighbours are more apart than `max_step` (default 0.5 mm) and none turn about the axis by more than `2 acos(1 - tolerance / r)` at the point's radius `r` (at most 5 degrees): a chord then lies within the path tolerance of the arc and the depth holds between points. The common `stock_to_leave` is not used (a warning says so): the line is cut below the surface by design, so the output has no finish to leave. The contact point for the feed is the nearest point of the tool to the surface point (`cutter::cutter_contact`), which for a ball in a groove is the tip.
5. **Line width follows depth (slice item 4).** A tool cuts a groove whose half width, at a depth, is the radius at which its profile is that high (`RevolvedProfile::height_at`). With `line_width` above zero the depth is `height_at(line_width / 2)`: for a V-bit `width / 2 / tan(half angle)`, so a 60 degree V-bit cuts a 1 mm line 0.866 mm deep. A width wider than the tool is refused, and so is one the tool's flat tip already exceeds (zero depth). A depth deeper than the flutes is refused. The note on the path says the width the tool cuts at the depth, so a user who set `depth` sees the width it makes. `line_width` replaces `depth`; a warning says so when both are set.
6. **Engrave and carve (slice item 4).** `mode = engrave` cuts one line along each chain. `mode = carve` cuts a band `carve_width` wide at the surface, centred on the chain: lines offset across it, perpendicular to the tool axis (`axis x tangent`), from `-reach` to `+reach` with `reach = (carve_width - width_at_depth) / 2`, evenly spaced no more than `stepover` apart. A line beside the chain follows the surface at its own offset (a ray along the tool axis from outside; a miss uses the chain's level), so a carve on a curved surface has the same depth across it. A carve narrower than the line the tool cuts is refused, as is a carve with no width. A chain that runs along the tool axis (a radial edge) has no side: it is engraved, and in a carve it is skipped with a note. Both modes cut in levels of `depth_per_pass` (default 0: all at once), equal in size and ending at the full depth; each chain's levels are cut one after the other, shallowest first.
7. **Direction and start point (slice checklist).** `start`: `first` (the chain as chained, deterministic: by the welded vertex it begins at), `reversed`, `low_x` and `high_x` (the end of an open chain, or the point of a closed one, at the lowest or highest X), `angle` (nearest the `start_angle` about the rotary axis, from +Y towards +Z). A closed chain is rotated to its start and keeps its sense unless reversed. Chains are cut starting from the lowest start by X, Y and Z, each next the nearest start to the end of the one before (ties to the earlier), which is deterministic and keeps links short.
8. **Entry is a plunge only (owner's decision, 16 Oct 2026: plunge only for now; the other entries stay refused).** A lead, ramp or helix entry is checked against the finished surface, which an engraved line goes below by design, so the style is refused with a message naming `entry`. The shared services still link passes through the clearance geometry (OP-14) and time the cuts at the contact feed (OP-13).
9. **A region is refused.** The strategy does not honour regions (`honours_region` is false), as `passes_in_region` defaults say, with a message saying to select the edges to follow; there is nothing to cut in a region of faces.
10. **Parameters (OP-16).** Uniform with the others: the common ones and `mode`, `depth` (default 0.2 mm), `line_width`, `depth_per_pass`, `carve_width`, `start`, `start_angle`, `crease_angle` (30 degrees), `min_length` and `max_step`. `stepover` is required as everywhere (the application fills it from the tool's cutting data) and is used only by a carve. The count of points, levels and offset lines is limited (2 million points; 1000 levels or lines) with an error that says what to increase, so a bad value is refused instead of exhausting memory (NFR-07). Output is deterministic and identical at 1, 2 and N threads (NFR-06): all parallel work is `parallel::map_in_order`, and chains, levels and offsets are ordered as above.
11. **No format or family change.** New modules (`curves`, `edge_following`) with new types; the registry offers one more strategy; no public struct gains a field, so `cargo-semver-checks` has nothing to flag. No project schema number is taken.

## Alternatives considered

- **Passing the chains in `StrategyInput`:** rejected. A new field on a public struct that hosts build by literal fails `cargo-semver-checks` (decision 0026, "Library API"), and a strategy-level seam serves lane P without it.
- **A `CurveSource` on the `SurfaceSource` port (a provided method):** possible and compatible, but it ties edges to the surface type, so a drawing's curves or a user's chosen subset would need a wrapper surface. Kept as a note for lane P (OI-204 covers where chosen chains arrive).
- **Taking `feature_edges` from `openchuck-geom`:** not allowed by the dependency rules (item 2). The rule is the same and the test for the crease angle covers it.
- **Tool axis along the surface normal at the edge:** a feature edge has two normals, so the axis is ambiguous at a sharp edge, and a normal-tilted axis would need the simultaneous solver of [0036](0036-slice-13-simultaneous-finishing.md) with a gouge check that does not apply when the cut goes below the surface. Radial is what the machine can do at every point of a rotary part and is what "constant depth on a curved surface" asks for. Edges on a face turned along X (an end face) are reached from the side only (OI-205).
- **Alternating directions on levels and offset lines to shorten links:** deferred (OI-206); every pass retracts to the clearance geometry, which is safe and simple, and a long multi-level engraving pays for it in time.
- **Unrolling the chain with SP-7's 2D offsets for the carve:** rejected for now. The carve lines are parallel offsets of a polyline at constant depth, which the radial projection gives directly, and the offsets are small compared with the part radius. A V-carve of a region, which would need the unrolled region offsets, is OI-207.

## Consequences

- **Code:** `openchuck-path` (`curves`, `edge_following`, `Registry::builtin`). Nothing else changes; no viewport, UI or project change.
- **Documents:** the work breakdown (lane K, slice 16 status and checklist, the next free numbers), verification plan row OP-08, the open-items register, the decision index, the `openchuck-path` changelog. `xtask/traceability.toml` is not changed: slice 16 stays open for lane P and OI-204.
- **Open items:** OI-204 (choosing and storing edge chains: a selection kind and a schema number, for lanes K and P and the viewport's edge picking), OI-205 (radial tool axis only: edges on end faces, tilted axes, a check against the walls beside a groove), OI-206 (links: alternating directions on levels and offset lines), OI-207 (carving is parallel lines at constant depth: no V-carve of regions, no flat-floor carve), OI-208 (curves from a drawing: IMP-08 and SUG-06 need an importer and a rule for placing a flat curve on the wrapped part, of which the radial projection is the first).
