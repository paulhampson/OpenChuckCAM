## Decision

1. **OI-117: tools say whether they can cut straight down (accepted).** `Tool` gains `centre_cutting: Option<bool>` (TL-04), written only when set. `Tool::can_plunge()` is the stated value, or when it is not stated, true for every shape but a custom profile (nothing says what a custom profile's centre does). `EntryParameters::check_tool(can_plunge)` refuses `entry = plunge` for a tool that cannot, with a message that names the parameter; `ramp`, `helix` and `lead` are not refused here, their geometry is checked when the entry is built. The application calls it for every operation. This is a field added to a public struct that hosts build by literal, so `cargo-semver-checks` would fail; the owner chose to add the field and move the library family to **0.7.0** (as for OI-123; lane F had already taken 0.6.0 on `main`, so this is the next bump). Project **schema 8**: a plain addition, one migration step that changes nothing, no fixture folder.
2. **Layers are radial (accepted).** The stock is removed from the outside in. A layer cuts with the tool tip at `max(level, floor)` measured along the tool axis, where `floor` is the lowest the tip may go with the stock to leave (the tool is dropped onto the part grown by the stock to leave, as in helical finishing). The layer levels run from the stock radius to the lowest floor in equal steps, none deeper than `stepdown`. Over the part the layers are flat above it and follow it inside the band of the layer that reaches it, so the stock to leave is left everywhere the tool can reach and a cut is never deeper than one step.
   - **Continuous** (item 3): each layer is one helix about the rotary axis, A turning once every pitch of X (at most the stepover), the tool across the axis; A is continuous (OP-15).
   - **Indexed** (item 2): A is held at positions `index_angle` apart; each layer is lines along X at the stepover across the sector the position faces; A does not move while it cuts.
   - A node of a layer is cut only where more than `min_cut` of material stands above the tool's underside among the cells the tool wholly covers (item 3 below). The path between two nodes is refined, down to a quarter of their spacing, where the tool follows the part and the straight machine move would stray by more than 0.4 of the tolerance (decision 0018 item 12). Runs of cut nodes are passes; a pass ends where nothing is left to cut and the shared services link the next.
   - The roughing covers the part's length plus three tool radii and the stock to leave at each end, so an entry has room beside the part. Stock beyond that is not touched.
3. **Remaining stock is a heightfield (item 1).** `openchuck_path::field::HeightStock` holds the radius of the top of the material on a grid over (X, A), read once from the `StockModel` it is given (the dexel stock of the setup, with the material the earlier operations left, SIM-06, or the analytic stock). Cells are a third of the tool radius (larger when the grid would pass 400,000 cells). Rules, all on the safe side: material under an undercut in the setup's stock is taken as solid; a cut removes a cell only when the whole cell is inside the tool, and the same rule decides whether a node has anything to cut, so a cell the tool only partly reaches cannot keep a second run busy; `contains` and `ray_entry` treat a cell as occupied when it or a neighbour is. The field is cut as each layer's passes are planned and copied when a layer starts. It implements `StockModel`, so the shared services read it like any stock.
4. **A pass carries its stock (item 4).** `Pass` gains `stock: Option<PassStock>`, the material when the pass's layer starts: one copy per layer, shared by its passes. It can hold more material than is left when a later pass of the layer starts, never less, so every check against it errs on the safe side; a copy per pass would hold a whole field (up to 1.6 MB) for each of the hundreds or thousands of passes of a part, and the owner chose not to (15 Oct 2026). Hosts that build passes by literal add `stock: None` (part of the 0.7.0 bump). The shared services use it for the pass's entry, its leave and the link from the pass before it; a pass without it behaves exactly as before and the golden files do not change. With it, the entry and leave probe the stock across the tool's whole width, not only along the axis, because the tool beside the line it comes down on can clip stock the line does not touch.
5. **Parameters (item 4, OP-11, OP-16).** `rotary_roughing` has the common parameters, with the entry parameters of decision 0026 (default `plunge`), and: `form` (`continuous` or `indexed`, default continuous), `stepdown` (mm, required: the application fills it from the tool's cutting data for the setup's material, TL-05, as for the stepover), `index_angle` (degrees, default 30), `start_angle` (default 0), `max_angle_step` (degrees, default 5) and `min_cut` (mm, default 0.15, which is more than one simulation cell at the default 0.1 mm, because the remaining stock is only known to a cell). Stock to leave is per operation; per selection stays with slice 12 item 4. Engagement-based feeds (SIM-10) are P3 and not built; the feed is the one number the operation sets.
6. **Links are checked against what is left (item 6, OP-14).** For a pass that carries its stock, the link from the pass before it no longer goes to the clearance cylinder when it need not: the tool leaves the cut upward, travels at the height of the highest stock under its footprint at any sample of the straight machine move (samples at most half a tool radius apart, A included) plus the approach distance, and descends to the entry. When that height is not below the clearance cylinder the old link is used. The checks use the stock the pass's layer starts with, so a link never relies on material its own layer has yet to cut. Links of passes without a stock are unchanged.
7. **Leftover report (item 7).** `openchuck_sim::Deviation::leftover(leave, tolerance)` counts the part's surface samples at, above and below a stock to leave (least, mean and most left, the share at it, the worst place above it). `openchuck-cli simulate` prints it for a setup whose last operation is rotary roughing, with a tolerance of two cells. Where the report shows material above the stock to leave it is a place the tool could not reach (item 8), not an error.
8. **Limits kept honest (OI-150 to OI-153).** Narrow strips next to a part's end faces and in concave corners are left because the tool cannot get closer than its own size; the report shows them and rest machining (slice 15) is the cure (OI-150). Ramp and helix entries are refused, with the existing message, where a pass starts over a slope or at the edge of an indexed sector, because the entry circle then runs into the part; the default stays `plunge`, and placing entries away from the part is OI-151. `min_cut` is not tied to the project's simulation cell (OI-152). The shank, holder and flute length are not checked against the depth of a layer (COL-05, OI-102, OI-153).

## Alternatives considered

- **Slab layers for both forms:** simpler, but a continuous pass over a round part would then leave a faceted envelope and a leftover that is not uniform. Rejected by the owner.
- **A mutable `StockModel` in the library** (or the dexel stock cloned and cut while planning): the library may not depend on the simulator, and cloning a dexel stock per layer costs memory proportional to its columns and cuts at the simulator's cost. The heightfield is exact enough for an outside-in cut and cheap to copy.
- **Retract to the clearance cylinder between every pair of passes** (the existing link): safe and what finishing does, but a roughing layer has many passes and the retract is most of the time. The owner chose the stock-aware link.
- **Add the tool field without a bump,** as a separate type beside `Tool`: avoided breaking the library but left a tool record that must be read twice. The owner chose the field and the bump.
- **A per-tool-shape rule for centre-cutting only:** no stored field, no schema change; rejected by the owner, who wanted the user able to state it.
