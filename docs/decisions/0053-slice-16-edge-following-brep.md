This is lane P: slice 16 item 1 for B-rep edges. It builds on [decision 0047](0047-slice-16-edge-following-mesh.md) (accepted; lane K's strategy, the `CurveSource` seam, `EdgeChain`, `EdgeFollowing::with_source`), which it does not change, and on the STEP importer and `BrepSurface` of [0027](0027-slice-11a-step-import.md) and [0017](0017-sp-9-exact-surfaces.md). It claims record **0053** and **OI-226 to OI-230**, assigned by the orchestrator, and **no project schema number**: choosing and storing edge chains is a new selection kind and a schema bump owned by the viewport edge-picking work (OI-204, owner's decision 16 Oct 2026), so nothing here touches a project file or the viewport.

## Context

A B-rep knows which curves are edges. Each `BrepEdge` has a stable ID, the IDs of the faces it joins (de-duplicated by the importer: `occ_step.cpp` sorts and removes repeats, so a seam, where a face meets itself, lists its one face) and a polyline sampled at the model's tolerance (default 0.01 mm and 0.1 rad). Lane K's source for meshes has to guess the edges from the triangles' crease; a B-rep need not guess, but it has edges a user does not mean: the seam of every cylinder, cone, sphere and torus, and the boundary where a fillet runs tangent into its neighbour.

## Decision

1. **The source is `BrepEdges` in `openchuck_path::brep_edges` (design question, proposed).** `BrepEdges::new(&BrepModel)` evaluates each edge once and implements `CurveSource`. `with_crease`, `with_min_length` and `with_edges(&BrepModel, &[EdgeId])` (the edges of the existing `Edges` target: only those, so a user's selection is what is followed) set what to keep; `shared()` gives the `Arc` for `EdgeFollowing::with_source`; `edge_ids()` lists the edges kept. The chains come from the edges' own polylines (their tessellation), never from the face tessellation. The surface argument of `chains` is not used. New items only: `cargo-semver-checks` has nothing to flag, and the `openchuck-path` changelog says so.
2. **Which edges are real (design question, proposed; the choice a reviewer is most likely to change).** Evaluated with the exact faces (`ExactFace::closest`, the face's outward normal), at the midpoints of up to 32 segments of the polyline:
   - an edge between two faces is kept when the angle between the faces' normals reaches the crease angle somewhere along it. **The default crease angle is 1 degree**, so the tangent-continuous boundary of a fillet or of two halves of one cylinder is not an edge, a 90 degree corner and a 30 degree chamfer are, and the sharpest turn along the edge decides (a variable-radius blend counts where it is sharp). `with_crease(0.0)` keeps every edge between two faces, the tangent ones too, for a user who wants to engrave the line where a fillet starts;
   - a **seam** is never an edge: the edge lists one face, and at every sample the closest point of that face is on the boundary of its parameter box with the opposite side of the box at the same place (0.1 micrometre scale test, `1e-4` mm). A cylinder's seam is therefore dropped and a split line a quarter turn round is not;
   - an edge of one face that is not a seam (the boundary of an open surface), an edge of three or more faces (non-manifold) and an edge whose normals cannot be evaluated are always kept: the topology says it is an edge;
   - an edge of no length (a degenerate edge, a pole) is dropped.
   This differs from the mesh rule on purpose. The mesh rule must infer edges from a crease; here the crease angle only decides between two real topological edges, so its default is small, where the mesh default is 30 degrees (OI-13, the viewport's). The operation parameters `crease_angle` and `min_length` are lane K's, read by the default (mesh) source; for a source given to `with_source` they do nothing and the strategy says so in a warning (decision 0047). The B-rep source holds its own values; carrying the operation's values into it belongs with the pipeline wiring (OI-226).
3. **Chains are joined by the corner, not by the vertex ID.** Edge ends within a hundred-thousandth of a millimetre are one corner, as for the mesh. Edges are joined end to end (reversing a polyline where its sense differs) where exactly two kept edges meet; a corner of one or of three or more ends a chain, so the twelve edges of a box are twelve open chains and a cylinder's rims are two closed chains. A closed edge (its two ends at one corner) is a closed chain by itself and repeats its first point last. Only kept edges join, so a dropped tangent edge breaks a chain there.
4. **Order is deterministic (NFR-06).** Edges are taken in ID order, which the importer fixes (decision 0003). Closed edges come first, in ID order; then open chains, each started at the end of the first unused edge (in ID order) that is not an ordinary join, walking in the edge's own sense from that end; then loops of ordinary joins, started at their lowest edge ID. The strategy then orders the cut by its own rule (decision 0047 item 7).
5. **Chains lie on the surface; the strategy does not project them.** `lies_on_surface` is true. The polylines lie on the exact faces to the model's tessellation tolerance (a chord of a curved edge is inside it by at most the linear deflection, 0.01 mm by default); the engraved depth is therefore constant to that tolerance plus the strategy's path tolerance, as the tests show. Projecting each point onto the exact face would remove the chord error but would also cast from outside and could land an edge hidden behind a lobe on the lobe in front, which decision 0047 item 3 avoids for the part's own edges. A model tessellated more coarsely than the cut's tolerance says so: the depth error is the model's (OI-229).
6. **The importer is unchanged; the library takes a `BrepModel`.** Only `openchuck-import` may use the kernel, and `openchuck-path` may depend only on `openchuck-types`, so the source takes the plain `BrepModel` the importer returns. Tests that import STEP are in `openchuck-app` behind `kernel-tests`, as `step_exact` is.
7. **Selection in the application is not wired (open, OI-226).** The application's operation pipeline reads every model as a placed triangle mesh (`crate::model::placed_mesh_hashed`, `MeshSurface`) and has no path from a STEP model to a `BrepSurface` for any strategy, so there is no place yet to choose the B-rep source for a STEP part. The library is ready (`EdgeFollowing::with_source(BrepEdges::new(&model)?.shared())`); building the B-rep path through the pipeline (the surface, the placement of the model's edges and the choice of source by the model's kind) is OI-226. The registry still offers the mesh strategy only.
8. **No format, family or schema change.** New module, new types, one export in `lib.rs`; the registry is not changed.

## Alternatives considered

- **Every topological edge, seams included:** rejected. Every cylinder, cone, sphere and torus has a seam that is not a feature of the part; engraving along it would be a surprise (and a line the user cannot see in the viewport).
- **The mesh rule on the face tessellation (`MeshFeatureEdges` over a `BrepSurface`):** works today with no new code, but throws away the edge IDs and the exact edges, would cut along the triangles of a fillet at the crease angle, and cannot take a user's chosen edges (the `Edges` target).
- **Dropping tangent edges always (no `with_crease(0.0)`):** simpler, but a designer's fillet boundary can be exactly the line to engrave; the angle is a number the user chooses, as in the mesh case.
- **Keeping tangent edges by default:** every box with a filleted edge would give a chain on each side of the fillet that the viewport draws as no edge at all (the viewport draws feature edges at the crease angle, OI-13).
- **Joining by vertex ID:** possible (each `BrepEdge` has `vertices`), but an edge without vertices (`None`) would need a second path, and the welded position works for every edge. The two agree for kernel output.
- **A crease angle per operation parameter shared by both sources:** the cleanest for a user (one number), but a source built before the strategy runs does not see the parameters; the application wiring can pass them (OI-226).

## Consequences

- **Code:** `openchuck-path` (`brep_edges`, `lib.rs`). Nothing else changes; no viewport, UI, importer or project change.
- **Documents:** the work breakdown (slice 16 status and checklist, the next free numbers), verification plan row OP-08, the open-items register, the decision index, the `openchuck-path` changelog.
- **Open items:** OI-226 (the pipeline has no B-rep path: choosing the B-rep source for a STEP part, passing the operation's crease angle and shortest chain), OI-227 (the chain does not carry edge IDs, so a viewport highlight or a stored choice of chains needs a map from chain to edges; with OI-204), OI-228 (a seam wanted deliberately, or a tangent edge only on one side of a face, are not offered; the rule is per edge), OI-229 (depth accuracy is the model's tessellation tolerance; a snap of each point to the exact edge curve or to the faces is not built), OI-230 (edge ends are joined by position; a model whose edge ends differ by more than a hundred-thousandth of a millimetre at a join, from a lossy CAD export, would give separate chains; joining by vertex ID is the alternative).
