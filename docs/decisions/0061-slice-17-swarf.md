## Context

Slice 17 (work breakdown; OP-07): cut ruled or near-ruled selected surfaces with the side of the tool. Spike SP-11 ([decision 0021](0021-sp-11-swarf.md), accepted) settled the method: detection by a straight-line fit per iso-line family, placement from the ruling and the normal, the four-axis projection of the ideal axis with the tilt reported, the measured deviation as the gate, one cone model for straight and tapered flanks, and pose spacing from the tolerance. It left the tool detail (OI-97) and the machine conventions (OI-98) to the slice. Swarf is B-rep only (OI-96), so the strategy reads exact faces; lane D's `BrepSurface` ([decision 0027](0027-slice-11a-step-import.md)) and lane R's precedent of a provided `SurfaceSource` method for what only an exact surface can answer ([decision 0051](0051-slice-18-radial-drilling.md) item 2) shape how. The slice's design refinement asks for the detection method and tolerance, which tool shapes can flank cut, and whether P1 is limited to analytic surfaces (it is not: decision 0021 item 1).

## Decision

1. **Faces reach the strategy through the port (design question).** `SurfaceSource` gains a provided method `exact_faces() -> Vec<FaceRef>` (a face number and a `&dyn ParametricFace`), empty for a mesh; `BrepSurface` answers with its faces and `CompositeSurface` forwards the model's. The strategy `swarf` (`openchuck_path::swarf::Swarf`, in `Registry::builtin`) cuts every face it is given, so a user's selection of faces through `BrepSurface::with_faces` is what is cut, as for drilling. A mesh is refused with a message saying a mesh has no rulings (OI-96). No required trait item is added and no host breaks.
2. **Detection** (`detect_rulings`; decision 0021 item 2, with one refinement). For each family of iso-lines, 17 lines are sampled at 33 points, a straight line is fitted to each (the principal axis by power iteration, seeded from the chord, or from the chord to the middle sample on a closed line, so a circle does not read as straight), and the worst sample distance is the residual. A family within the operation's tolerance is ruled. **Refinement (design question): when both families fit, the one whose rulings lie closer to perpendicular to the rotary axis is used, then `v`**, where decision 0021 said the smaller residual then `v`. A plane is ruled both ways with residual zero, and only the family the four axes can reach is any use: the wall of a flat whose `v` runs along X would otherwise be refused as unreachable. A face ruled in neither family is refused naming the residual and the limit. The twist of the normal along the rulings is measured and reported.
3. **The flank is the straight part of the profile that reaches the cutting radius** (`Flank`; decision 0021 item 5): the first straight piece rising to the cutting radius, or the straight side above the piece that reaches it. The side of a flat or ball end (half angle 0), the taper of a tapered ball, the V of a V-bit or chamfer mill, all as one cone. The contact sits on it, so a ball or corner radius is below the engaged part and never on the wall (OI-97). The tool tip sits `tip_extension` (default 0) beyond the end of the ruling along the tool axis, and the flank's start is at that end. A ruling that needs more flank than the tool has (ruling length times the cosine of the half angle, plus the extension, against the flank's length) is refused naming the lengths; a tool with no straight side (a ball whose flute length is its radius) is refused too. Cutting a tall wall in several passes along the ruling is not built (OI-239).
4. **Placement** (decision 0021 item 4). The ruling is the iso-line's extent on the trimmed face (its ends found by bisection against the trim test); its direction `d` and the outward normal `n` at its middle give the ideal axis `cos(a) d + sin(a) n` and the axis point `r` from the contact (`r` the flank radius at the contact height, with the contact offset by the stock to leave along `n`). The four-axis axis is the projection into the plane perpendicular to the rotary axis; **A is `RotaryMapping::a_facing` of it**, the convention every strategy uses, so the Z1's A direction comes from the machine profile (OI-98 closes; a first-principles test checks the axis is the machine's vertical at each pose). Rulings along the rotary axis have no projection and are refused saying no taper helps (OI-94). The tilt is the angle between the two axes; per pose the half angle that would make the ideal axis perpendicular to the rotary axis is computed, and when every tilted pose needs the same one (within 0.05 degrees) the refusal and the note name it.
5. **Which end the tip sits at is chosen once per face (design question):** the end with the smaller four-axis tilt at the face's middle ruling; on a tie (every straight tool, and a cone with its matching taper, tilt the same both ways) the end nearer the rotary axis, then the smaller Y, then the smaller Z. The tool therefore reaches in from outside the part, which is what a boss wall or a pocket wall on a rotary part needs, and A never jumps by half a turn along a face. `tip_end = reversed` takes the other end (the tool comes from below in the model, which A = 180 makes upright). `direction = reverse` cuts the face in decreasing parameter. A face whose directrix comes back to its start (a whole boss) is one closed run whose last pose repeats its first; a face whose rulings are missing over part of the directrix (a trim) gives one run per stretch that has them.
6. **Pose spacing** (decision 0021 item 6): 8 initial intervals on a closed face, 4 on an open one, then each move is halved until the tool half way along the machine's straight move (X, Y, Z and A linear in the machine frame) keeps the ruling there within `CHORD_FACTOR` (0.4) times the tolerance beyond what the pose at that ruling leaves anyway (a tilted flank never lies on its ruling, and more poses cannot help that), and until A turns no more than 5 degrees in a move (`MAX_ANGLE_STEP`: a helicoid is swept exactly by A alone and would otherwise be five poses over 200 degrees). At most 12 halvings per move and 20,000 poses per face (NFR-07).
7. **The gate** (decision 0021 item 3; OP-12, OI-95). Samples on every pose's ruling and every mid-move ruling (25 along each) are measured against the union of the tool at every pose and at three points of each move between poses, by the signed distance to the revolved profile (continued upward at the cutting radius; the shank and holder are not known, OI-237), less the stock to leave. **A cut deeper than the tolerance refuses the face** with a message naming the depth, where, the twist (when the face is ruled but not developable, with a smaller tool radius as the remedy) and the tilt with the taper that would remove it. **Material left beyond the allowance has three levels (the owner's change):** within the tolerance it is silent; more than the tolerance but no more than `max_material_left` is a note naming the amount and where; more than `max_material_left` refuses the face naming the amount, where, the limit and what to change (the taper that removes a tilt, a smaller tool radius for a twist, otherwise a tighter tolerance for closer poses or a larger limit). `max_material_left` is a strategy parameter in the uniform schema (OP-16); zero, the default, takes 0.1 mm on every pass: ten times the default tolerance, a step a finish cut shows and the simulation's finest setting resolves (verification plan 4.1, 0.02 mm). A set value wins. It is not a project format change (operation parameters are a free map, decision 0008 item 6). *Revised by the owner on 17 Oct 2026, relayed by the orchestrator session (decision 0049):* the first acceptance asked for a limit less stringent on a roughing pass than on a finishing pass, and it was built as a default read from the stock to leave (the one sign of the pass's role the strategy has), with a common `role` parameter or a `StrategyInput` field named as the options for a real role. The owner then decided that one limit, configured by the user for each operation, is enough: the role-dependent default and the role options are dropped, and OI-258 (the same rule for the other strategies) is closed as not needed. On a developable face with no tilt the leftover is the chord between poses, under 0.4 times the tolerance by item 6, so the note and the refusal are reached by a face the four axes cannot follow or by a limit set below the chord; a gouge is refused first. Every face gets a note with its family, residual, twist, pose count and the deepest cut. The gate measures the machine's motion, not the discrete poses: between two poses the flank sweeps the wall, so the scallop a check of discrete poses shows (0.02 mm for a 1 mm flank radius at 0.4 mm spacing, in the cone test) is not real; the tests' independent checker sweeps the motion too.
8. **Parameters are uniform (OP-16).** The common parameters first (stepover is described as not used: one pass per face; the tolerance's description says what it gates) and then `tip_extension`, `max_material_left`, `tip_end` (`auto`, `reversed`) and `direction` (`forward`, `reverse`). Regions and leave zones are refused, as for drilling: the faces are the selection.
9. **Determinism (NFR-06).** Faces in order, rulings and poses in parameter order, the gate's samples through `parallel::map_in_order`; the result is identical at 1, 2 and N threads.
10. **Not built here:** the LinuxCNC gate 3 replay and the UI's "B-rep only" message for a mesh selection (OI-96, OI-238). The application wiring itself arrived with [decision 0056](0056-brep-path-through-the-pipeline.md), merged before this record: a STEP part is machined on its exact `BrepSurface`, built from the operation's Faces set, inside the composite surface, which forwards `exact_faces`, so the registry's `swarf` strategy runs on a STEP part unchanged. What remains for OI-238 is a reference job with a ruled wall through the application so gate 3 replays a swarf program; neighbouring faces, holder and shank in the gate (OI-237); several passes along a ruling (OI-239); sampling limits of detection and the gate (OI-240); a lead-in tangent to the wall (OI-241).

## Alternatives considered

- **A `Swarf::with_faces` constructor taking the faces** (as `EdgeFollowing::with_source`): rejected; the registry's strategy would then not work on a generic input, and the port already carries what only an exact surface knows (`holes`, decision 0051).
- **Returning `&[ExactFace]` from the port:** ties the port to the library's evaluator; a host with its own kernel implements `ParametricFace`, so the port returns that.
- **Smaller residual then `v` when both families fit (decision 0021 item 2 as written):** refuses a plane wall whose `v` runs along X. Reachability costs one number per family.
- **Noting material left but never refusing it** (as first proposed): rejected by the owner; excess material is poor performance to be addressed, so a limit refuses it, and the limit is a parameter rather than the tolerance itself so a note band remains.
- **Treating a tilted flank as a chord error and halving until the limit:** runs every move to the depth limit on a cone cut with a straight tool, tens of thousands of poses, and gains nothing; the gate refuses it anyway.
- **A separate ruling tolerance:** decision 0021 ties the fit to the operation's tolerance; one number.
