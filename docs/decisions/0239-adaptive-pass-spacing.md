## Context

A fixed stepover, and the ideal scallop formula for a ball or a bull nose, do not show how much material is really left between passes: on a steep or vertical profile the cusp the formula predicts is not the cusp the tool leaves, and fitting points within one pass does not choose where the next pass goes. The owner asked for adaptive spacing on every operation that cuts passes along or round the axis, keeping each operation's own target (its stock to leave and its layers) and any A positions the user fixed.

## Decision (the owner's individual choices, in plain words)

1. **What is measured.** The height of the material left between neighbouring passes, above the operation's own target: the surface plus its stock to leave, and the layer it is cutting (decision 0345). What the operation is meant to leave is never counted as missed, and the spacing is never refined to cut below it.
   - **Rotary roughing (the owner's choice (c) on OI-1453, 31 Oct 2026, relayed by the coordinator, decision 0049).** Roughing is measured only against the last layer's target, the part with its stock to leave, wherever a layer's tool rests on the part; a roughing layer's own level above the part is not a target, because the layers below cut it again. **Roughing layers above the last are not individually checked for excess.** This amends the layer sentence above for rotary roughing only; finishing layers keep their own targets (decision 0345).
   - **Flat Facing (agreed with the coordinator, 31 Oct 2026).** Facing's target is its face, on the layer that cuts it, and the part with its stock to leave where the part rises above the face. The measure always runs, and the spacing is never wider than the Stepover. A flat end on the flat face leaves no cusp between lines no wider apart than its flat, so it adds nothing there; lines are added for a tool with a corner radius or a ball, and where the part rises above the face.
2. **Which operations.** Helical finishing and every operation that cuts passes along the axis: axial and indexed finishing, the axial pattern of simultaneous finishing, both axial forms of roughing, and flat Facing. The stepover is the widest spacing, reduced locally where needed. Facing's defaults and equal-depth layers are kept. Patterns that do not cut along the axis are not changed.
3. **Fixed A stays fixed.** An operation whose A positions the user chose (indexed finishing, a chosen side) gains no cutting angle and no rotary cutting. Where rotary cutting is allowed, a partial clean-up arc between passes is a permitted result, not a ring the operation must add; it keeps the stock to leave, the selections and protections, the cutting direction, the travel limits and the entries and links.
   - **Indexed finishing (OI-1452, the owner's choice (a) on 31 Oct 2026, relayed by the coordinator, decision 0049).** Indexed finishing cuts one pass at each listed position, so it has no stepover to space. Where the material between two neighbouring positions stands higher than the Allowed excess, passes are added at the same A as the nearer position, offset across the axis towards the other, down to the Smallest spacing. No A angle is added and nothing cuts while A turns; with adaptive spacing off the path is unchanged. What a vertical tool at one A cannot reach stays unverified, with the warning.
4. **One height setting.** Scallop with adaptive spacing off (0 mm by default, exactly as before) and Allowed excess with it on (0.01 mm by default). A default applies only when the operation has no value. The measure uses the operation's own validated cutter model, including flat, tapered and custom tools where the operation supports them; the closed-form scallop is only a first guess.
5. **Turning it on from a saved zero.** An operation saved with Scallop 0 (which meant off) asks for the height before adaptive spacing is turned on: it suggests 0.01 mm, allows a deliberate 0, and explains that 0 here asks for no excess at all and may stay unverified or meet the limits. Cancel changes nothing. Confirm sets the switch and the height together in one undoable step. The value is shared: turning it off again keeps the height chosen as the Scallop.
6. **Per operation.** On for a new operation of these strategies; an existing operation is migrated to an explicit off (project schema 24); explicit settings are kept. **Changed wording, for the owner to see at acceptance:** the owner's choice named schema 23, shared with decision 0344; schema 23 merged with #412 before this was built, so the coordinator assigned schema 24 on 31 Oct 2026 (relayed). The choice itself is unchanged. No project-wide or global default.
7. **Smallest spacing.** 0.01 mm by default, editable. A stepover already below it is not enlarged.
8. **Limits, per operation across all its layers.** 1,000,000 adaptive geometry queries; 1,000,000 points emitted (counting the entries and links); 10,000 added passes; 20 levels of refinement; 65,536 pending intervals. They bound work, not time or memory, and are charged before the work is done, with checked arithmetic, so the result does not depend on the thread count or the machine's load.
9. **What the limits cover.** Adaptive planning, measuring and fitting. The final protection, entry, clearance and replay checks run in full under their own limits, and their cost is reported apart, so a whole generation may make more than 1,000,000 queries.
10. **A partial result.** Where a limit is reached or the excess cannot be confirmed, the valid path made so far is kept, a prominent warning says where, and export asks the user to acknowledge that current result first. An acknowledged result is never called complete. Bad input, cancelling, a gouge or a protection reached, clearance, entry and replay failures stay errors.
11. **Uncertain places.** Where samples meet the height but no bound justifies it, refinement stops there and the place is marked unverified, rather than subdividing blindly. Excess that is found is refined within the limits. A narrow feature may be missed, and the warning says so. A cutter the measure cannot model is reported as unverified, never ignored.
12. **What is reported.** The number of passes added, the smallest spacing used, which limits were met and why places are unverified, with where when known; progress and the generation time include the refinement.
13. **Off, and versions.** Off, the output is byte-identical at the same version. Because the default for new operations changes the output, the workspace's minor version is bumped (extending decision 0048 beyond API breaks); public API changes still follow decision 0048.

## How it fits what is there

- Facing's merged `schema_for` and `resolve_parameters` hooks carry the mode-dependent settings: the full schema knows every name, the active one gives the active controls and defaults; resolving refuses unknown names and drops known inactive ones, which the host keeps.
- Roughing's layer engine stays ROUGHPATH's: its groups, pass-start stock, links and rotary resets are kept. Facing's strict completion checks are separate from adaptive coverage and are not waived by an acknowledgement. Coverage windows are not protective geometry, and warn-only samples are not clearance certificates.
- Exact contacts (decisions 0260, 0277) are used as they stand on `main`; nothing is forced through a mesh approximation, and the strategy's own sampler is never the proof. **Added sentence, for the owner to see at acceptance** (agreed with the coordinator 31 Oct 2026): the bounded checker of decision 0255 is test-only (`openchuck_testkit::contact`, independent of `openchuck-path` by its item 6), so the product's excess is bounded in `openchuck-path` on the operation's own cutter model over exact drops, the tests grade the result with the testkit checker independently, and a place where no bound justifies the height is reported unverified (item 11).

## Alternatives and risks

- **Keep only the ideal scallop formula:** cheap, but it does not measure what is really left; it stays as the off route.
- **Extra cutting angles or mandatory rings:** would change what a fixed-angle operation means; not chosen.
- **Two height fields:** rejected for one shared value.
- **A wall-clock limit:** would make a partial result depend on the machine's load and the thread count; not chosen.
- **Refusing every partial result:** not chosen; a warned result acknowledged at export was.
- **Risks:** closer passes and arcs add work, output size and possibly engagement (OI-1373). Faces the tool cannot reach, contact, tessellation and trim uncertainty, and the limits can leave places unverified. No acknowledgement or extra pass proves coverage, clearance or that a program suits a machine: generated G-code remains the user's to verify (NFR-12).

## How stage 3 builds it (lane ENDFACE, branch `claude/lane-endface-adaptive`)

- **Where.** `openchuck-path::adaptive` holds the settings, the limits, the measure and the halving; each strategy gives its passes as a family (across it: an angle for passes along the axis, an offset along X for the wraps of a helix; along it: X, or the degrees turned) with its own drop, region and parts with their own stock to leave.
- **The measure.** At stations along two neighbouring passes the tool (grown by the stock to leave) is dropped at 8 to 64 evenly spaced poses between them, as the operation drops it. Each contact's clearance to the real tool at the nearby poses of either pass (the machine's straight moves between the fitted points, densified so the travel along a pass adds no more than a tenth of the Allowed excess), less the stock to leave there, is the material left over the target. Between samples the height is bounded from the samples' own slope, `(p + q + d) / 2`; where the samples meet the Allowed excess and that bound does not, the place is unverified and not refined (item 11). The samples are where the operation's own drops land, so surface no drop between the two passes reaches is not sampled: for indexed finishing the offset across the axis is taken on the circle through the tool's centre, so the contacts reach the whole angle between the positions (with the part's radius alone they stopped short, and the tests caught 0.69 mm left). It is not a proof: the tests grade the result with the test kit's coverage of the exact surface.
- **Halving.** Where a station holds too much, a pass is added halfway between the two passes over that stretch and a tool radius past it, and each half is measured again, left first, never closer than the Smallest spacing.
- **Limits.** Queries are drops and rays through a counter; the stations of an interval are measured in parallel, their most queries charged first, so the work is the same at any thread count; a candidate pass that would pass a limit is dropped whole. The point limit charges each pass's entry and link at 16 points (OI-1455).
- **Built:** helical finishing (clean-up arcs between the wraps; the helix's ends as before, OI-1454), axial finishing, the axial pattern of simultaneous finishing, indexed finishing (passes at each position's A, offset across the axis; past 75 degrees from both positions a vertical tool is taken as unable to reach, and the place is reported). **Second pull request (the owner's choice, 31 Oct 2026, relayed by the coordinator, decision 0049):** rotary roughing's two axial forms and flat Facing have no adaptive switch or setting in the first; a second pull request of the lane adds their measure and the switch, on for a new operation, with its own explicit-off migration (OI-1453). R1 is met once both are merged (the owner's agreement, relayed).
- **The second pull request (OI-1453).** Rotary roughing's indexed form along its own direction, its continuous form along the length, and flat Facing gain the switch (on for a new operation, shown only for those forms and that shape), the Allowed excess (the operation's `scallop` value, shown only with the switch on, since neither has a Scallop height) and the Smallest spacing; project schema 25 migrates their saved operations to an explicit off.
  - **Inside ROUGHPATH's layer engine.** Each layer's lines are cut as before (groups, pass-start stock, links and rotary resets kept); then, with the switch on, lines are added between neighbouring lines of the layer where the material over the target (item 1) is too high, and the layer's passes are emitted in order across the part. A line along X at one A is a pass of a family: across it the offset from the axis (the indexed form, flat Facing) or the angle (the continuous form, whose last line's neighbour is the first, a turn on).
  - **Only what a line could cut is measured.** A sample counts only where a line of the family at that offset or angle would run: where, on the family's own nodes either side, the region allows a cut and more than the Smallest cut stood above the tool as the layer started. Added lines are cut on the same nodes, so what the measure counts an added line can reach. Without this, material at the stock's ends that no line cuts was refined to the Smallest spacing and met the query limit.
  - **Every A position against the stock as the layer started.** One position takes material another overlaps; measured against the stock just before each position, a ridge one position left below the Smallest cut was measured by neither (the coverage check found 0.017 mm at a sector edge).
  - **Between the indexed form's A positions.** The gap to each neighbouring position is measured against the nearer outer line of that position, as seen from this one; a place there that holds too much is reported (the tool, held vertical at one of the operation's angles, cannot reach there), not refined, as for indexed finishing (item 3). A flat end's corner leaves a ridge there that no line at either A cleans (0.339 mm on the test cylinder with a 6 mm flat end and 30 degree positions); it was left silently before, and is now said.
  - **Measured on a radius 10 cylinder from radius 12 stock** (stepover 3, stepdown 1.5, the default Allowed excess; the most left, by the test kit's coverage over X 2 to 10, off then on): a 6 mm flat end, indexed, 0.339 both, no lines added, the gap between positions said; continuous along the length, 0.073 then 0.0045 (78 lines added); a 4 mm ball, indexed, 0.220 then 0.0061 (264 lines); continuous along the length, 0.400 then 0.0092 (182 lines), with the places at the part's two end edges reported unbounded (item 11: the excess there steps from 0 to a plateau just under the Allowed excess, and the bound between stations beside a step is above it); relaxing item 11 there is OI-1510, for the owner).
- **Partial results.** `PathWarning::requires_acknowledgement`; the leave-zone route returns the strategy's notes and warnings; `openchuck-app`'s output plan must be acknowledged for its own run before it writes, and the command line takes `--acknowledge-partial-path`.
- **Library version.** 0.29.0 (decision 0048, and item 13).
