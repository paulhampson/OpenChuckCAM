## Context

Decision 0115 found the blisk's helical overcut (OI-331) in the shared path fitter's test: a move was accepted when the straight machine move was within the chord limit at its middle alone, and where the dropped tool's height along the move is S-shaped with its inflection at the middle, the whole move passed while cutting 0.2 mm into a blade's flank a quarter of the way along. The shared fitter (`wrap_fit`: helical, axial and rest machining) now tests the quarter points too. Simultaneous finishing has its own fitter with the same test at the middle alone (decision 0036 item 7, `simultaneous::Fitter::refine`), left unchanged by that lane and recorded as OI-348: the lobed reference job passes the independent checker at its dropped points, but nothing checked between them.

This lane was asked first to measure whether simultaneous finishing can overcut the same way, on the owner's blisk and on the lobed reference job, with the independent checker as the measure, and then to remove the weakness if it is real.

**The weakness is real, and shown on an exact surface.** Decision 0115's cubic ramp (`crates/openchuck-path/tests/fit_interior.rs`, `CubicRamp`: a 40 mm cylinder whose radius ramps by a cubic over one 72 degree seed interval, point-symmetric about the interval's middle) run through simultaneous finishing with the tool axis on the normal (`max_tilt` 0, where decision 0036 item 1 makes the path the helical one) keeps the whole 72 degree move and cuts **0.1869 mm** into the ramp a quarter of the way along, by the independent checker on the exact surface between the points of every cutting move (eight poses a move): 5 of 245 moves cut more than the 0.01 mm tolerance. This is the figure decision 0115 measured for the shared fitter before its change (0.186 mm), from the same cause.

**The lobed reference job does not overcut**, before or after. Generated as `openchuck-app/tests/reference_jobs.rs` generates it (the exact lobed vase meshed at 201 rings by 720, the 3 mm ball, 2 mm stepover, 2 degree step, tilt 10) and checked between the points of every cutting move against the exact vase, the tool cuts at most **0.0039 mm**: the chord limit itself (0.4 times the tolerance, decision 0018 item 12), which says the middle test already holds the straight move everywhere on this part. At 5, 10 and 20 degree steps and with tilt 0 the figure is 0.0039 to 0.0040 mm and no move is over the tolerance. The lobed vase's radius is a sine along X and a cosine round the axis; the ball rolling over a lobe is what splits its moves, by the middle test, and no move has the ramp's shape (an inflection of the dropped height at its middle with the slope large either side), so the quarter points find nothing to split: **the move count is unchanged at every step** (1,800 moves at 2 degrees; 966, 954 and 954 at 5, 10 and 20) and the golden programs are byte-identical.

**The blisk cannot be measured under simultaneous finishing.** Every run is refused before any program exists, as OI-318 reports and OI-337 explains (the design limits at the blade roots and the shoulder): with the 3 mm ball and 0.3 mm stepover, a 5 degree step (tilt 10) refuses at X 9.821 by 0.0122 mm and a 2 degree step (tilt 10 or 0) at X 9.580 by 0.0278 mm, both on the shoulder; limited to the blades and hub by a region of the triangles at X 12 and beyond, a 5 degree step refuses at X 13.523 (the blade roots) by 0.1000 mm with tilt 10 and 0.1002 mm with tilt 0. (The refusal points have moved since OI-318's figures, which were taken before decision 0113.) So there is no simultaneous program on the blisk to check between its points; OI-337 is the item that would make one.

## Decision

1. **Simultaneous finishing's fitter accepts a move only when the straight machine move is within the chord limit at its middle and at the middles of its two halves** (the quarter points), each measured as decision 0036 item 7 measures the middle: the tool dropped along its axis at the straight move's pose there, the drop's offset taken along the surface normal (positive cutting in, negative leaving material; the test is on the magnitude, as before). Otherwise the move is halved and each half is tested the same way. The quarter points of a move are the middles of its halves, so their drops are handed down (`Fitter::refine_from`): a move that is split costs no drop it did not cost before, and an accepted move costs two more. `CHORD_FACTOR` (0.4), `MAX_DEPTH` (12) and the seeds are unchanged.
2. **A quarter with no material under it is no reason to split, unless a region constraint forbids cutting there**: then the move is halved so that the half finds the gap at its middle and cuts the pass short at the limits either side, as decision 0036 item 7 does at a middle (decision 0115 item 2, the same rule).
3. **The refusal of decision 0036 item 7 is unchanged**: a move still cutting in by more than the tolerance at its middle after `MAX_DEPTH` halvings is refused, naming its X and A. At that depth a half is 1/4096 of a seed interval and the quarter points are not tested (as in the shared fitter, which tests nothing at its depth limit), so a gouge at a quarter of such a move is kept; the message's advice is OI-337's to change.
4. **The two fitters stay two.** Sharing `wrap_fit::Fit` was considered (below) and is recorded as OI-390 for when the library API has settled; this change is the 30 lines that remove the weakness, with no change to any public item.
5. **No golden file changes**: the lobed reference job's moves are the same before and after (item 1 splits nothing there), and simultaneous finishing is in no other golden program.

## What a sampled test still cannot see

Decision 0115's bound applies here as it is: a deviation within the chord limit at a move's ends, middle and quarters but not between them is still missed, and on a surface a ball of radius `R` can follow, the dropped height along a move has no feature narrower than about `2R`, so at the default 5 degree step a feature would have to be narrower than an eighth of the seed step. The simulation (SIM-05) remains the check on the whole program (NFR-12).

The generation time roughly doubles: an accepted move now costs three drops where it cost one, and on the lobed job every seed move is accepted at its first test (1,800 seeds, 1,800 moves). Decision 0115 accepted the same cost for the shared fitter; the alternative is a cheaper test, which the alternatives below reject. The job's whole pipeline (import, generate, post, gates 1 and 2 at 1, 2 and N threads) is measured by `reference_jobs.rs` and the command-line end-to-end test; neither has a time limit (NFR-04 sets none), and the strategy's share stays well under a second.

**Test times.** The two new tests take 0.7 s (the ramp) and 3.4 s (the lobed job) uninstrumented in a debug build, so neither is long-running. Every existing test of `crates/openchuck-path/tests/simultaneous.rs` that generates a path takes about twice as long, for the reason above (alone on one test thread, uninstrumented: the independent checker on the reference parts 86 s to 203 s, the contact feed on the lobed part 53 s to 112 s, the tilt bound 27 s to 58 s, the A reversals 24 s to 46 s, every tool profile on the lobed part 15 s to 27 s, the surface of revolution 10 s to 22 s, the axial passes 7 s to 17 s, the 1, 2 and N threads test 7 s to 14 s; the whole binary 238 s to 516 s). The first four were already listed as long-running; the next four cross a minute in a full `cargo nextest run -p openchuck-path` (176, 113, 73 and 70 s on the 8-vCPU container, where the long tests share the cores) and are now listed in both profiles of `.config/nextest.toml`, with `coverage-full` priorities so that they start early in the nightly coverage run. No test takes 10 minutes alone. The full run of the path crate's 255 tests took 900 s.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Share `wrap_fit::Fit` with simultaneous finishing** (decision 0048: the best library API, with a version bump if it breaks). The two fitters bisect the same way, but their nodes differ: the shared fitter's node lives on the development of the unrolled surface with the tool axis through the rotary axis, and simultaneous finishing's node carries a track parameter, the planned tool-axis angle, the drive normal and the lead; the deviation is measured along the contact normal in one and along the drive normal in the other, and only simultaneous finishing refuses a move. One fitter needs a trait over the node, the drop and the deviation, and a module of the crate whose API lane TP is about to settle for publishing; the duplicate is 30 lines. Deferred as OI-390, to be done with the API work, not before it.
2. **Measure at more stations** or **bound the error from the drop's curvature**: decision 0115 alternatives 1 and 2; the same reasons apply.
3. **Test the quarter points at `MAX_DEPTH` as well, and refuse on them.** A half at that depth is 1/4096 of a seed interval (0.0005 degrees at a 2 degree step); the middle test there is the refusal rule as written in decision 0036 item 7, and a quarter at that depth is the middle of a half that is never made. Not done; item 3.
4. **Leave the fitter, since the reference job shows no overcut.** The ramp shows the weakness is the fitter's and not the part's, with the same figure as the shared fitter before decision 0115, and the two fitters now disagree on which moves are safe; a part with the ramp's shape under simultaneous finishing (a leaning flank an A axis can follow) would gouge. Rejected.
